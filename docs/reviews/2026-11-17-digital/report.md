# The digital technologies behind six open links

**17 November 2026.** Starting from the six open links in the [contemporary inventory](../2026-11-17-modern/report.md), this wave prioritized technologies with documented use in chip production, working image sensors and scientific computing. Six investigations followed those branches. A seventh checked the identity of one steel batch. Selection followed downstream use and the availability of original evidence, without geographic quotas.

The wave added **13 discoveries, 25 dated events and 62 sources**. It updated 10 existing discoveries and two earlier events. **Seventeen relationships became established**, including 16 additions and one promotion from candidate status. Fourteen describe a dependency or development step and three describe a shared principle or device family. Two other original questions acquired narrower, established answers alongside their remaining open claims. Three stayed open. ## The highest-impact paths with direct evidence

**Instruction sets reached working scientific applications.** Arm previewed Armv8-A in October 2011 and released its A64 instruction-set overview in November. The 2016 Scalable Vector Extension uses A64 instruction encodings. Fujitsu implemented 512-bit SVE on A64FX. Independent researchers measured the instructions on A64FX and ran a stellar-merger application across up to 1,024 Fugaku nodes. This gives a three-step path from a published architecture to an instrument scientists used. The K computer provides a separate branch from the 1994 SPARC V9 specification through SPARC64 VIIIfx. An independent solver port documents actual use of VIIIfx instructions on K. [Arm instruction-set overview](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall22/cos217/reading/ArmInstructionSetOverview.pdf) · [A64FX measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03013v2) · [Fugaku application paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11002v1) · [K solver port](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00355)

**Transistor geometry and factory process inheritance have different evidence.** Hitachi’s 1989 DELTA device wrapped the gate around a vertical silicon channel. The Berkeley collaboration’s working device paper specifically describes modifying that geometry. The established link concerns the fabricated design and accommodates differing recollections of how the fin idea first arose. Farther downstream, the N7/N7+ teardown identifies commercial chips unambiguously. A complete path from the Berkeley laboratory device into a particular TSMC manufacturing process remains open. [FinFET device paper](https://ptacts.uspto.gov/ptacts/public-informations/petitions/1558711/download-documents?artifactId=BDV2BZyLzeP3hk7cV83fQ1anWraNzk8G2sLS__GXHjbCou_o01kbc4E) · [Interview study](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8wq302v2/qt8wq302v2_noSplash_b2d02d917d85f44a83b2c653bebd30fc.pdf) · [Teardown and historical record](lanes/finfet.json)

**Working imagers can be traced beyond the original CCD concept.** A 1970 Bell Labs experiment measured the transfer of charge between adjacent MOS capacitors. The 1971 line scanner produced optical images from 96 elements. Fairchild developed a 500-element buried-channel imager and subsequently offered the CCD101. Interviews document contact and recruitment between the Bell and Fairchild programmes. The graph records transmission of the broad charge-transfer principle and a shared buried-channel architecture. Transfer of the exact Bell buried-channel design remains unresolved. These early sensor signals were analog. [Charge-transfer experiment](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1970.tb01791.x) · [Independent 1972 imaging account](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/ARCHIVE-RCA/RCA-Engineer/1972-08-09.pdf) · [Sensor evidence and product chronology](lanes/sensor.json)
