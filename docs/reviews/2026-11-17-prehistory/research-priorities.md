# Which connections to research next

Start with Eynan and Kfar Samir. Both have a named museum or excavation object that can be checked against an existing record. The metal-supply and crop-cultivation questions below would distinguish competing historical explanations but need more demanding comparisons. This ordering reflects what a researcher could resolve next.

1. **Eynan, burnt-lime plaster and Shelter 1.** Trace laboratory sample 86-1 back through the collection register to its excavation number. The original analysis identifies plaster in a bench-like feature at Eynan. An architectural study independently identifies a plastered bench in Early Natufian Shelter 1. A common inventory number or annotated excavation photograph could tie the laboratory result to the specifically dated feature. Until then they remain separate observations. [Original material analysis, table 1](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/90d7b2ae-44bf-4e69-909e-6315a31a861f/content) · [Shelter excavation and plan](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0130121)
2. **Kfar Samir, basketry and olive-oil extraction.** Match the object pictured in the original olive assemblage report with the later inventory entry KS 2. Then check for fibre-level residues or wear that could distinguish pressing from carrying or storing olives. The basket's construction is documented. Its proposed use for pressing currently rests on an interpretation shared by the reports. [Basket catalogue, KS 2 and figure 7](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/14/12/2373) · [Evidence and competing uses](lanes/olives.json)
3. **Belovode and the Pločnik copper ring.** Compare ring C_P2/13 and the Belovode Feature 6 metal with independently characterized ore sources and dated metal stock at intermediate sites. The isotope comparison gives a specific pair of objects to investigate. A common ore source could account for the match even if the ring's metal never passed through Belovode. The separate Pločnik tin-bronze foil has its own unresolved history. [Primary provenance analysis, chapter 41, group 3](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10134137/) · [Minerals review](reviews/minerals.json)
4. **Kuk, raised planting plots and bananas.** Look for banana remains on additional intact mound surfaces, with samples that test whether material washed into adjacent depressions. Independent observations support the constructed plots and local banana cultivation. Most of the relevant plant evidence comes from feature fills, which leaves open exactly where the bananas grew. [Phase 2 excavation, pp. 210–212](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n2540/pdf/ch12.pdf) · [Plant samples, table 10.T2.1](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n2540/pdf/ch10.pdf)
