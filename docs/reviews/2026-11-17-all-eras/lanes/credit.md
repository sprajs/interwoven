## Proposed

- Around 810 CE, the *Xin Tang shu*, juan 54, describes merchants depositing coin in the Tang capital with circuit liaison offices and other intermediaries, travelling elsewhere and redeeming cash by matching tallies. The Met chronology supplies the approximate year. A distinct 812 event records court approval for merchant exchange through three fiscal offices after prior restrictions. The *Tang huiyao*, juan 89, transmits the approved memorial under Yuanhe 7, month 5. The *Xin Tang shu* calls this proposed arrangement flying cash. The two texts may draw on common records, and the event is scoped to the decision because the passages do not establish completed remittances under it. [Xin Tang shu](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=新唐書/卷054&oldid=2017604) · [Tang huiyao](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=唐會要/卷089&oldid=2402026)
- The existing transition event now identifies the Yizhou Jiaozi Office, the court's approval of a ban on private issue and the officials' stated trade rationale after private payment failures. Li Dao, *Xu Zizhi tongjian changbian*, juan 101, dates the decision to Tiansheng 1, eleventh month, wuwu. Tang Shichi, *Lingyan ji*, juan 4, gives the same date in a separately composed commemorative account. The accounts' use of earlier common material remains unknown. The eleventh month of the regnal year beginning in 1023 began on 16 December (Julian), xinmao. The wuwu day was 27 days later, on 12 January 1024 (Julian). The event concerns the edict. The release date of the first government issue and the reserve backing require separate evidence. ## Deferred

- The candidate Tang-to-jiaozi link now cites the Yuan-era *Song shi*, juan 181, opening of “Huizi”. The compilers say that huizi and jiaozi probably drew on flying cash (蓋有取於唐之飛錢). This is an explicit retrospective inference. The passage names no route, institution or person transmitting the Tang practice into the first Sichuan notes. The earlier sources independently document the practices in their own contexts. The Met chronology and Von Glahn's chapter summaries do not identify this specific route. Next, check Von Glahn's full 2026 chapters 3–4 and the Song financial records they cite for an early Sichuan issuer or an intermediary operating both instruments.
