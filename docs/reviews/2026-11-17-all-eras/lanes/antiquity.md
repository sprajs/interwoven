# Antikythera’s Metonic calendar pointer

Researcher gpt-6.2-sol, high effort. Proposal from dataset commit `612d88b398f13b6a74ae3126db6801ebabce3178`. Independent admission review pending.

**Proposed.** Extend event `archaeological-evidence-100bce` on `toothed-gearing-in-the-hellenistic-greek-world`. The Hellenistic Greek instrument's upper back dial displayed a 19-year calendar in a five-turn spiral of 235 lunar months. The back-cover inscription gives the division count. Part of the dial and its pointer survive in Fragment B. Anastasiou and colleagues reconstruct the damaged mounting as a pointer that rotated with the shaft and slid through its hub, its pin following the gap between turns. The final drive gear is missing. The structured date follows Jones’s approximate range from c. 225 BCE to before the wreck. Its latest year, 50 BCE, is the later end of his probable wreck estimate of 60 ± 10 BCE. Freeth and colleagues’ construction estimate around the end of the second century BCE remains attributed in the event text. The wreck is the findspot.

**Original evidence.** Anastasiou et al. (2014), §1 and Fig. 1, §2.1 and Figs. 3–5, §2.2 and Fig. 6, documents the physical remains and checks its reconstruction against the fragmentary inscription. Jones (2020), §2, pp. 3–4 and Fig. 1, reads Back Cover Inscription II.3 against the surviving geometry and identifies the calendar; §1, pp. 1–2, distinguishes the epoch of the dial data from construction. The inscription and the physical parts furnish distinct observations of one instrument. Freeth (2006), p. 588 Fig. 3, interprets the guide pin as held in a sleeve sliding along the pointer. Anastasiou et al. (2014) use CT of the same fragment to argue for a pointer sliding through the hub. Seiradakis coauthored both studies. That refinement is attributed in the event. Bitsakis and Jones (2016), text and translation of BCI II.3, II.5–7 and commentary pp. 244–245, check the text and credit Anastasiou’s identification of the mounting. These studies share evidence and authors. The quoted passages and checksum for the 2014 CONICET PDF are retained in the source record and cache descriptor.
