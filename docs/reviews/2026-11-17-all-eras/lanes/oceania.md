## Proposed changes

- Add `marshallese-wave-model-instruction` for the community practice of teaching with physical wave models. Gulick's June 1862 account describes tied sticks representing waves or currents and island relations, used to retain and impart knowledge. It treats the Marshall Islands broadly. Winkler's 1898 account, inspected in its 1901 English translation, attributes chart explanations gathered at Jaluit in 1897 to Lojak and Laumanuan, mediated by Joachim de Brun. It distinguishes a general teaching *mattang* from more geographically specific *meddo* and *rebbelib*. Chart I illustrates the abstract form. [Gulick, p. 304](https://clockwk.com/NM/The_Nautical_Magazine%201862.pdf#page=319) · [Winkler, pp. 487–490 and 494–496](https://habeleinstitute.org/w/img_auth.php/3/3e/DYW5NCIC.pdf#page=645)
- Identify the existing `marshallese-wave-navigation-chart` as Smithsonian E432083-0. The dated event is collection in 1940–1959. Its maker, original users and particular chart class remain unresolved. The new teaching record shares the existing concept and covers the practice attested by multiple sources. [Smithsonian, Collection Date and Notes](https://www.si.edu/object/nmnhanthropology_8552873)
- Add a 2006 teaching event for Thomas Bokin's instruction to Korent Joel on Ujae, given by weaving a *meto* model through his mat. Genz also identifies earlier teaching by Isao Eknilang using the *wapepe*. Add the dated voyage to `korents-wavefinding-demonstration`, scoped to Kwajalein–Ujae and back. Genz's 2016 account describes an outbound passage using the engine, dead reckoning and a sighting of Lae, and gives fuller evidence of wave piloting on the return. The model-teaching relationship is an event-scoped candidate with a teaching carrier. The return account describes using diminished swells in the lee of atolls; the physical explanation of the *dilep* stays unresolved. [Genz 2016, pp. 12–15 and 29–34, fig. 7](https://escholarship.org/content/qt43h1d0d7/qt43h1d0d7.pdf) · [Waan Aelõñ in Majel, 2014](https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/2014/09/reviving-navigation/)

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Gulick and Winkler report separate encounters and independently attest the broad use of diagrams to convey knowledge. Only Winkler distinguishes the three historical forms here. He identifies a misunderstanding in his early account and later emphasizes instruction and personal reminders. His translated discussion of using charts to settle differences among pilots does not specify where that discussion took place. Both reports are outsider accounts, and their references to principally held knowledge describe their own encounters. The Smithsonian's specimen record is independent collection evidence. Its interpretation of instruction cites a Smithsonian magazine article, so those accounts share a lineage.
- Joel and Alson Kelen coauthored the 2009 field study, which distinguishes shore instruction from experience at sea. Kelen publicly presented Eknilang's chart as a model of swells in 2017. WAM's 2014 article records a participant interview with Genz, who also wrote the detailed 2016 report. These accounts do not independently establish Joel's exact use of both models on the 2006 return. Seek a directly attributable account by Joel or Kelen about what Joel recalled and applied on that leg. [Genz et al. 2009, pp. 235–236, fig. 2](https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/docs/22-2_genz.pdf) · [Harvard Radcliffe Institute's recording of the 2017 panel](https://www.radcliffe.harvard.edu/event/2017-wave-piloting-in-the-marshall-islands)
- The coauthored 2009 study pp. 239–240 describes feeling a cue near landfall on a stormy westbound trial. Genz 2016 p. 29 describes a lack of confidently identified swell or current patterns on the likely corresponding outward leg. The proposed navigation statement describes the 2006 return, without concluding that the outward leg used no wave cues. [Genz et al. 2009](https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/docs/22-2_genz.pdf) · [Genz 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt43h1d0d7/qt43h1d0d7.pdf)
- Genz reports that Joel learned with his grandfather at a Rongelap training reef, where a learner could feel how the canoe moved around a coral islet. Displacement associated with nuclear fallout interrupted the training tradition, while knowledge survived among elders. The 2006 teaching records activity in a documented revival project. Neither the collection interval nor the publication dates establish an archipelago-wide end of practice. Look for further named makers, objects and school-specific accounts to assess transmission between the historical and recent models. [Genz 2016, pp. 12–14 and 32–34](https://escholarship.org/content/qt43h1d0d7/qt43h1d0d7.pdf)
- The teaching practice, the specimen and the demonstration were compared against the related buoy survey, Majuro cross-swell study, Hawaiian and Satawalese navigation records, concepts and leads. Shared navigation cues alone give no specific transfer to those practices. The evidence offers no historically specific canoe design for a prerequisite link from this case.
