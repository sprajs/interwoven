## Evidence and scope

- The punched arrangement at left and the dates on Babbage’s own Sketch 13 are visible in the archive image. The central text dates the introduction of the bevel wheel to 18 March 1837 and indicates several drafting stages. The anchor uses the catalogue year for the surviving design sheet. [Science Museum Group, BAB/A/042](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/documents/aa110000176)
- Babbage’s December 1839 letter to Arago, printed as footnote 3 in the British Association committee report, explicitly credits the Jacquard system for communicating calculating instructions. The report’s section IX is a separate later assessment based on papers and machinery available to the committee. It judged that the complete design still required development and declined to recommend construction. The letter is available as a printed quotation and a modern transcription of the draft. Those witnesses trace to one piece of correspondence. The British Library manuscript was unavailable for direct inspection. [1878 committee report, printed 1879](https://www.fourmilab.ch/babbage/baas.html#note3) · [Draft transcription, Add MS 37191, ff. 287–289](https://babbagedifferenceengine.blogspot.com/2014/09/correspondence-in-british-libary_21.html)
- Menabrea’s description explicitly credits Jacquard and explains sequential mechanical selection and the Engine’s separate operation and variable cards. Menabrea heard about the design from Babbage. The English translation and the 1842 French text are one textual lineage. Lovelace’s Note E describes the proposed Engine prism and its reverse motion to reuse cards. Note C discusses backing in general and specifically explains how the reverse prism might work in a loom. The engineering sheet separately corroborates that a card mechanism entered the design. [Scientific Memoirs III, pp. 677–678, 706 and 720](https://www.hist-math.fr/textes/Lovelace1843_SketchAnalytical.pdf)
