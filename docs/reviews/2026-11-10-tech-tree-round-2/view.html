Proto-porcelain in Shang China
Yin interprets the glaze chemistry of sampled Deqing proto-porcelain, including four Shang-period Nanshan glazes, as evidence of deliberate application of lime-rich wood ash.
Scope: The analysed Deqing wares, including four Nanshan glaze samples; functional process relationship, not a claim of transmission or universal use across all proto-porcelain.
Uncertainty: The process is reconstructed from archaeological chemical analysis; how the ash was prepared remains uncertain.
Source passages
  • The earliest high-fired glazed ceramics in China: Scientific studies of the proto-porcelain from Zhejiang during the Shang and Zhou periods (c. 1700 – 221 BC) · p. 3; pp. 73–75, Table 3.1; pp. 132–135 and 138–143; Appendix 3, Table A 3.1, p. 271
    Yin reconstructs deliberate application of lime-rich wood ash and its reaction with the body from the analysed Deqing sherds. Table 3.1 lists 12 excavated Nanshan sherds, while Table A 3.1 presents glaze analyses for NS-1, NS-4, NS-8 and NS-11; ash preparation remains unresolved.
Unresolved questions
  • Whether ash was washed, obtained as potash-depleted kiln firebox ash, or otherwise prepared.
  • Distribution beyond analysed Deqing wares and origin of the glazing process.
  • Kiln design and historical lineage to later wares require independent primary evidence.
