**Independent criteria for the early design audit — 5 November 2026**

These criteria were formulated before reading the programme's design documents. They are standards for a useful comparative explanation, not demands for exhaustive coverage or experimental identification of every historical cause.

1. **Define the object and outcome.** Distinguish an artefact, a technique, an organisational system and an infrastructure bundle. Locate each case in a region, period and population. Separate invention, availability, trial, sustained use, intensity of use, diffusion, exclusion, abandonment and replacement. “Widespread” needs a denominator or an explicit qualitative proxy; “slow” needs a comparison.
2. **Explain decisions and constraints at the relevant level.** Identify inventors, funders, producers, intermediaries, purchasers, operators, workers and affected non-users where they differ. Explain their feasible alternatives, capabilities, incentives, beliefs and bargaining power. State when a decision was voluntary, constrained or imposed; benefits to a purchaser need not be benefits to a worker or a population.
3. **Trace mechanisms through time.** Connect an enabling condition to an actor's action and an observable consequence. Seek within-case evidence, reversals and rival explanations. Distinguish what actors knew at the time from retrospective engineering assessments. Allow adoption to change costs, reliability, demand, institutions and infrastructure; those variables may be consequences as well as causes.
4. **Make dependencies stronger claims than chronology.** Specify whether an edge means physical necessity, a locally contingent complement, knowledge transmission, financing, organisational change or competition. Provide evidence for the asserted relation, timing and scope. Permit alternative routes, independent reinvention, uncertain ancestry, bundles requiring several inputs, and feedback. A plausible earlier predecessor is not automatically an actual historical cause.
5. **Compare cases that could challenge the explanation.** Choose comparisons because their similarities and differences help discriminate mechanisms, not only because outcomes are famous. Include non-use, sustained niches, interrupted adoption, failed diffusion and displacement without treating those outcomes as inherently irrational. Consider changes within a region and comparable differences between regions; record selection and source-availability limits.
6. **Keep evidence inspectable and claims defeasible.** Attach precise sources to discrete factual and causal claims and distinguish contemporaneous observation, retrospective reconstruction and analyst inference. Record consequential disagreement and missing evidence. Explain what could weaken a claim; use calibrated uncertainty rather than a confidence score that merely counts citations.
7. **Examine distribution and external relationships.** Trace who gained, lost, supplied unpaid work, bore risk or was denied alternatives. Consider empire, conquest, forced labour, property regimes, standards, environmental constraints and resource transfers when relevant. Avoid treating interconnected regions as isolated observations or a European sequence as a universal path.
8. **Preserve structure without manufacturing precision.** Use stable identifiers and explicit links among cases, claims, sources and graph edges. Accommodate date ranges, changing boundaries, missing denominators and incompatible evidence. A machine-readable graph must not silently turn contested, inferred or merely correlational links into established dependencies.
9. **Bound implications for future human roles.** Distinguish tasks, jobs, livelihoods, unpaid work, power and welfare. Separate historical explanation from analogical speculation and state the conditions required for transfer. Aggregate productivity or net employment alone cannot establish which people can move into which roles, on what terms or after how long.

A convincing programme need not quantify every case. It should make its strongest conclusions traceable, its alternatives testable where the record permits, and its limitations visible in the synthesis and graph as well as in prose.
