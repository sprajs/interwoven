# MED limitations, rivals and follow-up evidence

This edition contains eight cases and sixteen inspected sources, selected for mechanism contrast rather than representativeness. It is strongest on institutions and published evaluations and weakest on recipients' own testimony, enforcement histories, prices and household opportunity costs.

## Priority disconfirmation work

1. **MED-C01:** Compare manuscript/local-language accounts with the European correspondence used by Boylston. Do not infer unique origin, continent-wide coverage, a common ancestral route, or Onesimus's precise birthplace. The rival to elite brokerage is that dissemination already operated through practitioner and household networks; epidemic timing may independently explain uptake. Search for a denominator of inoculated people and comparable nonrecipients before making risk comparisons. Early-origin claims and the identity labels imposed on enslaved informants need separate specialist scrutiny.
2. **MED-C02:** Retrieve district vaccination returns, expenditure accounts and petitions. Do observed supply or staffing gaps predict coverage within the same legal regime? Do refusals persist where provision is reliable? Separate promulgation, enforcement, certification and successful vaccination. The original Ottoman laws and 1846 *Menafiü’l-Etfal* were encountered through Evered and Evered, not inspected independently; their asserted representativeness is an important limitation. Distributional burdens of penalties and exclusion need recipient testimony.
3. **MED-C03:** Inspect hospital casebooks and contemporaneous comparisons across defined operations, operators and wards. Can selection, changing case mix, epidemic cycling or general improvements account for the apparent gains? How much did hands-on exposure change performance, independent of reputation? The Glasgow comparisons in Tröhler partly depend on Hamilton's historical reconstruction, which was not separately acquired. No claims about Semmelweis's personality, priority or institutional rejection are made: that separate archive was not examined.
4. **MED-C04:** Reproduce the 2022 water debate with harmonized death counts, population denominators and partial-year coding. This edition inspected the July 2019 Anderson–Charles–Rees manuscript and the Cutler–Miller 2022 abstract, not all final-paper appendices, response materials or data. Never cite the 2019 manuscript as an inspection of the 2022 final text. Determine household connections, operating quality and external pollution effects, not only opening dates. Test spillovers across nominal treatment/control boundaries and contemporary political determinants of investment.
5. **MED-C05:** Replace or supplement the ACS/Pfizer and USDA commemorations with procurement files, production logs and independent industrial histories. Measure yield, contamination losses, capacity opportunity costs and allocation by recipient population. Test whether prior fermentation experience actually predicts successful scale-up, and whether purchasing commitments rather than technical learning explain investment. Neither sole-inventor nor inevitable-wartime-success claims are supported here.
6. **MED-C06:** Obtain district-level blood-examination denominators, staffing, spray coverage, migration, conflict exposure and resistance surveillance. Reconcile retrospective incidence series before combining them. The accessible WHO 2016 speech verifies the certification announcement but is not an independent causal evaluation of its own success account. WHO's 2012 and 2018 Sri Lanka case-study PDFs were located but their full text could not be acquired through attempted public routes; no detailed claim depends on those inaccessible reports. Cohen et al.'s cause frequencies are reported attributions with overlapping categories, not estimates of causal prevalence.
7. **MED-C07:** Reanalyse original 1993 household microdata and use definitions; check whether the abstract's 60% figure is a reporting error or silently restricted denominator. The table gives 51.8%, 54.5%, 53.0% for all diarrhoea; the discussion excludes dysentery to obtain >60%. The broad ORT category includes other home fluids and does not establish strict formulation fidelity. Separate stock availability, demonstrated competence, reported use, treatment quality and health outcomes. Programme-affiliated investigators and retrospective recall make independent replication useful. Ruxin's interviews privilege surviving scientific participants; nurses, local staff and families require further evidence.
8. **MED-C08:** Extend standardized-patient evidence beyond one city and young adults with mild presentations. Test actual policy reform, replacement revenues, care substitution and treatment access. Randomized scripts identify encounter-level responses; they do not measure resistance, long-term welfare or every component of financial motivation. The alternative explanation that buying elsewhere signals distrust or counterfeit risk was addressed by further scripts, not eliminated under every conceivable interpretation.

## Major coverage gaps

- Sub-Saharan African health institutions and practitioners are visible only through testimony in MED-C01 and comparative leads in MED-S12. They require stand-alone cases, for example the Pare–Taveta or Garki malaria projects and their aftermath, studied in original programme records and local scholarship.
- Latin American vaccination, sanitation, primary care and pharmaceutical production are absent. Brazil's coercive vaccination campaigns and later local vaccine production would be particularly useful contrasts, provided resistance is reconstructed from recipients' sources rather than official accounts alone.
- Chinese inoculation traditions are a qualified lead rather than an independently investigated Chinese-language case. Japanese municipal water systems and Latin American sewerage could test how far the US complementarity findings travel.
- Ancient and medieval sanitation, midwifery, indigenous medicine, diagnostic laboratories, blood systems, disability access and rural transport are not covered by this contribution. Their omission must not imply technological inactivity or lack of expertise.
- This edition cannot rank cost, trust, state capacity and knowledge by universal causal importance. Nor does it estimate a historical success rate from its selected cases.

## Important source-access distinctions

The sixteen ledger records describe the actual works inspected. Fifteen offered complete texts; the exact sections read are recorded. MED-S08 was inspected only at abstract level. The Ottoman article's full text was obtained through Europe PMC XML after the ordinary PMC route presented a browser challenge. Lister's 2010 reprint was read as full HTML through a public retrieval route; the original BMJ scan was located but not used as though independently inspected. Ruxin was read through the Cambridge PDF's parsed text, with focused inspection of methodological and implementation sections. Chowdhury et al. was checked in both the public full-text rendering and the publisher PDF's parsed tables; a stable DOI is recorded, without the temporary access-bearing PDF URL. This is text verification, not a page-by-page visual audit.

## Conditional synthesis questions

When is the scarce component a substance, a skill, a trustworthy relationship, a finance arrangement, or the capacity to notice failure? Can a service preserve appropriate use after disease becomes less visible? Which apparent resistance is refusal, and which is inability to comply? When does easier purchasing enlarge benefit, and when does it enlarge inappropriate use? These are historical research questions. No future adoption or occupational forecast is made.
