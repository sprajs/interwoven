# NET uncertainty and next investigations

## Questions that could change the explanation

1. **Paper, NET-C01:** Obtain regional mill accounts and matched paper prices, including costs of rags, water access, fuel, transport and finishing. Bloom's explanation of European paper's market position is not a measured decomposition. In particular, his medieval hydraulic-mill transmission claims rely partly on inference from location and analogous machinery; no necessity edge for a specific mill lineage is included.
2. **China, NET-C02:** Compare accounts for the same kinds of text printed from woodblocks and movable type. Measure setup labour, composition/sorting, paper requirements, block storage, damage, capital recovery, reprinting intervals and unsold stock. The current evidence supports conditional workflow differences, not a universal price ranking.
3. **Korea, NET-C03:** Use individual rental manuscripts and printed variants to connect material choices with reader experience and business accounts. How often was copying cheaper, more available, more adaptable or more prestigious? These motives must not be inferred solely from the survival of manuscripts.
4. **Europe, NET-C04:** Re-audit the exclusion restrictions in the Mainz-distance and printer-death designs. Could printer deaths directly alter local expertise, finance or network contacts? Could migration generate city-growth effects without equal gains in national productivity? The 2019 working paper's introduction gives over 295,000 publications, while §3.1 gives over 299,000 editions; do not silently treat these as an identical denominator.
5. **Ottoman printing, NET-C05:** Inspect the original probate document and its title groupings, compare actual sales ledgers if available, and audit later work revising Müteferrika's estate/output numbers. Sabev's 2007 reconstruction contains estimated runs for three editions, stock rather than sales records, gifts, and unequal exposure times. The often repeated 69.3% is not a directly observed sell-through rate.
6. **Navigation, NET-C06:** Establish the actual prevalence of magnetic compasses and wooden altitude instruments by task, fleet, region and decade. Staples describes integration into stellar nomenclature; Tolmacheva says compasses were rarely used. Neither provides a carriage denominator. Do not translate either into a universal dependency.

## Rival mechanisms and discriminating evidence

| Proposed mechanism | Rival | Evidence that would discriminate |
|---|---|---|
| Papermaking costs encourage local production | Cheap imports satisfy use without domestic skill acquisition | Paper provenance, trade prices and mill entry/closure in the same place and interval |
| Script inventory explains print technique | Expected run, reprinting frequency, labour and storage explain technique | Same-script, comparable-content workshops with differing cost structures |
| State control suppresses publishing | State exemplars, equipment and training also support nonstate production | Licences, denied petitions, type loans, workers' careers and private editions |
| Printing automatically reduces end-user prices | Working capital, distribution and market power control the delivered price | Matched editions and transport routes, transaction records and competition shocks |
| Religious opposition explains Ottoman limits | Particular grants, textual accuracy and chosen subject markets explain them | Original permissions, prosecutions, endorsements and buyers' records |
| Instrument availability creates ocean capability | Pilot expertise and route-specific environmental knowledge remain binding | Logs comparing crews and routes, actual equipment, failures and recruitment |

## Important uninspected or incomplete leads

- Jonathan M. Bloom, *Paper Before Print* (2001): publisher description located, **book not inspected**. It is not counted as an acquired source; the inspected 2017 chapter is NET-S01. Follow original document citations through to their editions before using the survey's finer chronology.
- Lucille Chia, *Printing for Profit*; Cynthia Brokaw, *Commerce in Culture*; Kai-wing Chow, *Publishing, Culture, and Power*: **not directly inspected**. References in McDermott/Burke are leads, not extra sources or independent corroboration.
- Ya Zuo, *Shen Gua's Empiricism* (2018): the online translated appendix is inspected as NET-S04; **the rest of the book is not claimed as read**. Clay composition and modern reconstruction debates require the cited experimental reports.
- The Academy of Korean Studies *Early Printings in Korea* PDF timed out; it contributes **no claim**. A direct Korean-language archival study of court foundries and early metal-type output would strengthen the gap between Jikji and later circulation evidence.
- Hangeul metal-type scholarship and Young Kyun Oh's *Engraving Virtue* are promising for language, illustration and readers outside elite literary Sinitic circles; **not inspected** here.
- Gerald R. Tibbetts's translation of Ibn Majid (1971/1981), Ibrahim Khoury's Arabic editions (1970–1972), and later Omani manuscript editions: **not directly inspected**. Staples notes translation/distribution problems and overreliance on Tibbetts in English-language scholarship. Detailed nautical measurements must be checked against these texts before calibration or accuracy claims.
- Precision clocks, Islamic astronomical instruments, Ottoman timekeeping, Japanese publishing and Indian lithography are **outside this six-case contribution**, not negative adoption cases.

## Access and representativeness audit

Fifteen sources are in sources.json: 13 have an accessible full article/chapter/web text or a complete public-domain translation; two are publisher previews containing the relevant complete chapter but only selected passages were inspected, conservatively labelled partial-text. A full-text label describes available text, not a claim to have read every page. The locator and scope_limit fields identify actual inspected parts. No abstract-only or search-excerpt-only item supports a claim.

Primary-record access is mediated: Shen Gua and the voyage journal were read in modern English translations; the Diamond Sutra colophon was read in the holding institution's translation. Original Ottoman archival documents were not directly inspected. Jikji's date and technique rely on its custodian's description, not independent material examination.

Ordinary readers, illiterate listeners, women outside elite/rental reading settings, nonelite artisans and failures are unevenly represented. Surviving editions and catalogues are not censuses; neither selected probate inventories nor publication counts measure literacy. No regional ranking of intelligence, religiosity, welfare or technological aptitude follows from this sample.

The most significant internal source conflicts are retained: broad Islamic-print resistance in NET-S14 is excluded; narrow Ottoman documentary evidence in NET-S11 is preferred for that issue. NET-S12's optimistic commercial framing is separated from its raw quantities. NET-S01's universalising explanations for India, West Africa and hydraulic diffusion are not used as established causal claims.
