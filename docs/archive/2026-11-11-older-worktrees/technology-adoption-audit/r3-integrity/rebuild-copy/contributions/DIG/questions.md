# Questions, limits and next discriminating investigations

This is a seven-case purposive comparison, with 25 bounded claims and 16 inspected source documents. Document count is not independent corroboration: the Ofcom report, underlying tables and technical report repeat/document one survey; the Kenyan papers extend one research programme; DoT compiles TRAI returns. The evidence lineage is recorded in every source.

## What was not resolved

- **DIG-C01:** Did permissive licensing materially accelerate deployment once network access and usable browsers are accounted for? The original proposal and institutional release history establish choices and permissions, not a controlled adoption effect. Acquire implementation correspondence, contemporary competing-system costs and deployment records. A comparable proprietary system spreading just as quickly would challenge a licence-centred account.
- **DIG-C02 / DIG-K25:** Are co-owned computers and handhelds actively complementary? The 2015 ACS lacks task logs and disposal histories. Measure active use by the same person, including idle legacy devices, before treating household co-access as a mechanism.
- **DIG-C03 / DIG-K24:** Pocket access, apps, social coordination, constrained alternatives and habit all fit the observed preference pattern. No independent measure ranks their causal importance. A task-specific experiment or credible change in one component, with service and price held comparable, is needed. Perceived document difficulty cannot settle overall utility.
- **Device prices:** “Most laptops are cheaper than most smartphones” remains unestablished. Define country, date, new/used/refurbished condition, product universe, transaction versus list prices, unit-sales weights, subsidy/financing treatment and recurring data/service expenditure. Neither selected price anecdotes nor average revenue per GB answers this distributional question.
- **DIG-C04:** Is lack of study access caused by device sharing, adult control, connectivity, content, language, electricity, instructional design or income? ASER establishes an availability/access gap but not its unique cause. An intervention assigning personal access and suitable content separately could discriminate. Learning outcomes and unpaid support time require additional measurements.
- **DIG-C05:** The strongest welfare rival is selective economic development around early agent expansion. The main Science paper describes pretests and attrition sensitivity, but the supplementary tables and raw replication were not independently acquired or reproduced here. Reanalyse those tests, occupation outcomes, attrition assumptions and national extrapolation. The 2024 FinAccess cross-section does not validate a 2008–2014 causal estimate.
- **DIG-C06:** Obtain a genuine retained-use panel: purchase or first trial, returns, active days/hours with an explicit window, task mix, abandonment and follow-up by age/accessibility group. The UK G1 measure has no weekly/monthly threshold and cannot supply retention. Repeat the work experiment across improved/optimised configurations and tasks that exploit spatial displays. Neither one office setup nor one gaming cross-section represents all VR.
- **DIG-C07:** Independent carrier/distributor incentives, developer economics, app availability and buyer switching evidence are missing. These are needed to distinguish network coordination from timing, execution and acquisition valuation. The company’s unified-app strategy is an interested statement, not causal identification.

## Geographic and countercase gaps

China, Latin America, the Middle East, South-East Asia and most of Africa are absent as directly investigated populations. Kenya is not representative of mobile-money adoption elsewhere. Priority negative comparisons include mobile-money launches with strong coverage but weak sustained use, successful feature-phone services, and capable smartphone platforms that succeeded in particular local markets despite weak global shares. They must be independently sourced before insertion.

Stable niches also matter: specialist VR training/design, shared headsets or location-based entertainment, physical computers retained for accessibility, and offline/assisted services. These were not assumed absent merely because the inspected household surveys omit them. Later headset generations and the 2026 market are outside this report's empirical scope.

## Source access and integrity notes

- Every source ledger entry distinguishes what was available from what was actually inspected. “Full-text” means the full source was accessible/acquired, not that every page of a long report was read. Claims use the listed sections or tables.
- Direct downloads succeeded for Census, Pew, DoT, ASER, NBER, the Science article hosted by FinDev Gateway, FinAccess and the VR author paper. Local acquisition hashes and failures are in `cache/download_manifest.json`.
- The web tool returned an error for the FinDev article and timed out on FinAccess; direct requests successfully acquired their PDFs. Thus those sources were inspected in full relevant text rather than inferred from abstracts. The NBER alternate `system/files/working_papers` path was used successfully.
- Direct downloads of the Ofcom chapter and Online Nation report returned 403, but full searchable PDF extraction was available through the web tool. The original Core Survey tables and technical report were also read through that tool. No paywall or login was bypassed.
- The original signed CERN release was **not acquired**; the licence claim uses the named CERN participant retrospective. This is not a claim that the original is inaccessible. The Science supplemental materials were **not acquired**, rather than claimed unavailable.
- Ofcom Online Nation 2024 p. 68 has an internally inconsistent comparison-year parenthesis. Its 4% VR level is verified against 2023 Core Survey Table 74; no trend is derived from the parenthesis. The 2015 Ofcom materials also vary in the quoted 2014 smartphone-importance percentage; this report uses only the 2015 level.
- Company filings are primary financial records with commercial framing. Audited accounting totals do not validate promotional explanations of adoption or establish user counts.
- No inaccessible or search-snippet-only item is used to support a substantive claim. No current global device-price ranking or future market forecast is supplied.

## Labour and actor follow-up

The case records separate chooser, payer, operator and affected nonchooser. Evidence is especially weak on repair/support workers, household technical help, care burdens, disability, device resale, bargaining power and workers' destinations after restructuring. The Kenyan panel offers occupational outcomes but not a complete account of job quality, earnings distribution or transition duration. These gaps prevent converting adoption or investment figures into forecasts about occupations.

Follow individuals and cohorts through changed tasks, training, debt, entry barriers, ownership and bargaining arrangements. Compare new paid roles with displaced work and unpaid maintenance. Aggregate company losses, household access and perceived productivity are different outcomes and cannot substitute for this evidence.
