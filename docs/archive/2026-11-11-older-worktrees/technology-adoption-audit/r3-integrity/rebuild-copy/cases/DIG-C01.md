# DIG-C01 — CERN distributed information and Web software reuse

**Period:** 1989–1994. **Region:** Switzerland; CERN international research community. **Domain:** networked computing; information institutions.

A distributed information-management proposal and later reuse permissions reveal technical and institutional complements; they do not independently establish global diffusion causes.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/DIG/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** 1989 proposal builds on existing hypertext ideas and author’s Enquire experience; no exclusive first-invention claim.

**feasibility:** 1993 release names operational client/server/library components; detailed reliability benchmarks not inspected.

**deployment:** CERN early implementation and software release, bounded by documentary evidence.

**diffusion:** Permission for external reuse documented; no population diffusion curve acquired.

**scaling:** Not quantified by these sources.

**non-adoption/niche:** Proposal describes existing hierarchical and keyword-based systems as practical alternatives with limitations.

**displacement/reversal:** No measured disappearance of prior systems.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** 1989 distributed hypertext design at CERN; separate 1993/1994 client/server/library licensing subepisode.

**task:** Find and maintain information across changing research teams and heterogeneous computers; permit others to reuse implementation.

**population:** CERN collaborators and prospective code implementers; worldwide adopter denominator unavailable.

**interval:** 1989 design; 1993 public-domain and 1994 licence changes.

**contemporary alternatives:** CERNDOC/hierarchical documentation; VMS HELP and keyword systems; Personal linked notes/Enquire; Commercial or academic hypertext systems mentioned by proposer

**adoption measure:** Qualitative design and release documents; no comparable user counts or retention window.

**observation limits:** Institutional participant sources; proposed benefits are not measured realised adoption.

## Actors

**chooser:** CERN management could authorise work; implementers could choose software reuse. Individual adoption decisions not observed.

**payer:** CERN and participating organisations supplied staff/computing resources; spending and payer incidence not quantified.

**operator maintainer:** Software developers and information contributors; distributed maintenance proposed, actual time burden unmeasured.

**affected nonchooser:** New researchers and colleagues whose information became findable; affected nonusers not surveyed.

**ability to refuse:** A proposal and permissive reuse do not show mandatory adoption; ability of individual staff to avoid the system is unknown.

## Labour

**tasks:** Documentation retrieval, linking and updating; predecessor information transfer through people and newsletters. No net employment estimate.

**entry barriers:** Computing access and implementation knowledge; credential/occupation barriers not measured.

**bargaining ownership:** CERN controlled initial code-release terms; subsequent ownership and bargaining distribution outside scope.

**transition:** 1989 proposal to early-1990s release chronology; no individual occupational transition evidence.

**distribution:** The proposer expected reduced information loss, but distribution of realised time savings not measured.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer displacement of librarians, administrators or developers, or net jobs from a design proposal.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Linking existing information lowers migration demands: design rationale, uptake effect unmeasured.; Cross-system compatibility and remote access support the specified organisational task.; Reuse permissions can lower legal entry costs; comparative importance underdetermined.

Existing systems were useful; later graphical interfaces or network expansion could explain wider uptake. Original proposal is not a success evaluation.

## Evidence links

- [DIG-K01](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k01): The 1989 CERN proposal specified remote access, heterogeneous computers, decentralised linking and access to existing information; text on 24-by-80 screens was initially sufficient and elaborate security/accounting was not a primary local requirement.
- [DIG-K02](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k02): CERN released specified Web components to the public domain in April 1993 and later used permissive royalty-free licensing; this removed a reuse-permission barrier, but its contribution to later diffusion is not identified here.
