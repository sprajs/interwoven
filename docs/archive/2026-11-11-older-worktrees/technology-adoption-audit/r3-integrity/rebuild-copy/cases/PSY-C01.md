# PSY-C01 — Harvest-timed fertilizer offers in Busia: use without persistent adoption

**Period:** 2000–2005; primary offer experiment 2003–2005. **Region:** Western Kenya; Busia district. **Domain:** agriculture; behaviour; distribution.

Research-assisted dose trials and randomised offers show that the configuration and timing of access matter; neither every recommended input package nor a universal present-bias account follows.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/PSY/report.md).

## Stages

**feasibility:** On-farm trials establish output and calculated returns for specific CAN doses, not fertilizer generically.

**deployment:** SAFI sold vouchers at market fertilizer prices with free delivery at specified times.

**diffusion:** Household any-plot use rose during treatment seasons.

**non-adoption/niche:** Many eligible farmers did not buy; treatment effects did not persist into later seasons absent renewed offers.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Maize top-dressing CAN, with dose experiments distinguished from market-price fertilizer vouchers and delivery offers.

**task:** Raise maize returns and arrange purchase before top-dressing time.

**population:** Dose trials: farmers sampled via school-parent lists. SAFI: 1,230 farmers sampled from parents of fifth/sixth graders in 16 schools; outcome covers any household plot, not just respondent-controlled plots.

**interval:** Six agronomic seasons starting July 2000; primary SAFI offers for 2004 long/short rains and follow-up in 2005.

**contemporary alternatives:** No fertilizer or customary low input; Purchase fertilizer privately near application; Late free delivery; Late 50% subsidy; Early offer with chosen timing

**adoption measure:** Self-reported fertilizer use on any household plot by season. S01 Table 4 adjusted first-season effect +14.3pp against 24.0% comparison; adjusted second-season +18.1pp against about 26%. Denominators differ by season/controls (first-season full-controls N=716).

**observation limits:** Not a population diffusion curve; households without school-age pupils underrepresented; adoption data obtained for 924/1,230 sampled farmers (75.1%, S01 footnote10), with varying regression denominators and reporting limits; agronomic plots research-assisted.

## Actors

**chooser:** Sampled farmer accepts or refuses offer; household plot decisions may involve spouses.

**payer:** Farmer pays fertilizer price; NGO funds delivery/experiment and the subsidy arm.

**operator maintainer:** Farmer/household applies inputs; field staff assist agronomic trials and deliver vouchers/fertilizer.

**affected nonchooser:** Other household cultivators and members bear resource allocation and farm-work consequences.

**ability to refuse:** Explicit voluntary purchase; nonpurchase observed; fertilizer ownership allowed resale, although few reported it. Household members’ bargaining and independent refusal not measured.

## Labour

**tasks:** Input acquisition, transport, dose application, cultivation and harvest; delivery shifts a market trip to programme staff.

**entry barriers:** Cash/harvest resources, access to a market and plot, knowledge of dose; individual skill or land-access barriers not separately identified.

**bargaining ownership:** Private purchases and household plots; within-household control of gains not established.

**transition:** Seasonal use observed; no permanent occupational transition followed.

**distribution:** Dose-specific average returns and treatment averages; not effects on hired labour, landless people, wages or gendered task burdens.

**evidence limit:** Research-assisted plot profits and uptake cannot establish whole-farm livelihood gains or durable labour demand.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: timing of acquisition cost; seasonal resources; possible commitment/procrastination; agronomic dose fit

Late-delivery estimate +9–10pp is imprecise, not zero; early–late contrast p=.08. More intensive official package had negative mean calculated return. Timing-choice result does not fully fit authors’ simple model.

## Evidence links

- [PSY-K01](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k01): In Busia’s research-assisted on-farm trials, half-teaspoon CAN top-dressing produced a mean calculated seasonal return of 36%, while the full Ministry-recommended fertilizer/hybrid-seed package had a negative mean return; higher physical yield did not make every input configuration profitable.
- [PSY-K02](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k02): Randomised harvest-time SAFI offers increased household fertilizer use in treated seasons (adjusted +14.3pp and +18.1pp in the two main seasons), but did not produce a detectable persistent increase after the offer ceased.
- [PSY-K03](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k03): SAFI’s pattern is consistent with procrastination/commitment mechanisms, but does not uniquely identify present bias: late free delivery estimated +9–10pp without significance, the early–late difference had p=.08, and timing-choice take-up did not fully match the simple model.
