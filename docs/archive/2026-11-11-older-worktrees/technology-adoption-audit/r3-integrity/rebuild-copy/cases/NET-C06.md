# NET-C06 — Indian Ocean navigation as instruments, route knowledge and pilot access

**Period:** ca. 1400–1560, with a separately bounded Malindi departure in April 1498. **Region:** Indian Ocean; East Africa; Arabia; South Asia. **Domain:** navigation; maritime knowledge; instruments; trade.

Arabic/Turkish textual navigation combines route-specific seasonal knowledge, stellar methods and instruments, with uncertain carriage prevalence. A 1498 voyage narrative shows the use of a local pilot alongside coercion in obtaining assistance. Operating knowledge and access to people cannot be reduced to possession of one device.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/NET/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Surviving manuals and later descriptions are not invention dates for compass, altitude instruments, routes or oral knowledge.

**feasibility:** Prescribed methods and the narrated crossing demonstrate particular operational practices, not a measured instrument accuracy or safety rate.

**deployment:** Texts discuss stellar directions and measurement aids; the 1498 account records departure from Malindi with a supplied pilot.

**diffusion:** No fleet-level instrument-carriage denominator. Sources differ on magnetic compass prevalence and share much of their underlying corpus.

**scaling:** Knowledge transmission through texts, verse and pilots is examined qualitatively; no training-capacity or trade-volume attribution.

**non-adoption/niche:** Absence of maps attached to one corpus does not prove universal chartlessness. A compass is not made a universal ocean-voyage prerequisite.

**displacement/reversal:** Imported or standardising instruments coexist with stellar conventions and regional expertise; replacement of pilot knowledge is not established.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Late medieval/early modern northern Indian Ocean routes using seasonal/stellar knowledge with optional measurement aids; separate 1498 Portuguese fleet episode acquires a Malindi-provided pilot.

**task:** Choose departure windows, bearings and routes and manage local hazards; the fleet episode concerns one departure/crossing rather than all ocean exploration.

**population:** Pilots and practices represented in Ibn Majid/al-Mahri/Sidi Ali Reis textual traditions and one anonymous voyage narrative. Many East African and Asian crews are underrepresented.

**interval:** Main textual comparison ca. 1400–1560; Malindi events 22–24 April 1498 are separately scoped.

**contemporary alternatives:** Stellar directions and observations without a magnetic instrument; Fingers or wooden altitude aids; Text, verse, oral instruction or charts where available; Recruiting local pilots rather than treating all expertise as portable equipment

**adoption measure:** Documented prescriptions and one narrated use of a pilot. No compass-carriage, chart-use, crew-training, voyage-failure or all-ship denominator.

**observation limits:** Unequal textual survival, shared source traditions, translation choices and normative instructions; the chronicler's religious identifications are unreliable.

## Actors

**chooser:** Captains, ship patrons and pilots choose routes and assistance within weather, political and skill constraints; the Malindi ruler supplies a pilot in the narrated episode.

**payer:** Voyage patrons and trading interests provide resources, but the pilot's remuneration and general training/maintenance costs are not established in the inspected passages.

**operator maintainer:** Pilots, navigators and crews interpret observations and maintain vessel/instruments; knowledge cannot be assigned solely to the captain.

**affected nonchooser:** Crews, passengers and coastal actors bear risks; the seized Malindi court servant is directly subjected to coercion in the reported episode.

**ability to refuse:** The seizure constrains the servant and applies pressure to the court. The pilot's own consent/remuneration is unknown, and no universal account of coercive recruitment follows.

## Labour

**tasks:** Observe stars/winds, measure, recall or consult route instructions, recruit pilots and apply local knowledge; instrument possession does not remove interpretive work.

**entry barriers:** Experience, route knowledge, learning networks and access to ships/instruments; formal credential, training duration and class/gender exclusions are not systematically measured.

**bargaining ownership:** Local knowledge can make pilots valuable, but coercion and political mediation complicate bargaining. One episode does not quantify their power or earnings.

**transition:** No longitudinal evidence follows crews adapting to new instruments or old pilots losing work; coexistence cannot establish transition speed.

**distribution:** Commercial interests, crews and coerced coastal actors face different returns and risks; voyage completion alone cannot aggregate these into welfare.

**evidence limit:** No comparative safety/accuracy effect, employment forecast, standard wage or general claim that pilot recruitment required violence.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: route-specific environmental knowledge; integration with existing conventions; embodied expertise; multiple knowledge representations; diplomacy and coercion in skill access

Prescription does not prove practice on every vessel. Instruments' relative accuracy and actual carriage remain unmeasured; charts may not survive. The single pilot episode cannot isolate the causal contribution of coercion or a pilot to success.

## Evidence links

- [NET-K21](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#net-k21): In the northern Indian Ocean routes described by fifteenth-/sixteenth-century navigation texts, knowledge of regional and transitional winds mattered within the broad monsoon pattern; season was part of route selection.
- [NET-K22](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#net-k22): Staples describes wooden star-altitude aids and magnetic compasses using stellar direction names in late medieval Indian Ocean navigation; Tolmacheva calls magnetic use rare, leaving carriage prevalence unmeasured.
- [NET-K23](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#net-k23): Ibn Majid's surviving instructions include verse suitable for memorisation and lack associated maps, while Portuguese reports describe some Indian Ocean chart use; absence in one corpus does not establish universal chartlessness.
- [NET-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#net-k24): The anonymous 1498 voyage narrative reports da Gama seizing a Malindi court servant while demanding pilots, then receiving a pilot and departing with him on 24 April; this documents local expertise and coercion in one crossing episode.
