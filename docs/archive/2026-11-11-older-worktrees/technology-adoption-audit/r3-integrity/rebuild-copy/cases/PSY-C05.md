# PSY-C05 — Bicycles and girls’ schooling: distinct Bihar and Zambia institutional packages

**Period:** Bihar 2006–2008 with later exam checks; Zambia 2017–2021. **Region:** India; Bihar with Jharkhand comparator; Zambia; Monze, Mazabuka, Kalomo districts. **Domain:** transport; education; gender; accessibility.

Two explicitly separate school-access episodes show that finance, infrastructure, maintenance and gendered circumstances jointly shape mobility. Neither identifies a universal psychological effect of a bicycle or of paying for it.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/PSY/report.md).

## Stages

**deployment:** Bihar cash earmarked for bicycle purchase; Zambia distributed bikes with mechanics, monitoring and school-use custody conditions.

**diffusion:** Bihar programme exposure affects grade-age enrolment; Zambia randomised school offer affects access, travel and attendance. These are not comparable bike-ownership diffusion curves.

**non-adoption/niche:** Those outside school/distance eligibility are largely unobserved; distant girls retain constraints; several agency and educational outcomes have null/imprecise effects.

**scaling:** Bihar is statewide programme evaluation; Zambia is a 100-school trial. No extrapolated national saturation claim.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Bihar: Rs2,000 earmarked grant after grade-9 enrolment, school receipt certification. Zambia: durable bicycle plus trained mechanic, supervisory committee and four-year resale prohibition, with randomised $5 maintenance-fee versus free variants.

**task:** Access school and reduce commuting burdens for adolescent girls; ownership and broader mobility are separate outcomes.

**population:** Bihar: DLHS-3 household members age14–17, treated age14–15 versus16–17, girls versus boys, Bihar versus Jharkhand; full-control main Table2 N=30,112. Zambia: 100 public basic schools, initially enrolled grade5–7 girls walking >3km away; urban and some very remote schools excluded. 25 payment, 20 free, 55 control schools. Survey frame2,471; Table1 commute N=1,879 and Table4 attendance N=1,952.

**interval:** Bihar grant launched June2006; DLHS late2007/early2008. Zambia baseline July–August2017, distribution September–November2017, follow-up October–December2018, administrative follow-up2019–21.

**contemporary alternatives:** Walking to existing school; Do not enrol or discontinue schooling; Other transport where available, not a uniform available substitute; School construction/relocation not randomised in these studies; Zambia otherwise identical bike package with/without nominal fee

**adoption measure:** Bihar age-appropriate enrolment/completion proxy from policy exposure (+5.2pp in main adjusted estimate), not measured riding. Zambia self-reported access, minutes one-way and days absent/late last week, plus agency indices; later administrative retention/attendance conditional samples separate.

**observation limits:** No pooled effect/denominator. Bihar triple-difference assumptions; Zambia differential attrition and self-report, later administrative data not preregistered. Final Zambia full text not acquired: detailed evidence from May2025 manuscript; its imprecise pooled aspirations differ from the final abstract’s positive assertion, unresolved until final tables/definitions are inspected. Disability/cycling-ability representation and route accessibility not established; no such exclusion rule identified in inspected sampling passages.

## Actors

**chooser:** Governments/NGO define eligibility and conditions; households choose Bihar purchase/type; Zambia girls/guardians accept allocated bikes and girls operate them subject to family and school rules.

**payer:** Bihar state funds grant; households may cover extras/maintenance. Zambia programme funds bicycles/support; treatment families pay nominal maintenance fee only in payment arm.

**operator maintainer:** Girls ride; families share some use; Bihar local repair arrangements unmeasured; Zambia school-linked trained mechanics support repair.

**affected nonchooser:** Household members, excluded/out-of-school girls, siblings and school staff; sharing and monitoring can redistribute resources/control.

**ability to refuse:** Zambia refusal reported but almost universal take-up among eligible recipients; primary-school use and resale restrictions limit subsequent choice. Bihar household discretion/receipt conditions documented; adolescent bargaining or independent refusal not fully measured.

## Labour

**tasks:** Travel time shifts; bicycle repair/supervision tasks; household asset-sharing. Zambia Table2 lower odds of girls’ income-generating time but no clear changes in most other reported time categories.

**entry barriers:** Documented Zambia eligibility is school enrolment in grades5–7 and walking distance; school sampling excludes urban and some very remote schools. Disability, cycling capacity and route accessibility are unmeasured applicability limits here, not established eligibility rules; household permission may constrain use.

**bargaining ownership:** Bihar grant earmarked to eligible girls; Zambia conditional custodianship and household sharing. Selected self-reported bargaining improves, not unrestricted asset control.

**transition:** School outcomes followed up to four years; adult employment, wages and occupation transitions not established.

**distribution:** Gender targeted; distance heterogeneity and imperfect road-connected comparisons. Out-of-school/noneligible girls outside Zambia target; disability/cycling-ability representation and corresponding outcomes not established.

**evidence limit:** School participation or agency scores cannot be converted into future earnings, occupational access or universal emancipation.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: reduced travel burden; conditional asset access; physical infrastructure; household allocation; possible safety/agency feedback

Bihar schooling/safety reforms and roads are rival/complementary processes; norm change not separately measured. Road proxy is imperfect/nonrandom. In the May2025 Zambia manuscript, fee/free-arm differences and pooled general mobility/aspirations are imprecise while selected reported agency outcomes improve. The final abstract says educational aspirations improved; final tables/definitions and pooled-versus-arm-specific interpretation were not inspected, so this difference remains unresolved.

## Evidence links

- [PSY-K15](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k15): For Bihar’s bicycle-grant exposure, the adjusted triple-difference estimate was +5.2pp in age-appropriate grade-9 enrolment/completion for girls, relative to older cohorts, boys and Jharkhand; it is not an estimate of actual cycling or total lifetime secondary enrolment.
- [PSY-K16](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k16): Bihar’s distance heterogeneity and ineligible-grade placebo favour a school-access channel, but do not separate saved travel time, safety, peer travel and changed social norms.
- [PSY-K17](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k17): A later Bihar reanalysis finds stronger bicycle-programme estimates in villages with all-weather road connectivity, but its health-facility road proxy and nonrandom infrastructure do not establish that such roads are strictly necessary.
- [PSY-K18](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k18): In Zambia’s 100-school experiment, the bicycle-plus-support package reduced self-reported one-way commute by 35.08 minutes (control 103.77) and last-week absence by 0.29 days (control 1.01) at one year; the final article abstract rounds these to 34% and 29%.
- [PSY-K19](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k19): Zambia’s nominal-fee versus free bicycle arms did not yield precise differences in first-stage transport or reported empowerment outcomes; a parental-payment/psychological-ownership explanation is therefore not identified by one arm being significant and the other not.
- [PSY-K20](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#psy-k20): In Zambia’s May2025 manuscript, the package improved selected self-reported agency measures, notably locus of control and bargaining, but general mobility and pooled aspirations were imprecise; aggregate empowerment varies with specification/correction. The final abstract says educational aspirations improved, an unresolved difference from the inspected pooled tables. The inspected evidence does not identify a distinct norm-change mechanism.
