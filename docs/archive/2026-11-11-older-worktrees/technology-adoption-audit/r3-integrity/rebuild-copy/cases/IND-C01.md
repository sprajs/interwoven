# IND-C01 — Central European mine pumping and remote mechanical power

**Period:** c. 1550–1700. **Region:** Erzgebirge; Harz; Central Europe. **Domain:** mining; hydraulic power; mechanical transmission.

Mines used a portfolio of human, animal and water power; remote rod transmission expanded the usable hydraulic choice set without requiring steam.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/IND/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Multiple devices predate the interval; exact priority for crank/rod combinations is disputed, not fixed here.

**feasibility:** Agricola gives operating descriptions; later scholarship compares feasible direct and remote-drive arrangements.

**deployment:** Named mine accounts and later depictions support installations; drawings alone are not a census.

**diffusion:** Continued descriptions and installations provide qualitative evidence; no denominator established.

**scaling:** Remote transmission allowed a stream to serve distant pumps; scale in total regional output unresolved.

**non-adoption:** Site water, transmission expense and maintenance constrained individual choices; no general hydraulic absence inferred.

**displacement:** Human, horse and hydraulic devices coexisted; no complete replacement date established.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Direct and remote-drive hydraulic mine pumps, alongside human and animal devices; direct pumps and later remote rod work are explicit subepisodes.

**task:** Remove water and raise mine loads sufficiently to continue extraction.

**population:** Selected central European mines described by Agricola and later technical authors and represented in mining imagery; not all German mines.

**interval:** c. 1550–1700

**contemporary alternatives:** Human windlass and pumping; Animal whims and hauling; Direct hydraulic pumps where streams could be diverted; Remote rod transmission; Drainage tunnels or foregoing deeper extraction

**adoption measure:** Qualitative descriptions, named installations and later depictions; no verified total installation denominator.

**observation limits:** Treatises select remarkable machinery; artistic depictions may conflate arrangements. Availability of a description is not evidence of a region-wide installed stock.

## Actors

**chooser:** Mine owners, investors and technical managers, as described by treatises; precise governance differs by mine.

**payer:** Owners and shareholders supply resources; Agricola describes contributions but no comparative capital accounts audited here.

**operator maintainer:** Mine workers, pump attendants, smiths and carpenters; task descriptions exist but occupation-specific earnings not established.

**affected nonchooser:** Miners whose work depends on drainage, hauling labourers, and downstream water users; effects on the latter unmeasured.

**ability to refuse:** Agricola says workers may leave when provisioning raises required wages, but this prescription is not a labour-mobility census. Authority and miners’ actual refusal rights remain uncertain.

## Labour

**tasks:** Hauling, tending water supply, maintaining chains, rods and pump components; human prime-moving effort could be reorganised rather than eliminated.

**entry barriers:** Mechanical craft skills, access to materials and mine finance; exact apprenticeship routes and exclusion unknown.

**bargaining ownership:** Owners allocate capital; treatise acknowledges provisioning and wage demands. Actual division of gains is not measured.

**transition:** Later descriptions indicate changes over decades; no followed worker or cohort transition.

**distribution:** No verified group-specific earnings or welfare series. Heavy machinery introduced hazards as well as reduced hauling requirements.

**evidence limit:** Sources are strongest on apparatus and stated decision criteria, weak on workers’ options, incomes and displacement rates.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: site-specific power access; transmission engineering; repairability; capital and operating costs; provisioning and political security

First-use dates and prevalence are uncertain; celebratory images can be selective; adequate hydraulics delaying steam remains a hypothesis.
