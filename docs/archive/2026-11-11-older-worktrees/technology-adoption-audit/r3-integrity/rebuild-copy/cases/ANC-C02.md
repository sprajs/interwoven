# ANC-C02 — Barbegal's watermill complex: seasonal operation and staged cessation

**Period:** Second–third centuries CE; terminal reconstruction concerns selected western mills' final roughly 8–9 years. **Region:** Roman Gaul; Barbegal/Arles, present-day southern France. **Domain:** grain milling; hydropower; maintenance.

Material traces establish operating watermills and changes in equipment; market destination and final abandonment cause remain uncertain.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/ANC/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No first-invention claim; the case concerns an installation.

**feasibility:** Operational deposits and architecture support repeated use.

**deployment:** Sixteen wheel positions in two trains; selected surviving casts.

**diffusion:** No regional denominator; Barbegal does not establish commonness.

**scaling:** Substantial integrated capacity, without adopted output tonnage.

**non-adoption/niche:** Seasonal operation inferred; possible ship-biscuit market not confirmed.

**displacement/reversal:** Different western flumes ceased in stages while water continued; later reuse occurs.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Two trains of eight aqueduct-powered wheels; terminal evidence specifically from three western flumes.

**task:** Grinding grain; final consumer and possible biscuit manufacture uncertain.

**population:** Operators and consumers of this installation, identities and number unknown.

**interval:** Second–third centuries CE, with distinct terminal subepisodes per flume.

**contemporary alternatives:** Hand, animal and smaller watermills as contemporaneous technological alternatives; no evidence the owner explicitly priced and rejected them.

**adoption measure:** Installation configuration plus sampled isotope/microstratigraphic sequences. No population of eligible mills or percentage adoption.

**observation limits:** Wood decayed; carbonate preserves selected parts and years. Wheel positions are not year-round output.

## Actors

**chooser:** Commissioner/proprietor unknown in inspected sources.

**payer:** Financing and ownership unknown; substantial construction does not identify public or private payer.

**operator maintainer:** Milling and woodworking/repair workers implied by physical interventions; legal status and pay unknown.

**affected nonchooser:** Potential grain suppliers, consumers and workers, but identities and distributions not recovered.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown for Barbegal workers; Roman slavery elsewhere cannot determine their status.

## Labour

**tasks:** Grinding energy supplied by water; grain handling, millstone care, woodworking and flume adjustment persisted. Net labour change unmeasured.

**entry barriers:** Access to an appropriate supply/fall, machinery and repair competence; entry cost not quantified.

**bargaining ownership:** Ownership and allocation of saved effort or revenue unknown.

**transition:** Years of component replacement are visible; worker biographies or displacement duration are not.

**distribution:** No observed wages, consumption distribution or compensation for displaced millers.

**evidence limit:** Capacity cannot be translated into employment eliminated or general emancipation from labour.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Water infrastructure and machinery complement one another.; Repair preserves capacity; water availability alone is insufficient.; Demand and seasonal operating schedules may interact.

Shared carbonate archive limits independence of studies; discharge, markets, ownership and the eastern train's exact fate are incompletely known.

- [ANC-K06](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k06): Three western Barbegal flumes indicate staged terminal operation while aqueduct water continued; loss of water supply alone does not explain that observed sequence.
- [ANC-K07](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k07): Roman productive waterpower and enslaved hydraulic maintenance contradict an absolute incompatibility between slavery and complex machinery; the effect of labour costs on marginal adoption remains unidentified.
