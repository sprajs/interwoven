# MAT-C07 — Ile-Ife glass: local primary production within exchange networks

**Period:** Primarily eleventh–fifteenth centuries CE; later Osogbo comparison remains a lead. **Region:** Africa; Nigeria: Ile-Ife and Igbo Olokun; West African distribution. **Domain:** glass; technical ceramics; beads.

Chemical groups, crucibles and semi-finished batch support local glassmaking; they do not settle all origins or organisation questions.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MAT/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Distinct local recipe documented; exact first development and external intellectual contributions unresolved.

**feasibility:** Finished glass and partially reacted batch demonstrate conversion from raw materials, beyond imported-glass remelting.

**deployment:** Bead-making debris and specialised crucibles document workshop activity.

**diffusion:** Related glass at other West African sites supports object exchange; craft migration is not thereby proved.

**scaling:** Many recovered beads and kilogram-scale batch capacities support substantial activity; no annual output estimate.

**non-adoption/niche:** Imported glass coexisted in the wider setting; analysed local workshop samples cannot measure city-wide preferences.

**displacement/reversal:** No demonstrated reason or precise date for cessation; later related production may reflect continuity, movement or redevelopment.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Ile-Ife high-lime/high-alumina and related glass made and worked in crucibles, principally for beads

**task:** Primary glass formation, colouring and bead manufacture; uses include adornment but buyer-specific priorities remain unmeasured

**population:** Excavated Igbo Olokun workshop debris and 52 selected analysed beads; additional museum-store fragments; users at other sites inferred from finds

**interval:** Primarily eleventh–fifteenth centuries CE; later Osogbo comparison remains a lead

**contemporary alternatives:** Imported soda-lime glass objects documented elsewhere at Ile-Ife; Secondary working of imported glass is a technically possible route and historically relevant comparator; Other local bead materials are possible demand substitutes; task-specific comparisons not inspected

**adoption measure:** Thousands of finds and batch-capacity estimates establish activity; chemistry of 52 selected beads and regional occurrences identify composition/distribution. No annual output or consumer denominator.

**observation limits:** Disturbed deposits, small selected chemical sample and museum provenance constrain chronology. Distribution of objects does not demonstrate transmission of manufacturing skill.

## Actors

**chooser:** Glassmakers chose materials/processes; purchaser and political control over production remain unknown.

**payer:** Unknown; bead quantity and prestige association do not identify workshop finance.

**operator maintainer:** Raw-material processors, crucible makers, glassmakers and bead workers inferred from production sequence; whether separate people is unknown.

**affected nonchooser:** Fuel collectors, surrounding residents and consumers outside the workshop; their outcomes unmeasured.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown; no workshop contracts, rights or coercive institutions established by the inspected analyses.

## Labour

**tasks:** Raw-batch preparation, crucible fabrication, firing, colouring and bead forming; may involve overlapping practitioners.

**entry barriers:** Thermal/material knowledge, appropriate ceramics and supplies; apprenticeship or restricted access unknown.

**bargaining ownership:** Workshop ownership, political control and distribution of returns unresolved.

**transition:** Broad workshop period and possible later related sites; no artisan/cohort histories.

**distribution:** No measured income, gender division, occupational harms or ordinary-consumer access.

**evidence limit:** Recovered beads and vessel capacities cannot be converted into employment or productivity per worker.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: local mineral recipe; refractory ceramics; colouring and bead-forming skill; trade and demand; reuse of crucibles

Disturbed excavation deposits and museum provenance limit sequencing; local primary production does not logically prove isolation.

## Evidence links

- [MAT-K20](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k20): Chemical study of 52 Igbo Olokun beads supports locally distinctive glass groups; related beads elsewhere support exchange, while imported glass at other Ile-Ife sites rules out a wholly isolated setting.
- [MAT-K21](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k21): Ile-Ife glass crucibles were specialised ceramic complements; reported usable capacities are evidence of batch capability, not a measurement of sustained industrial throughput.
- [MAT-K22](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k22): Incompletely reacted glass batch on Ile-Ife crucibles directly strengthens the case for local primary glassmaking, while recipe uniqueness alone cannot establish an origin independent of all external ideas.
- [MAT-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k24): Across these purposively selected cases, repeatable processes, complementary crafts and task-specific demand are better-supported comparison variables than a universal ranking of materials.
