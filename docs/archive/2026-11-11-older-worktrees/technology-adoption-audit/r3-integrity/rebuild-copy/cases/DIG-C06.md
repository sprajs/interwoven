# DIG-C06 — VR gaming and prolonged computer work: three different outcome measures

**Period:** 2022–2024 reporting of 2022/2023 observations. **Region:** United Kingdom; Germany; Meta worldwide operations. **Domain:** virtual reality; consumer gaming; knowledge work.

UK participation, German work experience and global segment finances are kept separate; none supplies a global retained-user measure.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/DIG/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Outside episode.

**feasibility:** Commercial Quest 2 used for matched virtual-desktop experiment.

**deployment:** UK reported gaming and tested office configuration, not all VR uses.

**diffusion:** Single 2023 UK cross-section; no valid growth estimate from summary typo.

**scaling:** Meta 2023 segment resources/revenue, not user scaling.

**non-adoption/niche:** VR gaming minority compared with other devices in UK 2023; experimental withdrawals in prolonged work.

**displacement/reversal:** No evidence of broad replacement of conventional computers; no claim of permanent failure.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** UK 2023 VR gaming-headset question; separate Quest 2/Chrome Remote Desktop/K830 week-long work setup; separate Meta FY 2023 Reality Labs accounts.

**task:** Gaming in UK; prolonged ordinary desktop work in experiment; corporate investment/revenue accounting.

**population:** UK adults 16+ n 3643; 16 German-university worker completers plus 2 withdrawals; Meta consolidated segment, no user denominator.

**interval:** UK 27 Sep–12 Nov 2023; study reported Jun 2022, exact fieldwork dates not established; Meta FY ended 31 Dec 2023.

**contemporary alternatives:** Phone/console/tablet/computer gaming; Physical matched display and keyboard for experiment; Other VR/AR configurations not tested

**adoption measure:** Gaming participation 4% VR vs matched device questions; no weekly/monthly threshold. Experimental subjective ratings and withdrawals; segment revenue/loss, not shipments or active users.

**observation limits:** No pooling across these populations/measures. Quota survey, small occupational experiment and broad commercial segment; no global installed-base/retention estimate.

## Actors

**chooser:** Individual gamers; voluntary study participants; Meta management allocates resources. These are different chooser sets.

**payer:** Household/device purchasers unknown; study equipment provided under research design; company funds investment. Per-headset subsidy unmeasured.

**operator maintainer:** Users/developers/device vendors; researchers controlled test apparatus. Maintenance cost not measured.

**affected nonchooser:** Household members/bystanders and workers expected to use immersive interfaces are not adequately sampled.

**ability to refuse:** Two participants exercised withdrawal; general consumer ability to refuse workplace VR unknown. No inference of coercion from lab results.

## Labour

**tasks:** Prolonged knowledge-work interface tested; developer/content/research work implied by company operations but employment counts not derived.

**entry barriers:** Hardware/access, skills and fit constraints; occupational entry pathways and credentials unknown.

**bargaining ownership:** Employer/vendor ownership and platform terms could matter; experiment does not identify bargaining power.

**transition:** One week per environment, no retraining/career transition follow-up; corporate finances not worker trajectories.

**distribution:** Individual discomfort and withdrawals observed; no distribution of wages/jobs or representative disability outcomes.

**evidence limit:** No inference that VR replaces offices/workers or improves/damages all productivity; subjective ratings are not output.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Duration-specific wearing/interface costs; Task fit and available content; Social coordination, price and surroundings awareness remain unranked candidates

Younger adult VR participation higher; small long-work study does not test optimised spatial tasks; segment losses do not imply zero utility.

## Evidence links

- [DIG-K18](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k18): Ofcom’s 2023 Core Survey of UK adults 16+ reports gaming on VR devices 4%, phones 34%, consoles 22%, tablets 14%, laptops/netbooks 10%, desktops 8%; responses are non-exclusive and have no weekly/monthly threshold.
- [DIG-K19](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k19): A 2022 Quest 2 study found worse subjective ratings for prolonged VR desktop work than a matched physical setup among 16 university-worker completers; two additional participants withdrew on the first VR day.
- [DIG-K20](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k20): Meta reported FY 2023 Reality Labs revenue $1.896 billion and operating loss $16.120 billion for a segment including AR, mixed reality and VR hardware/software/content.
- [DIG-K21](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k21): The inspected Ofcom statistics establish minority UK VR gaming participation in 2023 relative to matched devices; they do not establish a global installed base, fixed-window use or retention rate.
- [DIG-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k24): Pocket internet, performance, ergonomics and cost cannot be causally ranked from these observations; the claim that most laptops cost less than most smartphones is unestablished by this source set.
