# GHA-C03 — Krachi–Salaga–Kpandai feeder roads: farmer initiative and maintenance tied to lorry use

**Period:** 1928–1945, with separately identified later oral context. **Region:** Krachi–Salaga–Kpandai area; Northern Territories/British Togoland; Ghana; West Africa. **Domain:** feeder roads; food marketing; community labour; maintenance.

Named village initiatives sought feeder roads and outside technical resources; a reported 1945 interruption of exports coincided with deterioration of one route, showing a conditional maintenance problem.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/GHA/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No road or vehicle invention claim.

**feasibility:** Cleared routes required bridges, culverts, suitable surfaces and lorry service to work as motor connections.

**deployment:** Bajamse sought route alignment in 1928; other named village connections and subsequent grants are recorded in 1937.

**diffusion:** Selected connections expanded by 1945 in the source; no complete denominator of villages, road users or failed requests.

**scaling:** Villagers’ labour and local knowledge were combined with scarce materials, skilled construction and administrative assistance.

**non-adoption/niche:** Assistance and participation were uneven; later oral examples are not backdated as evidence for every interwar village.

**displacement/reversal:** Bladjai’s road reportedly deteriorated when fuel shortages stopped exports in April 1945; duration and eventual recovery not established here.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Farmer/village feeder connections to larger roads and lorry-based food marketing, with materials and skilled work sought from officials/native authorities.

**task:** Connect villages to lorry routes and southern food markets; maintain usable passage when marketing continues.

**population:** Named village initiatives and reported Bladjai maintenance sequence; later interview populations are supplementary context, not the same observed 1928 cohort.

**interval:** 1928 Bajamse request; 1937 connections/grants; expansion and April 1945 Bladjai interruption. Later oral accounts are identified as later, without assigning their undated details to this interval.

**contemporary alternatives:** head-loading or bush tracks; remaining off motor routes; petitions for government bridge/material support; collective village work; hiring labour where finance permitted

**adoption measure:** Dated petitions, reported connections/grants and one qualitative maintenance reversal; no ownership, traffic, household benefit or participation-rate denominator.

**observation limits:** Selected administrative entries and later oral history; no systematic failed-project sample, unobserved household disagreement or weather-adjusted comparison.

## Actors

**chooser:** Farmers and villagers initiated particular requests; chiefs or other representatives negotiated; officials/native authorities selected assistance and expenditure.

**payer:** Villagers supplied labour and some local resources; administrative grants, tools, materials and skilled work supplied other inputs. Household burden shares unknown.

**operator maintainer:** Local road workers and hired specialists where obtained; lorry operators transported produce and farmers sometimes paid freight.

**affected nonchooser:** Households contributing labour or unable to use the road, adjacent land users and people whose request was denied; their separate outcomes are poorly recorded.

**ability to refuse:** Requests and village initiatives support local demand, but do not establish each resident’s consent. Later evidence of weak chiefs or failed collective participation informs interpretation without proving identical conditions in every interwar village.

## Labour

**tasks:** Clearing and recurring upkeep, materials gathering and seeking skilled bridge/culvert work, agricultural marketing and haulage.

**entry barriers:** Cost of bringing materials and attracting skilled workers, access to grants and lorry service/fuel; unequal assistance documented qualitatively.

**bargaining ownership:** Villagers could negotiate labour for assistance; officials controlled some technical/fiscal resources. Ownership of produce or lorry service remained distinct from performing road labour.

**transition:** Route-specific creation and decline rather than tracked occupational mobility. 1945 reversal does not establish permanent abandonment.

**distribution:** Potentially useful market access does not establish equal net benefit; full farm, maintenance, freight and household accounts absent.

**evidence limit:** No demonstrated worker-income gain or universal voluntariness; a qualitative export interruption does not identify a maintenance elasticity.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: local demand for market access; technical and fiscal complements; conditional maintenance incentives; uneven participation

Some routes in the same area had earlier coercive origins; a local chief’s petition does not prove all-household consent. Fuel disruption has potential confounders.
