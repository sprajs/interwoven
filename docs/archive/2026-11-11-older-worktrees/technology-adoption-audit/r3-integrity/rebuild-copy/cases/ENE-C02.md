# ENE-C02 — Nuclear construction: coordinated programmes, cost variation and finance boundaries

**Period:** Construction cohorts 1954–2008; French programme 1970–2000; US accounting 1976–1987 and cancellation context 2017. **Region:** United States; France; South Korea. **Domain:** energy; large-scale construction.

National completed-project cost histories and component accounts challenge both automatic learning and inevitable escalation. Rapid coordinated construction can coexist with rising unit costs; finance and cancellations remain outside overnight-cost trends.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/ENE/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Nuclear fission/reactor invention precedes this construction comparison; no invention claim made.

**feasibility:** Commercial reactor configurations were imported, licensed, standardized and revised; feasibility did not settle project cost.

**deployment:** France built a large PWR fleet under concentrated procurement; South Korea adopted commercial imports and domestic derivatives.

**diffusion:** Installed fleets and construction cohorts, not individual electricity-user choices.

**scaling:** Bulk orders, domestic manufacturing/engineering and multi-unit projects; cost effects differ by cohort and site.

**non-adoption/niche:** Completed-reactor datasets omit cancelled proposals and selected experimental designs.

**displacement/reversal:** VC Summer expansion abandoned in 2017 in the inspected US account; no systematic cancellation rate estimated.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Commercial power-reactor cohorts; France PWR programme; Korea imported and domestic designs; US Westinghouse four-loop cost accounts, with 2017 cancellation as a separate outcome.

**task:** Construct and operate grid-connected bulk electricity supply under national security, economic and regulatory objectives.

**population:** 349 reactors in seven-country compilation (focus US/France/Korea), completed-project selection; EEDB representative US plant accounts are a different population.

**interval:** Country construction-start cohorts through 2008 in the historical compilation; US accounting 1976–1987; historical cancellation observed in 2017.

**contemporary alternatives:** Coal/oil/gas generation under period-specific fuel prices; Hydroelectricity where sites available; Different reactor designs, imported or domestic; Delayed construction or cancellation

**adoption measure:** Completed reactor capacity/cohorts and construction time; overnight currency per kW in 2010 domestic prices. No uniform whole-project adoption hazard.

**observation limits:** Missing proposed/cancelled projects, financing, some experimental routes and major countries; different technologies, periods, deflators and record quality.

## Actors

**chooser:** Government/utility planners, regulators, reactor vendors and financing authorities; households generally did not choose the generation fleet.

**payer:** Utility capital, lenders and state-backed programmes, ultimately allocated through rates/taxes or losses; precise incidence not quantified.

**operator maintainer:** Engineering organizations, construction contractors, plant operators and fuel-cycle workers.

**affected nonchooser:** Electricity customers, site communities, construction workers, waste/fuel-cycle communities and taxpayers.

**ability to refuse:** France’s history describes concentrated technocratic decisions and weak public influence; this is not consent. Korean community/worker refusal channels and US project-level bargaining are unestablished in inspected cost records.

## Labour

**tasks:** Engineering design, scheduling, purchasing, on-site supervision, construction and quality assurance; operation continues after construction.

**entry barriers:** Large capital commitments, engineering capability, qualified vendors and safety requirements; individual credential thresholds not reconstructed.

**bargaining ownership:** Utility/vendor/state control of project choices; cost accounts reveal expenditure rather than worker bargaining or who absorbs overruns.

**transition:** No worker/cohort tracking across construction pauses or cancelled plants; costs cannot establish retraining outcomes.

**distribution:** Higher indirect spending is observed in a model; no supported wage, employment-security or regional-income distribution.

**evidence limit:** Cost and completion histories cannot settle occupational gains, welfare, accident risk or benefits from avoided fossil pollution.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Programme coordination; Licensed designs and standardization; Site-specific construction execution; Capital commitment and interest exposure; Scope and safety changes

Cross-country trajectories are not matched experiments; interest excluded, cancellations missing, and later French data revise earlier estimates.

## Evidence links

- [ENE-K05](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#ene-k05): The 2016 seven-country reactor-cost compilation records differing within-country overnight-cost trajectories, including declines across Korean construction cohorts and escalation in later US/French cohorts; it does not identify a universal sign of nuclear learning.
- [ENE-K06](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#ene-k06): France’s coordinated PWR programme combined bulk ordering, licensed designs, regulatory continuity and EDF engineering capacity with rapid deployment, yet its later construction cohorts still experienced real cost escalation.
- [ENE-K07](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#ene-k07): In Eash-Gates et al.’s US four-loop-plant reconstruction for 1976–1987, indirect accounts contribute 72% of the modeled cost increase, placing engineering, supervision and construction organization alongside hardware and regulatory requirements.
- [ENE-K08](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#ene-k08): Overnight reactor construction cost excludes interest during construction, and completed-project cost trajectories omit the outcome of cancellation; they cannot by themselves establish investor affordability or system welfare.
