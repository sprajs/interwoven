# MED-C07 — Oral rehydration research and household delivery in Bangladesh

**Period:** 1968–1993. **Region:** Dhaka, East Pakistan/Bangladesh; Calcutta and India–Bangladesh border refugee camps; Rural Bangladesh. **Domain:** health; household care; community implementation.

Operational evidence and household teaching mattered, but competence, product availability and use were distinct; an uptake denominator discrepancy is preserved.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MED/report.md).

## Stages

**feasibility:** Late-1960s practical trials and 1971 caregiver-administered camp treatment reported in historical scholarship.

**deployment:** Household teaching and ORS distribution through several organizations.

**scaling:** BRAC's 1980s national-scale teaching campaign; reach is not successful use.

**diffusion:** 1993 sample provides episode-based use measures and preparation tests.

**displacement/reversal:** Packaged ORS grew alongside continued home preparation; no claim that home use disappeared.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: research/camp oral rehydration delivery. B: home-prepared lobon-gur solution and packaged ORS supported by BRAC/community/distribution networks.

**task:** Prevent or treat diarrhoeal dehydration; households could frame the task as stopping visible diarrhoea instead.

**population:** A: defined clinical/camp populations in retrospective history. B: 90 sampled villages from three BRAC programme phases, not all Bangladesh.

**interval:** A 1968–1971; B 1980–1990 teaching, 1993 evaluation.

**contemporary alternatives:** Intravenous treatment where available; Other home fluids; Packaged ORS versus home preparation; Other medicines and no treatment

**adoption measure:** B: recalled episodes in preceding two weeks; Table 2 broad ORT rates 51.8/54.5/53.0% with 1525/1114/1114 episodes; separate chemical testing of 269 preparations.

**observation limits:** Abstract 60% differs from all-episode table; discussion excludes dysentery. ORT includes other home fluids; no random assignment of teaching, exposure or products.

## Actors

**chooser:** Researchers and programme designers shaped delivery; caregivers selected responses, with medical and household advice.

**payer:** NGO/government/donor programme resources; households supplied care time and sometimes purchased packets.

**operator maintainer:** Female teaching workers, monitors, retailers, medical staff and unpaid household caregivers.

**affected nonchooser:** Infants, children and ill adults dependent on others' recognition and care.

**ability to refuse:** No compulsory household participation reported; illness, care dependency, money and availability constrained effective options. Research consent histories not established here.

## Labour

**tasks:** Paid teaching/monitoring and retail production/distribution complemented unpaid preparation and administration.

**entry barriers:** Teaching and preparation skills; product access and time; exact hiring barriers unexamined.

**bargaining ownership:** BRAC monitored teaching performance and linked pay to assessed learning; caregivers' bargaining and income effects unknown.

**transition:** Decade-long teaching phases; no longitudinal employment/wage outcomes.

**distribution:** Knowledge and reported use differentiated by episode/campaign cohort; not a livelihood distribution.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer liberation of caregiver time or net professional displacement from a simple formulation.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: caregiver skills; trusted demonstration; monitoring; convenience and distribution; perceived purpose; measurement definitions

Programme-affiliated evaluation, recall, broad fluid definition and contemporary changes prevent isolating a campaign effect.

## Evidence links

- [MED-K19](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k19): The 1971 refugee-camp implementation led by Mahalanabis demonstrated family-administered oral rehydration in an emergency, extending evidence beyond tightly staffed research settings.
- [MED-K20](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k20): BRAC’s 1980s programme used household demonstration, monitoring and wider community engagement; its 1993 evaluation sampled 90 villages from three programme phases. Teaching coexisted with expanding packaged-ORS distribution.
- [MED-K21](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k21): Chowdhury et al. Table 2 reports 51.8%, 54.5% and 53.0% ORT use for all reported diarrhoeal episodes across cohorts; the abstract’s 60% conflicts with that denominator, while the discussion obtains over 60% after excluding dysentery.
