**P2-SP2-02 — Retain the difference between reward responsiveness and necessary incentives.**

Target: [SYNTHESIS.md, §6](full-snapshot/analysis/SYNTHESIS.md), line 69, the sentence saying Malawi experiments show that communication “requires work, incentives and contacts.” PSY-K07, PSY-C03, SYN's bounded behavioural claim and Candidate B already use more defensible language.

In the inspected copyedited advance version of BenYishay and Mobarak, Table 5 reports dissemination activity in unrewarded arms as well as a strong increase in rewarded peer-farmer effort. Table 6 reports recipient knowledge gains in some unrewarded officer/lead-farmer arms. Table 7 supports the important rewarded-peer adoption result. These observations support responsiveness under the experimental arrangements; they do not establish that rewards are necessary for communication, or that contacts and rewards are an invariably required bundle. Identity and communicator number also differ between lead and peer strategies. [Original paper](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/45/2021/11/Benyishay_Mobarak_Malawi.pdf), pp. 9–10, 18–22.

This matters because the prospective argument concerns when useful intermediation becomes work that people can enter and be paid for. A necessity formulation can lend that argument more support than the experiment provides. Replace it with observed responsiveness of effort, communication and uptake to rewards and contact arrangements, while retaining unrewarded activity and the absence of a general livelihood inference. No change to the supported experimental result is needed.
