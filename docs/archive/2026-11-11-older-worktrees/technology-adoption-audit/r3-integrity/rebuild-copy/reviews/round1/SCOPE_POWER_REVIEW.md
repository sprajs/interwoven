# Independent review: historical scope, power and comparison

Reviewer: independent scope/power review, 5 November 2026. Target: the immutable [round-1 snapshot](snapshot/MANIFEST.json), created 2026-11-05T14:27:23.496317+00:00. The snapshot has 59 cases, 210 claims, 129 source records and 41 comparisons. All 169 manifest-listed files matched their recorded hashes when checked. No snapshot or contribution files were changed. Other round-1 reviews and subsequent parent repairs were not read.

**Decision: make one assertion-scope correction before accepting this edition; retain the principal synthesis as a bounded comparative research argument.** Four P2 findings concern the historical account or its translation into future-role research, rather than demonstrated falsehoods. Two P3 refinements improve exports and comparison lineage. The programme does considerably more than attach caveats to a European progress narrative: many non-European cases define their own tasks, configurations and alternatives. The major remaining risk is that a careful local account becomes a stronger rejection, a thin account of power, or an opportunity analogy when compressed.

P1 here means an existing substantive assertion requires correction or withdrawal. P2 means a material evidence, selection or actor-scope limitation, with no inference that the opposite proposition is established. P3 means a bounded representational or methodological refinement. Planned Oceania, state/administration, psychology, repair and energy investigations are not evidence in this review and close none of its findings.

**P1-SP01 — Narrow the rejected Chinese-script hypothesis to the proposition actually tested.**

Targets: [HYPOTHESES_AND_NEGATIVE_CASES.md](snapshot/analysis/HYPOTHESES_AND_NEGATIVE_CASES.md), line 13; NET-K08, especially `inference_limits` and `test.strongest_rival`; NET-X01 `conclusion_limit`; corresponding contribution records and generated ledgers.

The table rejects the proposition that Chinese characters alone explain movable type's *limited relative uptake*. NET-K08's statement supports a narrower conclusion: the writing system does not specify the method for **every task**. These are different hypotheses. The existence of genealogical and imperial type projects rejects practical impossibility and a deterministic all-tasks rule. It does not, without comparative costs and an outcome denominator, reject a claim that character-inventory costs explain much of a low aggregate type share. Large runs, selected character sets or subsidies could permit niches while those costs remained important elsewhere. This is a logical compatibility argument, not a finding that script costs actually dominated.

I independently checked McDermott and Burke's introduction, printed pp. 11–15. It describes production arrangements and identifies niches; it does not provide a matched decomposition of their relative causes. NET-K07 already describes the cost/institution distinction as underdetermined. [Publisher preview](https://hkupress.hku.hk/image/catalog/pdf-preview/9789888208081.pdf).

Acceptance: rewrite the rejected proposition as practical impossibility or a uniform method implied by script; put the explanation of limited relative uptake under “not established/underdetermined”; align NET-K08 and NET-X01's rejection language. Preserve the supported production contrasts. No new archive is needed for this correction. Do not replace the rejected technological determinism with an equally unmeasured institutional explanation.

**P2-SP02 — Put project-level coercive leverage into the Gambia actor record, rather than locating constraint mainly inside the household.**

Targets: AGR-C03 `actors.chooser`, `actors.ability_to_refuse`, `labour.bargaining_ownership`; AGR-K07–K09 and AGR-X02; SYN-K12 and the Jahaly-Pacharr paragraph in [SYNTHESIS.md](snapshot/analysis/SYNTHESIS.md). The visible refusal field is [AGR-C03.md](snapshot/cases/AGR-C03.md), line 51.

The record correctly preserves women's agency, uneven alternatives and the difference between household income and individual control. However, its refusal field names household obligations and weaker outside options while omitting a directly documented enforcement mechanism. Carney reports that project access depended on production-credit repayment; threatened loss of usufruct enforced discipline. Her dated 1990 account describes plot seizures after arrears and a negotiated opportunity to recover them. The report also describes the project removing local control over irrigation access. These are statements in the inspected source, not unknown ancient institutions or a request for a new consent survey. [Carney 1992](https://media.odi.org/documents/8169.pdf), printed pp. 5–6, 10–12, 16.

The omission makes the structured causal account look too much like a technical package subsequently distorted by local patriarchy. Donor/project contract design and household authority jointly shaped the feasible choices. Conversely, the source does not justify labelling every participant or every workday coerced.

Acceptance: add a dated, attributed claim about the credit–land-access sanction and its enforcement; distinguish management's power to evict, household heads' control of crop proceeds, women's withdrawal/negotiation, and differences in outside land access. Carry it into the comparison and one synthesis sentence. Treat project enforcement and household reclassification as potentially interacting pathways, not automatically exclusive rivals. Retain the separate timing and observational limits of Carney and IFPRI.

**P2-SP03 — Test persistence against the adverse case already present in the Chinese textile source.**

Targets: IND-C07; IND-K20–K22; IND-X03; SYN-X02; [FUTURE_ROLES.md](snapshot/analysis/FUTURE_ROLES.md), Candidate D, line 51; the selection register's treatment of household labour and continued older practice.

The pre-1949 case is carefully bounded. [IND-C07.md](snapshot/cases/IND-C07.md), lines 39 and 65, correctly refuse to project later rationing backward. Independent checking confirms the reported hybrid yarn account. The selection issue is different: this contribution uses the historical background of Eyferth's article but excludes the period that contains its original interview/archival investigation. That later research examines persistence amid state procurement, scarce cash and cloth rations, gendered obligations, and burdensome unpaid work. It supplies a direct contrast to interpreting continued household production through technical fit alone. [Eyferth 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-social-history/article/womens-work-and-the-politics-of-homespun-in-socialist-china-19491980/1B4DC63727682F1EEC958A62A4126757), pp. 373–391.

This is not a falsehood in the existing case, nor evidence that all earlier persistence was imposed. It is a consequential selection: the omitted countercase is concrete, accessible, and connected to the same task and source, rather than a generic call for more countries.

Acceptance: create a distinct post-1949 case or explicit subepisode, preserve its own locations, source populations and institutions, and compare the different routes to persistence. Do not pool its evidence with pre-1949 quantities or count the same article twice as independent corroboration. If excluded, give a substantive selection reason and narrow Candidate D's historical basis to task persistence; do not treat it as evidence of desirable independent work. The useful discriminant is whether continued use follows task advantage, inability to obtain the substitute, or an ability to shift work onto someone with weak refusal options.

**P2-SP04 — Give African actors a transport history beyond responding to a colonial railway.**

Targets: MOB-C04, especially `actors.chooser` and `labour`; MOB-K11–K14; MOB-X02–X03; the Ghana paragraph in SYNTHESIS. [MOB-C04.md](snapshot/cases/MOB-C04.md), line 43, currently assigns initial choice to governors/planners and describes African farmers as respondents to opportunities.

The bounded spatial result and its welfare limits are defensible. I checked the January 2015 Jedwab–Moradi manuscript; its rail, crop, settlement and later-road outcomes are different measurements, as the case says. It is not a full history of African transport choice. [Author manuscript](https://alexandermoradi.org/research/rail.pdf).

Austin's inspected scholarship supplies a discriminating addition: African actors demanded or built local roads and owned/operated lorries, while land, credit and labour arrangements changed with cocoa cultivation. It also preserves coercive labour alongside later changes in employment relations. Thus “African agency” cannot be represented by a single autonomous farmer any more than by a passive colonial subject. The source links transport to institutions rather than offering a simple alternative cause that invalidates rail effects. [Austin 2007, accepted manuscript](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/4392/1/Labour_and_land_in_Ghana_(LSERO_version).pdf), printed manuscript pp. 5–7 and 17–18.

Acceptance: add a bounded road/lorry subepisode or comparison and distinguish growers, chiefs, owner-drivers, hired/migrant workers and obligated labour where evidence permits. Ask whether the same transport function could be supplied with different ownership and work arrangements, and who could enter them. Record shared historical references between the new source and the rail literature; this is additional actor evidence, not an independent replication of the rail estimate. No Ghana-wide welfare balance follows. This repair directly changes the comparison's causal alternatives; adding another African location without such a test would not close it.

**P2-SP05 — Use observed barriers to complementary work to discipline the future-role candidates.**

Targets: [FUTURE_ROLES.md](snapshot/analysis/FUTURE_ROLES.md), lines 15–59 and 77–83; LBR-C01; NET-C06; MED-C07; ANC-C03; SYN-X04 and SYN-K16.

The document's stated status is appropriate: research propositions, not occupational forecasts. It distinguishes contribution, value capture and entry, and requires modern evidence. I do not find a hidden claim that all five roles will remain human or profitable. The limitation is that its historical examples chiefly establish the existence of useful tasks, while the proposed tasks' access restrictions are left in other case records or expressed generically.

There is a more discriminating test in the already cited labour source. Feigenbaum and Gross discuss maintenance technicians as a possible complement to telephone automation but note that these workers were almost all men; the study does not find the relevant female cohort moving into other telephone-industry jobs. That is a direct warning against moving from maintenance demand to an accessible transition for displaced operators. [Published paper](https://www.dpgross.com/papers/answering-call-automation.pdf), pp. 1928–1929, Figure V. Likewise the 1498 voyage narrative documents pressure on a ruler through seizure of a servant when obtaining a pilot; it does not establish the pilot's consent or remuneration. NET-C06 preserves that distinction, whereas Candidate B's historical sentence mentions learned knowledge only. [Primary narrative in translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46440/old/46440-h/46440-h.htm), pp. 45–47.

Acceptance: attach these counterexamples locally to Candidates A/D and B, or supply equally discriminating alternatives. For each candidate, record whether the historical evidence observes task necessity, who performed it, voluntary access, remuneration/value capture, and an actual transition; leave missing cells explicitly unmeasured. Keep modern tests as a gate rather than as implied results. A new repair or psychology case closes this issue only if it measures the relevant access or value-capture margin.

| Candidate | What the historical examples can currently support | Evidence required before a stronger conditional opportunity claim |
|---|---|---|
| A: reliability | A process or installation can need adaptation; Meroe experiments expose operating variables | A contemporary failure constraint, a contribution attributable to a person, access/authority and a payer; distinguish ancient workers from modern experimenters |
| B: competent use | Interpretation, teaching and care are distinct tasks | Which work is paid, unpaid or compelled; whether the constraint is learning; whether contributors retain decision rights and returns |
| C: coordination | Other actors' choices can affect service | Compare feasible governance arrangements and include sanctions, veto, exclusion and distribution; coordination success for an authority is not sufficient |
| D: repair/adaptation | Older configurations can continue to perform tasks | Actual repair demand, permitted access, entry costs, compensation and feasible replacement; coexistence does not establish an independent repair occupation |
| E: evaluation | A metric can omit an actor or outcome | Evidence that a better measure changes decisions, that the evaluator can report inconvenient findings, and that someone rewards the work |

These are promotion conditions, not a demand to complete five contemporary market studies before publishing a clearly labelled question set.

**P3-SP07 — Specify what is connected in the comparison lineage.**

SYN-X03 `connections_nonindependence` says that some cases lie within imperial/state systems. Roman aqueducts, Gambian rice, one Chinese prescribing audit and colonial Indian railways do not become the same empirical history because all involve authority. SYN-X04's shared census traditions and MOB-X02's shared transport-economics conversation likewise distinguish common measurement or theory from identical underlying observations only loosely. By contrast, AGR-X02's Carney–IFPRI link, IND-X03's connected yarn trade, and MAT-X03's overlapping Ile-Ife research programme are substantively useful lineage statements.

Acceptance: name the relevant IDs and identify the connection as shared original records/dataset, shared inherited account, direct historical interaction, or common analytical framing. State unknown overlap as unknown. This is not a request for complete intellectual genealogy, and it should not convert unrelated cases into independent causal replications.

**Assessment of the wider historical scope.** The sample is geographically broad without being representative. Strong autonomous histories include Meroe's operating practices, Ile-Ife primary glass production, East Asian pottery, Chinese print workflows, Korean rental/manuscript circulation and Atacama household mining. They are not simply graded by when they approached European industrial production. MAT-C06 explicitly refuses to call the absence of a Eurasian-style iron sequence a failure. IND-C06 treats Japanese spinning as adaptation of a package, not an inherent national labour quality. These are substantive strengths, not geographical quota credits.

The imbalance is chiefly in the kinds of observation available. British industrial and modern US cases often supply prices, firm records or occupational series; many other periods appear through elite texts, excavated production, colonial records or externally studied programmes. Those sources answer different questions. A modern intervention's identified local effect cannot lend its certainty to an ancient institution's inferred motive. Nor should the colonial administrative boundary or a modern national tag define a homogeneous chooser. The records usually preserve these distinctions; the comparison and future-role repairs above keep them visible at the level of inference.

Independent checks did not support several possible accusations. Garrido's local-agency interpretation has concrete contrasts between camps and an official installation, although household architecture cannot establish unrestricted autonomy; MOB-C02 retains that uncertainty. Donaldson's rail result concerns a constructed agricultural income measure, and MOB-C03 does not turn it into household disposable income or a verdict on colonial rule. The Indian textile records explicitly retain linked Atlantic markets, asymmetrical political authority and shared scholarly evidence; they do not constitute independent country experiments. These protections should survive editing. [Garrido 2016](https://www.academia.edu/26536064/Rethinking_Imperial_Infrastructure_A_bottom_up_perspective_on_the_Inca_Road); [Donaldson 2018](https://dave-donaldson.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Donaldson_RRRaj_AER.pdf).

The v2 fields mostly do real work. AGR-C01 separates ancient fields from modern reconstruction; ANC-C05 separates Han repair attestations from a modern hydraulic model; NET-C03 separates Jikji from much later rental reading; DIG-C05 separates early M-PESA panels from the 2024 all-provider survey. Slavery, corvée, unpaid care and workers without purchase authority remain explicit where known. Unknown worker wages or consent are not silently filled from output growth. These findings justify retaining the framework rather than demanding implausible precision or rejecting historical work because household panels do not survive.

Selection and survivor bias remain consequential, but the correct response is discriminating contrasts. The most useful additions for this review are the connected Chinese textile periods, African-owned transport with heterogeneous labour arrangements, a task-specific printing cost comparison, and the observed gender barrier to complementary telephone work. None requires an assumption that traditional practices were benign, that states only constrained adoption, or that non-European outcomes need European explanation. Failures of the framework would include finding that a named complement does not alter performance, that its predicted entrant cannot access the role, or that a simpler price/performance account predicts a bounded task equally well.

**Final blocker and repair summary.** Correct P1-SP01 before the next accepted freeze. P2-SP02 is a concrete repair from existing inspected evidence. For P2-SP03–SP04, either incorporate the bounded comparisons or record a reasoned exclusion and limit the claims that use them; pending wave-2 work is insufficient. P2-SP05 permits the present document to remain a conditional question set, but blocks promotion to a substantiated opportunity assessment without the specified evidence. P3 changes should be reflected in both prose and machine-readable exports. Re-review should inspect the changed records and their downstream summaries, not merely an added general disclaimer. No finding licenses a universal adoption law, a net colonial welfare judgment or a forecast of human occupations.
