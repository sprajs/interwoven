These passages prevent the simple circular argument that technologies spread because they met needs, with the needs inferred from their success.

The main limitation is evidential reach. SYN-K10/K11/K12 use extreme rivals—availability suffices, one superiority ranking determines replacement, or one representative voluntary adopter suffices. Counterexamples can refute those claims. They do not by themselves select among the strongest remaining local mechanisms. This distinction is already acknowledged in the methods and uncertainty register; it should govern any future change from a conditional mechanism to a predictive or comparative ranking. A useful next test varies or traces a specified input, incentive, task or right and identifies what the realistic rival would predict differently. More heterogeneous examples alone do not strengthen identification.

The user's digital propositions are handled appropriately: portability is plausible but bundled with price, services and task differences; laptop/smartphone price ordering lacks a defined population; UK VR gaming evidence is not global failure or retention. The audit does not replace those unknowns with a contrary forecast.

**Future-role challenges and limits.** Three actual propositions were tested against original sources, with explicit [access and passage notes](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/r2-roles-network/proposition-audit.json):

- **Incumbent versus entrant outcomes:** the telephone paper supports the distinction, with the wording repair above. Adverse demand conditions test whether alternative tasks are actually available; they cannot be assumed from aggregate resilience.
- **Teaching and competent use:** the ORT paper reports intergenerational knowledge transfer while packaged supply expanded. This challenges a durable paid-teaching niche even if instruction once mattered. Candidate B already anticipates informal/free instruction and access/interface substitutes. Retain the conditional proposition; turn this into a concrete comparison of paid/unpaid time, funding, competent preparation and sustained use. [Chowdhury et al., original pp.58–59](https://academic.oup.com/heapol/article-pdf/12/1/58/6749104/12-1-58.pdf).

The original judicial accounts support this adverse case, not a complete welfare balance. Candidate C already warns about rents, exclusion and nonchoosers. Test incumbent members, other workers, entrants and payers separately; do not substitute aggregate trade gains for compensation or access. [1968 opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/390/261), [1987 opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/822/1203/373792/).

No major unsupported occupational forecast was found in this sample. This is acceptance of a well-bounded research agenda, not validation of contemporary opportunities. The historical evidence more often identifies necessary work than entry, wages, careers or who captures the gains. The role document correctly makes those separate empirical questions.
