# Technology in use: a research collection

Revised research edition, 5 November 2026. **88 bounded cases, 329 claims, 204 source records, 69 explicit comparisons and 299 scoped graph relations.** The collection reaches from early fired materials through ancient, medieval, early-modern and industrial systems to dated digital, manufacturing and behavioural studies. It contains 16 case-bearing contributions plus an integrated methods/inference strand. The second synthesis is now undergoing independent causal and scope review; final acceptance is recorded separately from author validation.

Start with the [synthesis](analysis/SYNTHESIS.md), or open the [interactive research explorer](index.html) to follow cases, dated routes, comparisons and supporting claims/sources. The [chronological/global map](analysis/GLOBAL_MAP.md) covers every included case; each case retains its qualified dates and observation limits.

The historical finding is conditional: useful performance, actual use, distribution of benefits and access to good work are different outcomes. The cases identify local mechanisms and challenge broad narratives; they do not establish a universal adoption law. The [future human roles](analysis/FUTURE_ROLES.md) document turns the evidence into testable questions about contribution, entry and value capture. None of its candidates is a validated present-day career or investment opportunity.

The canonical records are `contributions/*/{cases,sources,claims,graph,comparisons}.json`; integrated machine-readable outputs are in `data/`. Every historical relationship must be read with its scope, source, uncertainty and alternatives. Multiple incoming edges do not establish joint necessity, and chronology/comparison routes add no causal arrows.

## Limits

This is purposive research, with English-accessible scholarship and surviving records strongly shaping coverage. Source counts are not independent confirmations. The acquired source ledger contains 171 full-text, 22 partial-text and 11 abstract records; those categories describe access, not exhaustive reading or independent audit. Archaeological proxies, retrospective interviews, administrative histories, models and experiments support different inferences. The work does not estimate a historical technology-success rate or exhaust all regions, domains and possible explanations.

The user’s smartphone, price and VR propositions remain explicitly bounded: portability is plausible but not isolated as a universal cause; the laptop/smartphone price ordering is unestablished; dated UK VR gaming evidence is not a current global failure verdict.
