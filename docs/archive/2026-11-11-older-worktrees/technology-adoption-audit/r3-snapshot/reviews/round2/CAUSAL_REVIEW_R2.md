## What the revised account establishes

The central improvement is inferential. Observing coexistence can defeat a universal replacement claim without selecting a particular explanation of coexistence. A randomized package can change an outcome without identifying which component or psychological motive caused the change. A physical requirement for producing a service does not establish what caused someone to adopt it. These distinctions appear in both the framework and the substantive comparisons.

The underdetermination is usually substantive. The revision names alternatives that predict the same evidence—script-sensitive printing costs, changing demand for road upkeep, calculation assistance in seaweed summaries, configuration differences in laser builds—and identifies observations that could separate them. It also retains positive evidence where justified, including scoped intervention effects and documented physical or institutional contributions. It has not merely turned every claim into an uninformative disclaimer.

The new material changes the argument. The supported-use HAPIN null prevents maintenance from explaining every absent health benefit. PV decompositions separate cost accounting from independently identified causes. Behavioural studies limit inference from an intervention label to a latent motive. Tae Rak and Martu material make water rights and customary obligations part of the operating arrangement. Tool accumulation and gendered maintenance entry prevent useful work from becoming an assumed accessible livelihood. These observations narrow earlier propositions rather than count as additional votes for one theory.

The remaining framework is a disciplined research approach. It still has enough adjustable categories to accommodate many outcomes retrospectively. The edition appropriately declines to estimate their historical prevalence, causal shares or forecasting power. Future work would need an advance prediction with independently measured inputs and a credible rival, not simply another case fitted to the same categories.

## C2-01 — Keep necessity, sufficiency and an unresolved rival separate

**P3; nonblocking; still present in the original reviewed snapshot.** [SYN-K21](full-snapshot/contributions/SYN/claims.json) line 886 and [its specification](full-snapshot/contributions/SYN/synthesis_specs.json) line 274 say neither contact nor increasing precision is universally sufficient or necessary. [The hypothesis register](full-snapshot/analysis/HYPOTHESES_AND_NEGATIVE_CASES.md) line 36 groups failed contacts and rough-component success as a sufficiency rejection; [the synthesis](full-snapshot/analysis/SYNTHESIS.md) line 57 also says greater precision did not solve every attempt.

The supported directions differ: failed visits defeat contact's sufficiency; successful rough electrodes defeat a requirement for the cited strict precision across those configurations. They do not establish contact's universal non-necessity or test precision's sufficiency. Possibility of written-only success is not an observed successful written-only copy. Preserve that distinction rather than upgrading a limit on inference into a categorical result.

[SKL-K17](full-snapshot/contributions/SKL/claims.json) line 454 is also `evaluated` although its named rival—different configurations permit different tolerances—predicts the observed contrast, and its proposed common-configuration test was not performed. The [methods contract](full-snapshot/METHODS_ADDENDUM_V2.md) requires `underdetermined` when both accounts fit. This label matters even though the surrounding caveats are good.

**Acceptance criteria:** state the two supported counterexamples separately; say the opposite directions are not established by these observations; replace the prose about precision solving attempts with the actual contrast; and mark the configuration-specific SKL-K17 comparison underdetermined, or explicitly separate a categorical refutation from the unresolved causal test. Preserve the failed visits, rough successes and restricted 1975 access. Align canonical claims/specifications, prose and compiled exports. No additional historical research is necessary for this correction.
