# State power, finance and practical use: four bounded histories

Research contribution POL, 5 November 2026. This investigation follows four systems: Tokugawa firearms, Yuan paper currency, late-Qing telegraphs and French metrication. The cases were selected to challenge simple accounts of state promotion or suppression, rather than to represent the frequency of either. They show why technical possession, legal authorization, reliable operation, changing use and welfare must be measured separately. A government can preserve a technology while restricting its purposes; deploy a system whose financing undermines service; or announce a standard before people possess suitable instruments. These are different outcomes, not stages on a single inevitable path.

The governing question is who makes which choice. Rulers select military and administrative priorities; investors negotiate control and returns; operators maintain systems; households adapt within different degrees of compulsion. Calling all of these actors “adopters” hides the mechanisms. The accompanying ledgers therefore specify populations, alternatives, measurements, labour evidence and rival explanations. Thirteen sources were inspected: nine with full text available, four through abstracts or abstracts plus notes. Full-text availability does not mean every page was read. Precise inspection scope and overlapping evidence lineages appear in `sources.json`.

**POL-C01 — Tokugawa firearms, 1603–1853: persistence within differentiated rules.**

The categorical story that Japan eliminated firearms after political unification does not survive the inspected evidence. Enomoto’s reassessment reports, through Fujiki’s historical work, 436 hunting firearms among 955 households in Shiiba mountain villages in 1745, and 586 firearms in 1836. The latter figure has no supplied same-year household denominator. Other examples concern different places and dates and must not be combined into a national curve. These are recorded stocks, not rates of shooting, military competence or individual ownership. Nevertheless, they are positive evidence against general disappearance. [POL-S01, pp. 50–53, especially p. 51 and note 66](https://www.isc.meiji.ac.jp/~transfer/papers/en/pdf/06/04_Enomoto.pdf) (POL-K01–K02).

The contemporary system distinguished possession, carriage, purpose and status. A prohibition on carrying a sword or on unauthorized discharge cannot automatically be read as confiscation of every firearm. Agricultural protection and hunting provided authorized uses for commoners, alongside restrictions. Authorities therefore had more than two available policies: distribute weapons indiscriminately or eliminate them. They could permit a tool for one task while denying another use or user. This distinction is central to the proposed accommodation mechanism, and it prevents military policy from absorbing the history of rural livelihoods. It does not make those arrangements egalitarian or consensual.

Noordam’s recent field review describes elite marksmanship and ceremonial hunting as further peacetime niches. Firearms could become signs of elite competence rather than inherently threatening a warrior identity. This weakens an explanation in which attachment to swords necessarily required rejection of guns. The review also links reduced battlefield pressures with less military innovation, but this contribution does not turn that interpretation into a measured national decline in technical capability. The introduction summarizes Galindo and Howell; those passages are not independent archival corroboration of them. [POL-S03, “Japan,” especially p. 9](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-asian-studies/article/introduction-the-reception-of-the-harquebus-in-east-and-southeast-asia/AD08FBBF2D32EDBC28B3AE2D7200CBCF) (POL-K03).

A useful negative test distinguishes legitimate accommodation from mere inability to enforce a universal ban. Documented authorization and elite use favour the former over a story requiring only hidden weapons and weak policing. They do not establish how much persistence each mechanism explains. A stronger test would compare domains with similar crop and hunting needs but different licensing and enforcement, using inventories, petitions and sanctions. That comparison has not been performed here. Neither the low observed use of guns in a particular kind of protest nor the survival of guns establishes the amount of interpersonal violence; those require their own denominators.

Howell’s publisher abstract describes a change in the Edo hinterland from farm-tool meanings toward weapons from the 1840s, associated with anxiety about disorder. Because the detailed article was unavailable, this remains an attributed hypothesis. The evidence needed to distinguish actual behaviour from administrative vocabulary would include contemporary petitions, use reports and enforcement records. It should not be extended backward across the entire Tokugawa period or used as a sufficient explanation of regime collapse. [POL-S02, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10371390902780530?journalCode=cjst20) (POL-K04).

For labour, the case shows continuing hunting, crop protection and martial practice, not a clean occupational replacement. It cannot establish gunsmith earnings, household time savings or workers’ bargaining gains. Swords and bows remained alternatives for some purposes, but are not assumed to be interchangeable substitutes for every agricultural task. A useful technology could remain bounded by permissions and changing demand without disappearing. That is the result supported here; a national story of either technical stagnation or unrestricted abundance is not.

**POL-C02 — Yuan paper currency, 1260–1368: a payment system with changing commitments.**

Paper currency was already known before the Mongol rulers’ programme. The adoption problem was therefore not simply inventing a light physical object. It involved tax and salary uses, authentication, exchange institutions, political jurisdiction and the treatment of existing monies. Guan, Palma and Wu distinguish an initially silver-convertible regime, a period of nominal convertibility after the 1270s and a fiat regime from 1310. Actual redemption depended on local availability, not merely a stated promise. Their account also records private silver transactions despite official restrictions. Legal monopoly should therefore be treated as a rule whose implementation requires investigation. [POL-S04, accepted manuscript pp. 10–18](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Rise%20and%20Demise%20of%20Paper%20Money%20in%20Yuan%20China.pdf) (POL-K05, K08).

Polo’s narrative is unusually useful for separating different reasons for accepting notes. It describes official signatures and seals, replacement of damaged notes for a fee, and convenience in carrying money. It also reports threats for refusing the currency. His simultaneous claims of ready acceptance and coercion do not cancel each other: they concern different possible incentives. They also do not measure how often anyone refused or was punished. His expansive account of imperial wealth is rhetoric, not a balance sheet. The source establishes what an influential observer reported, not universal preference throughout an empire. [POL-S06, Book II, chapter XXIV, pp. 1–2](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/figures/ser_xxiv.pdf) (POL-K09).

Regional evidence further limits a unitary monetary story. Ichimaru’s English abstract reports different roles for *guanwen* and *dingliang* in Dadu, northwestern areas and Jiangnan, and distinguishes governmental finance from commerce. These are units of account, which must not automatically be equated with different physical currencies. The underlying Heicheng documents and Huizhou contracts offer a different evidentiary route from aggregate issue totals, but only the abstract was inspected. The conclusion warranted here is that uniform official money did not imply one homogeneous accounting practice. [POL-S05, English abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/68/3/68_KJ00004582583/_article/-char/en) (POL-K08).

The quantitative reconstruction also contradicts a century of uninterrupted hyperinflation. Its respectability price index gives annual rates of 11.0%, 1.8% and 4.5% for 1260–1290, 1290–1340 and 1340–1355 respectively. Those are modelled historical estimates: roughly a dozen observations for each commodity support interpolation and extrapolation, and the reconstructed monetary stock uses assumed attrition. The price window ends before the dynasty. The long relatively moderate middle interval matters, but exact annual values are less secure than a modern continuously measured series. [POL-S04, pp. 26–28, Figure 6](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Rise%20and%20Demise%20of%20Paper%20Money%20in%20Yuan%20China.pdf) (POL-K06).

War-related fiscal pressure is a plausible mechanism with a real rival test, but it is not causally settled. In the study’s regressions, civil war correlates with nominal and real note issues; imperial grants do not show comparable associations. External wars differ, and natural disasters have weak associations with real issuance in some specifications. “War caused inflation” would consequently lose essential distinctions. Fiscal erosion, monetary instability and rebellion may also reinforce one another, while surviving records and reconstructed controls constrain inference. [POL-S04, pp. 43–48, Table 4](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Rise%20and%20Demise%20of%20Paper%20Money%20in%20Yuan%20China.pdf) (POL-K07).

This case supports investigating a system’s fiscal commitments rather than judging paper by its cheap material alone. It does not establish that fiat currency inevitably fails, that Mongol rule lacked every relevant capacity, or that later European institutions provide an appropriate matched counterfactual. Administrative workers authenticated and replaced notes; merchants and officials performed accounting and exchange tasks. Their occupational trajectories and incomes remain unknown. Taxpayers, salary recipients, creditors and debtors could experience monetary changes differently, and the aggregate evidence cannot allocate gains or losses among them. Legal acceptance, commercial usefulness and distributional justice remain separate questions.

**POL-C03 — Late-Qing telegraphs, 1881–1911: strategic provision with uneven performance.**

The episode concerns a landline service and its institutions, not an isolated electrical apparatus. Offices, operators, coding practices, maintenance, capital and tariffs made messages usable. Gao and Lei describe strategic state priorities behind network expansion and state control over privately operated lines. Their quantitative sample records office access in 64 of 130 rice-reporting prefectures by 1904. That is a transparent deployment measure; it says nothing directly about household usage. The institutional discussion extends to the late Qing, while the economic panel runs from 1870 to 1904. These observation windows must remain distinct. [POL-S07, pp. 7–13](https://assets.aeaweb.org/asset-server/files/13141.pdf) (POL-K10).

A centralized label also obscures disagreements inside provision. Zhou and Ding’s Chinese and English abstracts portray Sheng Xuanhuai as sometimes pressuring shareholders from the court’s side, but also defending investor interests against official encroachment, with his own interests entangled. They identify a proposal to replace appropriation with government purchase. That is evidence of contested property and bargaining among powerful actors, not proof of completed reform or public consultation. Their archive-based study is a valuable lead; the detailed correspondence was not inspected here. [POL-S09, abstracts and bibliography](https://qsyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/Y2019/V0/I3/110) (POL-K12).

Yoon offers a sharper critique of service: dividend priorities, restrained equipment investment, high charges and unresolved problems of speed, reliability and security limited popular uptake. Because only the abstract and references were accessible, the financing mechanism is retained as a hypothesis. High capital costs and uncertain demand could also produce high charges and low investment. Distinguishing the accounts requires budgets and investment decisions, tariff changes, equipment performance and customer composition. A profitable enterprise is not automatically an accessible communications system, but an observed high price alone cannot establish exploitative managerial intent. [POL-S08, abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/abs/dashed-expectations-limitations-of-the-telegraphic-service-in-the-late-qing/62CACD9A5094EBEAFD5E0E760EB55C69) (POL-K11).

The negative account does not eliminate evidence of useful commercial effects. Gao and Lei estimate lower monthly maximum high-quality rice prices after office connection, about 4.2–5.5% of the sample mean, in a difference-in-differences design covering 130 prefectures in nine provinces over 420 months. Their data derive from official price reports, with route and opening information from maps, histories and gazetteers. Treatment means access to an office, not successful delivery of every telegram. [POL-S07, pp. 10–17, Table 3](https://assets.aeaweb.org/asset-server/files/13141.pdf) (POL-K13).

The design examines pretrends, transportation and treaty-port controls, selected-location exclusions and network/weather interactions. This makes it more discriminating than simple before-and-after chronology. It does not make placement random. Military priorities can coincide with other changes affecting markets, and the author-imposed June opening month reflects missing dates. The result is conditional on timing, reporting and model assumptions; it has not been replicated here. A future audit should also examine staggered-adoption estimators and whether improved communications changed the quality of price reporting itself.

The study’s most useful qualification is directional: access reduced sensitivity to local weather shocks but increased responsiveness to shocks in other connected regions. Previously volatile markets became more stable, while previously stable markets became more volatile. These results share one dataset and are not independent replications. They suggest redistributed exposure through trade, not uniform stabilization. Direct household consumption, income and mortality were not measured. A net buyer and net seller could evaluate the same price change differently. [POL-S07, sections IV–V](https://assets.aeaweb.org/asset-server/files/13141.pdf) (POL-K14).

Private letters and messengers remained contemporary alternatives; existing waterway and merchant networks also carried goods after information could move faster. The relevant complement was not simply more wiring. Operators, coding staff, builders and repairers acquired or reorganized tasks, but there is no inspected worker cohort showing whether courier livelihoods recovered or deteriorated. The apparent contradiction between limited popular access and detectable market effects therefore dissolves at the level of measurement: a relatively bounded group of users can influence prices faced by many nonusers. It does not follow that those nonusers authorized the system or benefited equally.

**POL-C04 — French metrication, 1795–1840: legal unity and practical conversion.**

The adoption task was to make quantities comparable in commerce and administration. That required usable instruments and methods as well as a scientific definition. Treat’s official historical study reports that secondary standards had not been prepared for departmental distribution when mandatory use was declared in 1795. The Isère archive synthesis likewise places municipal standards and conversion tables within implementation. This supports a material-complement hypothesis: a command cannot by itself supply calibrated equipment. It does not identify the share of delay caused by manufacture rather than habit, pricing interests or political disruption. [POL-S13, p. 10](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nbsspecialpublication345-10.pdf), [POL-S12, dossier, PDF p. 6](https://archives.isere.fr/sites/isere-archives-fr/files/inline-files/ca_13_poids_mesures_protege.pdf) (POL-K16).

The 1812 decree provides a direct test of the familiar claim that Napoleon simply abolished metric measurement. Article 1 explicitly retained the existing base units. Subsequent provisions ordered commercial instruments using familiar fractions or multiples, marked with comparisons between new and older divisions and names, while retaining official metric teaching. The compromise changed practical arrangements substantially without discarding the base standard. It was a distinct configuration, not merely incomplete execution of the previous one. The decree shows what authorities authorized, not how many households possessed compliant instruments. [POL-S10, printed pp. 453–454](https://annales.ensmp.fr/articles/1812-1/235-245.pdf) (POL-K15).

Débarbat and Quinn describe the subsequent retail arrangements and the 1837 law, which repealed the 1812 decree and set 1 January 1840 as the effective prohibition on other measures. Treat reproduces further restrictions on commercial instruments and legal documentation. These accounts rely on the same legislation; their agreement is not two independent observations of compliance. The deadline marks an institutional change, not a national household conversion date. The original 1837 bulletin was not inspected; its relevant articles were read in scholarly quotation and official historical translation. [POL-S11, pp. 13–14](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/10.1016/j.crhy.2018.12.002.pdf), [POL-S13, pp. 10–11](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nbsspecialpublication345-10.pdf) (POL-K17).

Customary measures were not necessarily arbitrary from users’ perspective. The Isère dossier explains agricultural area measures in relation to work and terrain, and describes later persistence of rural terms and task quantities. Its long-run coda is used only to challenge instant completion in 1840, not to reconstruct national nineteenth-century usage. Continued words do not always mean unchanged magnitudes. Nor does task usefulness exclude limited schooling, political resistance or opportunities to manipulate prices. Testing practical fit requires evidence of what users could calculate and how they measured particular transactions, rather than inferring beliefs from noncompliance. [POL-S12, PDF pp. 2 and 6–9](https://archives.isere.fr/sites/isere-archives-fr/files/inline-files/ca_13_poids_mesures_protege.pdf) (POL-K18).

Scientists and officials chose definitions and rules; instrument makers, inspectors, teachers, clerks and traders carried conversion into practice. The sources establish tasks and institutions more securely than livelihoods. They do not quantify replacement costs for poor households, gains to makers, or workers’ bargaining power. Administrative uniformity could improve comparability while imposing uneven learning and replacement burdens. The relevant alternative was often a conversion table or mixed measure, not a refusal to measure anything. Treating coexistence as mere backwardness would erase the very adaptations requiring explanation.

**Comparative interpretation and remaining tests.**

POL-X01 compares Japan and France to isolate a logical error: the wording of a regulation is not a measure of use. The cases do not identify a general effect of regulation, because their tasks, enforcement systems and evidence differ. POL-X02 compares Yuan finance and Qing communications to ask how strategic demand and financial commitments interact. It establishes no institutional transmission across centuries and no timeless Chinese capacity trait. POL-X03 preserves the telegraph’s internal negative outcome: connection could increase risk in some markets. POL-X04 examines coercion alongside convenience without treating money and physical measurement as interchangeable systems.

Three research priorities follow. First, inspect implementation where formal rules meet an operator: licensing records, exchange bureaux, maintenance budgets and local inspectors. Second, distinguish the chooser’s payoff from effects on nonchoosers; military command, investor returns and legal uniformity cannot stand in for welfare. Third, recover transitions at the level of people and tasks. These sources rarely follow individual workers or households through a change, so the labour objects deliberately preserve unknowns. Filling them with plausible stories about new jobs would weaken the investigation.

The graph accordingly encodes scoped contributions, competition and constraints, with hypothetical links labelled. It makes no universal prerequisite or transitive diffusion claim. The main unresolved limitations are inherited Japanese archival evidence, four abstract-level sources, sparse reconstructed Yuan prices, unreplicated Qing econometrics and local rather than representative French practice evidence. Those limits narrow the conclusions; they do not erase the positive counterexamples to abandonment, instantaneous adoption or uniform benefit.
