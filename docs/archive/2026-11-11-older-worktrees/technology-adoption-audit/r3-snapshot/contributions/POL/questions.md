# POL unresolved questions and source-access priorities

These are follow-up questions, not requests for approval or claims that the current cases are a representative sample. No forecast follows from the case counts.

1. **Japanese local rules and actual use (POL-C01; POL-K01–K04).** Read the original Shiiba and Matsumoto records cited through Fujiki, then compare domains with similar crop/hunting demand but different permission regimes. A record of authorized guns discriminates against total eradication; it does not quantify national prevalence or whether regulations caused low violence. Obtain same-year household denominators and distinguish stocks, operators, firing and repairs. Inspect Howell's full article and Tsukamoto's underlying records before elevating the 1840s disorder mechanism. Determine whether petitions and use records establish changed behaviour rather than administrative terminology. Continue to separate peacetime utility from battlefield effectiveness.

2. **Yuan convertibility, prices and fiscal sequencing (POL-C02; POL-K05–K09).** Check Yuanshi and Yuandianzhang entries and Li's raw commodity observations, including regional prices, market versus official rates, note replacement and retirements. The accepted manuscript's sparse-price interpolation, assumed 10% annual stock attrition and official conversion ratios are consequential modelling choices. Recompute sensitivity to basket weights, region, interpolation and note survival. Inspect fiscal shortfalls around civil-war events; inflation and unrest may cause one another. A null grant coefficient does not prove grants immaterial; disasters show weak real-issuance associations. The accepted manuscript's sentence on late-period doubling appears to contain a date typo; no claim uses it. Its boundaries between monetary regimes vary by one year in places. The 1350/1352 reform chronology needs an announcement/issuance distinction before adding exact dates. Do not infer every late payment became barter.

3. **Regional monetary practice.** Read Ichimaru's full Japanese article and its Heicheng/Huizhou contracts. Determine sample size, dates and whether units describe accounts, settlement media or both. Test the limits of note acceptance outside major exchange cities, including the reported Yunnan cowry exception. Polo's chapter is direct historical testimony in translation, but its sanctions and universal-acceptance statements require administrative and transaction evidence. No frequency of enforcement or popular consent is currently measured.

4. **Telegraph finance versus scarce capital (POL-C03; POL-K10–K12).** Obtain Yoon's full article/thesis and Zhou/Ding's full Chinese article plus the Sheng correspondence they cite. Compare dividends, retained earnings, equipment purchases, tariff changes, costs of repair, message delays and customer composition. Were high tariffs a managerial choice against viable reinvestment, or a consequence of risk/cost and low demand? Investor negotiation and a proposed state buyout do not establish popular participation or actual reform. Faster official command could benefit rulers even where users considered the service poor.

5. **Telegraph identification and welfare (POL-K13–K14).** Reproduce Gao–Lei results from the replication package before using their magnitudes in quantitative synthesis. Examine heterogeneous staggered-treatment estimators, regional war exposure, spillovers, office downtime and measurement errors from unknown opening months. Could telegraphs alter official price reporting, creating apparent changes? The accepted manuscript has inconsistent prose saying 40% despite explicit 147/299 and 64/130 counts; use counts. A weather footnote appears to assign identical numerical categories to flood and drought; check final publication/data code. Explore low-quality grain and regions outside the nine-province panel. Household net-buyer/seller status, consumption, wages and mortality would be required to assess welfare; lower reported wholesale maxima alone are insufficient.

6. **Metric supply, local transactions and compliance (POL-C04; POL-K15–K18).** Inspect Isère delivery ledgers, verification reports, prosecutions and household/shop records, then add regions with faster and slower implementation. Establish whether instrument availability precedes compliance rather than merely tracks it. Compare users able to employ metric units who still choose task measures with those lacking education or instruments. Distinguish words, calibrated magnitudes and transaction conventions. Read the original 1837 Bulletin des lois rather than relying on later quotations for any expanded legal claim. Débarbat/Quinn's narrative has an apparently incorrect toise equivalence; the report omits it. Do not use institutional descriptions of popular 'habit' as direct psychological evidence.

7. **Labour and distribution across all cases.** Current sources identify operators and tasks much better than wages, bargaining or career transitions. Missing evidence includes women’s conversion/accounting work, informal repair, training costs, those unable to pay for official service, debtors/creditors, and people coerced through state command. Find linked worker/household records before estimating job creation, displacement, transition duration or distributional benefit.

## Comparisons needing extension

POL-X01 tests only categorical law/use claims, not the size of a regulation effect. POL-X02 has unmatched centuries and institutions; 'China' is not a common controlled treatment. POL-X03 is one study's internal contrast. POL-X04 compares coercion/usefulness analytically but does not equate currency depreciation with measurement error. Omitted cases include other Asian military systems, African fiscal/communication systems, voluntary standards, rejected paper-money transfers and rapid metric adopters. They should be added for discriminating evidence, not to manufacture a geographic quota.
