# OCE questions, limits and next evidence

This contribution uses five purposively selected cases and fourteen inspected sources. It is not a census of Oceania, a summary of all Indigenous technologies, or evidence that the selected mechanisms predominate globally. Ten sources have full documents/pages locally acquired and relevant sections inspected; four have article/PDF text inspected through web extraction or an HTML mirror and are conservatively marked `partial-text` in the source ledger. Searches and rejected leads are not promoted into claim support.

## Consequential unresolved questions

1. **Hōkūleʻa: transmission versus full-system support.** Obtain the original 1980 course/fix logs, voyage protocols, training diaries and records of escort communication. Pre-specify what would distinguish non-instrument navigation from aid received during navigation, without mistaking documentation of position for guidance to the navigator. Compare multiple trained navigators and unsuccessful attempts. The present participant account establishes operation and a teaching sequence, not isolated causal shares or ancient-route history.
2. **Hōkūleʻa: entry and unpaid labor.** Read Wilson's full 2010 thesis and additional first-person accounts, including nonparticipants and people who stopped volunteering. Determine crew selection rules, care obligations, expenses and access to mentoring. Do not assume either universal exclusion or equal access from the visible roster. Existing accounts of cultural purpose must not be replaced with motives inferred from success.
3. **Tae Rak: how much functionality returned?** Acquire the 2006 hydrological feasibility and Environmental FLOWS reports, 2008 conservation plan, gate operations, matched rain/inflow/retention records and records of actual trap use. The negotiated 52.4 m AHD design and Muldoons exception are materially important. Distinguish possible operation, operated traps, catches and household/commercial benefits.
4. **Tae Rak: ownership, opposition and work outcomes.** Inspect original drainage opposition letters and authorization documents, and longitudinal construction-training/worker records if publicly available and appropriate. Current sources support opposed drainage and a training programme, not a quantified distribution of benefits or a complete legal history. Do not infer ancient labor rights from modern cooperative reconstruction.
5. **Martu: ecological and social rivals.** Compare matched vegetation/topography and rainfall cells; audit ignition classifications, residual mixed/unclassified areas, spatial autocorrelation and track-biased ground truthing. Direct fire observations and fire spread/fuel measurements would distinguish the mosaic mechanism from site-selection confounding. Separate measured fire patterns from proposed effects on mammals.
6. **Martu: governance and opportunity costs.** The 2012 study describes rights, retribution and recognition for caring for Country. More direct and plural accounts would clarify variation in permission, transmission and household effects. Changes in transport access or task returns might discriminate short-term food incentives from customary obligations, but should not be designed or interpreted as if the latter were absent. No inference about contemporary ranger jobs or carbon-credit programmes is warranted from these studies.
7. **Kuk: why the local interruption?** Seek finer chronological alignment among water-table change, ditch repair/cessation, dryland cropping and settlement/displacement. The candidate predictions differ: hydrological failure should track changed wetness, agronomic substitution should track viable alternatives, and exclusion should have independent evidence of disrupted occupation or access. The present record does not select among them; more repeated publications of Kuk plans would not add independent identification.
8. **Kuk: distribution and authority.** Explore excavator records on work estimates and settlement relationships without reading modern chiefly/state or egalitarian models into ditch geometry. Ancient users, gender division, coercion, ownership and returns remain unknown. More chronology alone will not fill those fields.
9. **Kūmara trials: which reconstruction is being tested?** Seek replicated soil/mulch and cultivar comparisons, realistic annual planting-stock survival, traditional-storage experiments and explicit treatment of pathogens, introduced weeds, climate and modern fencing. Modern treated stock may change yields; storage in contemporary conditions cannot estimate historical pit losses. Crop identity and collection lineage need to remain explicit.
10. **Māori archaeological sites: sample and purpose boundaries.** At M24/11, inspect the supplementary starch atlas, contamination controls and full Bayesian model. At Pōhatu, expand soil profiles and amendment candidates while retaining Mana Whenua authority over questions and knowledge disclosure. A weak match to marine inputs is informative but does not uniquely prove kānuka. No site sample establishes universal loss of a crop across southern Aotearoa.
11. **Pōhatu: recovery versus completed adoption.** Distinguish the documented ongoing garden collaboration at Koukourārata, the 2022–2023 Pōhatu soil investigation, and aspirations for Pōhatu cultivation. Follow actual land access, planting, maintenance and harvests before describing restoration as completed or economically viable. Do not imply that research itself restituted land.

## Geographic and selection limits

The contribution gives substantial attention to two Australian systems and three Pacific configurations, but Micronesia appears mainly through Satawal's active role in transmission; Melanesia mainly through Kuk. Torres Strait, Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, New Caledonia, Fiji, inland and coastal New Guinea diversity, and most Pacific colonial/commercial transformations remain insufficiently covered. Technologies of housebuilding, clothing, preservation, exchange, fishing, medicine, measurement and transport beyond voyaging would change the choice set. Comparison with Arctic, North American, Amazonian or African Indigenous systems is not attempted from superficial similarity.

The cases overrepresent publicly documented restoration and visible landscape interventions. Communities refusing outside access, knowledge considered inappropriate for publication, practices abandoned after careful local assessment, and low-visibility maintenance are poorly represented. A universal celebration of persistence would reproduce selection bias just as readily as a deficit narrative.

## Leads inspected only as leads, or deliberately not used

- Finney's 1977 *Voyaging Canoes and the Settlement of Polynesia* was located at abstract level. Its sailing-performance result and sweeping counterfactual about Polynesian settlement are not used as detailed evidence. The original full paper and later voyage logs would be useful.
- Lauriston Sharp's *Steel Axes for Stone-Age Australians* was considered as a tool-exchange case, but a single classic observer narrative without an adequately inspected independent critique or descendant account would risk reproducing a deterministic colonial account. It was not incorporated merely to satisfy a topic quota.
- The full 2010 thesis underlying Wilson-Hokowhitu's 2012 paper was located but not read. Signed/access-bearing repository links were not saved as citation URLs. The cited 2012 article is itself inspected original interview-based scholarship.
- Older colonial accounts cited by the Budj Bim dossier, Māori research papers, and Kuk comparisons remain mediated evidence unless separately inspected. No colonial writer's universal claims about absence of gardening, techniques or knowledge are adopted.
- The 2017 Kuk chapter's independent-innovation interpretation is stronger than this contribution needs. Sparse comparative archaeology cannot by itself exclude earlier external interactions; no universal independence claim is entered in the ledger.

## Source handling corrections retained for audit

- The parameterized NZ Archaeological Association URL for the kūmara experiment returned an unrelated Hane fishing paper through the web tool. Title, authors and page inspection exposed the mismatch. That response is retained under `provenance/REJECTED_S11_wrong_redirect.txt` and `web_inspection_S11.json`; it supports no claim. The correct article was inspected through an HTML mirror, verified by title, authors, journal pagination and 2003 publication footer. The journal volume carries 2001, which is not the publication year.
- PVS's general history gives 18 March 2007 for Pwo while the retrospective eyewitness post says 17 March. The narrative and records use **March 2007**. This discrepancy does not undermine the specific participant list, but is a reason not to imply the two pages are independent corroboration.
- OCE-S14's filename ends `137-143`, but the inspected PDF contains pages 137–147. Citations use printed page numbers.
- Raw acquired documents are for local inspection. Only the report, structured records, questions and audit results are intended as this contribution's synthesis outputs; no publication or correspondence was performed.
