# AGR: unresolved questions and disconfirmation work

The eight cases are selected contrasts, not a representative sample. No historical success rate or mechanism frequency should be calculated from them.

## Highest-value checks

1. **Titicaca project survival denominator.** Bandy's 2005 argument contains both reports of abandoned observed fields and reconstruction totals reaching 2000. Obtain Swartley's 2000 dissertation/2002 book and the Fernández Valdivia–García Chire 2000 five-season comparison. Separate observed communities, years and organisations; do not turn a local revisit into basin-wide extinction. Reassess the assumed maintenance labour and compare complete crop rotations with equivalent seed quality.
2. **Ancient raised-field chronology and political organisation.** Erickson's northern-basin chronology and Bandy's political-economic hypotheses cannot stand for all basins or periods. Seek excavation dates, palaeoenvironmental series and settlement evidence to distinguish climatic, demographic, institutional and labour-calendar explanations of abandonment. Proving irrigation existed is not proving centralised command.
3. **Bali's historical intervention evidence.** Obtain the project memoranda and independent field records behind the 1970s–80s narrative. The present account relies on Lansing-linked historical reporting, while Janssen supplies genuinely independent model reanalysis. Test whether alternative pest dynamics, water allocation rules, enforcement costs and distributional objectives alter the reported coordination advantage.
4. **Gambia's apparent disagreement.** Reconcile the same households and seasons in Carney's field research and IFPRI's production–income analysis. Do not interpret different study years as a controlled time series. Which rights and compensation changes preceded labour withdrawal? How much of later decline came from repair and diesel supply, prices and marketing changes, soil conditions, or labour reallocations that improved total household returns?
5. **Green Revolution identification.** Audit crop-specific coincident shocks around release dates and modern staggered-adoption estimators. Test how results change with excluded countries, crop groups, alternative innovations and spillovers. The 44% estimate is a model-based direct yield contribution holding allocations fixed; it is not the share of people made better off or a treatment effect for every farmer.
6. **Groundwater attribution.** The Rodell study measures regional 2002–08 depletion and investigates storage alternatives. It does not separately identify seed, electricity-price, pumping-access and crop-price effects. Seek regional water-accounting and policy designs that can separate these mechanisms, avoiding a claim that one seed technology alone caused depletion.
7. **Ghana tenure.** Verify exact field dates against the original Akwapim survey documentation before normalising the case to a precise annual timeline. The inspected article explains a two-year survey and uses 1997 monetary comparisons but the relevant survey-description passage does not state calendar dates. The present case is deliberately dated late twentieth century. Test plot-specific land quality, inherited status and non-tenure routes from political power to output.
8. **Ghana insurance welfare and durability.** The original paper flags unresolved profit effects. Examine renewal at full delivery cost, consumption risk and basis risk, including nonfarm responses. Independently vary payment credibility, index accuracy and premium timing to distinguish competing demand mechanisms. Do not infer a viable stand-alone insurance market solely from subsidised experimental demand.
9. **Cooking: product, complements and outcomes.** An equipment-only comparison is inadequate. Separate supply reliability, repair access, fuel costs, meal types, pot compatibility, ventilation, exposure and timing. HAPIN's high use under free fuel does not establish commercial uptake, and its birth-weight result is not an all-outcome health assessment. Later HAPIN outcome papers were discovered but not evaluated here; they must not be represented as covered.
10. **Household work denominator.** Reconstruct comparable time-use measures with explicit treatment of simultaneous childcare, purchased help, household composition and service quality. Use exogenous appliance or utility access to test causal time-saving, and examine domestic workers and poorer households excluded from Cowan's principal documentary population.

## Access and edition boundaries

- Hanna–Duflo–Greenstone: full 2012 working paper inspected; 2016 journal abstract inspected separately. Harvard and J-PAL final-PDF links returned access errors; MIT's public download returned a rate limit, and AEA displayed a subscription access page. No journal table is cited as inspected. The two versions have separate source IDs.
- Ramey: the 2008 NBER working-paper full text was acquired and inspected. The 2009 journal abstract agrees on the broad trends but its full text was not acquired. Table locators and exact values in this contribution refer to the 2008 version.
- Janssen: the accessible full text is a 2006 uncorrected proof of the 2007 article. Locators specify proof page numbers. Final pagination has not been substituted for them.
- Cowan and Bandy: complete article text was available in web transcriptions; layout/column extraction is imperfect and figures were not independently checked. Numerical statements used here are stated in prose, and the authors' more expansive causal interpretations are qualified.
- IFPRI: complete scanned report acquired; relevant summary, conceptual-framework, conclusion and survey-design pages inspected with OCR. This is not a claim of cover-to-cover reading or independent replication of its regressions.

## Geographic and thematic gaps

Ancient/preindustrial coverage rests heavily on Andean archaeology and a Balinese system studied in modern times. Prioritise African indigenous water and soil management, Chinese wet-rice and household histories, Mesoamerican food processing, Islamic irrigation, Pacific agroforestry and colonial crop transfers. Add cold-chain systems with matched energy and repair failures, farm mechanisation via service rental rather than ownership, and cases where non-adoption preserves ecological or culinary qualities. Sources in relevant local languages and local scholarship should challenge this edition's English-language selection.

Do not assume a common preference hierarchy: taste, bodily effort, calendar flexibility, social standing, religious obligations and control of output may carry different weights. The present studies identify some constraints but rarely directly estimate those weights. Further work should collect expressed reasons alongside observed choices, with feasible alternatives and coercive constraints documented.
