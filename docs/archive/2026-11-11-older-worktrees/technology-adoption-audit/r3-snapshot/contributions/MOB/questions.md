# Open questions, rival explanations and evidence limits

This register accompanies the v2 case, claim, source, graph and comparison files. Unknowns are deliberately retained. All source claims refer to inspected texts, not to uninspected works cited inside them. The research is bounded to historical outcomes; no future technology forecast is offered.

## Consequential unresolved questions

1. **Who could refuse infrastructure or its costs?** The corpus has much stronger evidence on government and company choices than on land users’ consent, taxpayers’ options, labour contracts or everyday evasion. Military use and conquest establish power asymmetries; they do not by themselves prove every local transaction was coerced. Conversely, voluntary crop shipment does not establish consent to colonization, land acquisition or taxes. The next useful sources are actual concession and acquisition files, petitions, labour contracts, court cases and oral histories, with named populations and dates.

2. **What happened to individual workers?** Company employment, dock-task relocation and official claims of upgrading are not career histories. The present cases do not identify whether displaced railway workers, head-loaders or dock workers obtained better jobs, how long transitions took, or what happened to unpaid household work. A follow-up should seek cohort records or linked employment/pension registers; access rules and occupational exclusion must be documented separately from technical training needs.

3. **What is the correct social accounting boundary?** Transport benefits should not be summed while omitting tax guarantees, construction deaths, maintenance, land opportunity costs, forced labour or pollution. No full common welfare metric is available here. Ghana’s forest conversion is described, not quantified; Singapore’s official land rationale lacks the other land users’ accounts; local air pollution and safety costs are not measured in the early vehicle and container cases. These are evidence gaps, not inferred zeroes.

4. **Can the mechanisms be distinguished from plausible alternatives?** `claims.json` records actual findings as well as proposed falsifiers. A shared prediction is marked underdetermined. The most discriminating inspected evidence is Donaldson’s planned-line/placebo analysis; the Ghana study’s spatial and planned-route comparisons; task-specific electric survival after a supposed universal failure point; and the container paper’s own failed pre-trend tests for some large estimates. None makes the entire strand an identified causal design.

## Case-specific next tests

- **MOB-C01, Roman freight:** Replicate the pottery-cost inference with a second region, other commodities and alternative demand/network assumptions. Distinguish military provisioning from market distribution. Actual handling charges or shipment itineraries could test whether a segment-price model misranks complete journeys. Do not treat ORBIS’s modeled routes as observed shares or its mixed-date network as a diffusion sequence. Archaeological road survival alone does not identify historical maintenance effort or voluntary uptake.

- **MOB-C02, Inka/Atacama:** Test informal imperial supervision against the autonomous-exchange interpretation. Seek independent storage, tribute or permission evidence, finer dating, and comparisons with clearly state-operated mines. For the pedestrian-versus-llama hypothesis, inspect corrals, dung and feed logistics before treating scarce camelid remains as negative evidence; consumption bones are not a traffic census. A road’s shorter distance does not remove the water budget. Do not generalize one 12 km segment to all Andean transport.

- **MOB-C03, India:** Audit the 192-district outcome construction and routes used in the placebo tests; inspect who was excluded by district aggregation. Compare landless labourers, cultivators and landlords rather than treating agricultural output per area as a person-level gain. For state takeovers, separate headcount, wage rates, service intensity and quality. Investor returns at market prices test one extraction mechanism; complement them with construction procurement, salaries/remittances, original subscriptions, guarantee outlays and foregone public spending. The 2011 cost manuscript and 2019 accepted-return manuscript are explicit editions, not silently interchangeable with their published versions.

- **MOB-C04, Ghana:** Inspect line-level repair, traffic and finance records around the post-1974 decline to distinguish road competition, managerial deterioration, macroeconomic shocks and changing cocoa geography. Seek evidence about cocoa tenure, migrant labour and forest users. Persistence with roads replacing rail is not persistence after transport access vanishes. Compare abandoned rail places lacking replacement access; the present sample may privilege places that survived. The 2015 author version is used, and the relevant Ghana endpoint is 2000, not the present.

- **MOB-C05, electric vehicles:** Obtain original EVC operating accounts and compare early firms with similar batteries but different financing and maintenance organization. Later fleets are negative cases against universal technical impossibility, but they are not matched controls for the 1897 business. Test the early-utility-coordination counterfactual with actual utility deliberations and investment thresholds; it remains unproven. Claims about gender, adventure or status need independently observed beliefs and behaviour, not post hoc inference from gasoline dominance. Trace worker outcomes rather than assuming maintenance specialization created universally accessible opportunities.

- **MOB-C06, containers:** Reproduce the published 2016 estimates, including pre-trends and product-classification sensitivity, before using a headline number. The author's 2012–2014 working papers are not additional corroboration. The causal interpretation of adoption-only estimates fails important checks in the published article. Further work should separate privately financed capacity, subsidy/standard rules and military demand using route/company records. Examine nonunion and inland labour, ports that lost traffic, container leasing and empty repositioning. The 1960 M&M agreement concerned mechanization broadly, and two court cases are not two independent labour-impact studies.

- **MOB-C07, Singapore:** Find contemporary feasibility/appraisal records, PSA annual reports and alternative-site comparisons, plus worker and waterfront-community accounts. Official sources encountered during discovery differ on pre-1972 decision dates (1966, 1967, 1969); this contribution deliberately asserts only the well-supported commissioning year from the inspected speech. Resolve that chronology before claiming a precise decision-to-deployment lag. A successful port and a well-articulated government rationale do not establish consent or complete compensation.

## Important leads not used as inspected-original evidence

- **Kirsch and Mom, “Visions of Transportation: The EVC and the Transition from Service- to Product-Based Mobility,” Business History Review 76(1), 75–110 (2002), DOI 10.2307/4127752.** Publisher landing page and references were available, but the full article was not obtained. The substantive case uses the full Kirsch 1997 dissertation summary and Mom 1997 article instead. [Publisher locator](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-history-review/article/abs/visions-of-transportation-the-evc-and-the-transition-from-service-to-productbased-mobility/1B8B1A0EAD59189B24BDE9D9A538C5A0)

- **John Hyslop, The Inka Road System (1984), and early chroniclers discussed by Garrido.** Their views are reported as part of Garrido’s critique, not represented as independently inspected sources. A critical reading of chronicle genre, retrospective imperial narratives and archaeological visibility is necessary before treating formal rules as actual universal practice.

- **Levinson, The Box; contemporary McKinsey port studies; Containerisation International.** These are important historical/data inputs to MOB-S11, not independently read originals here. Original US Maritime Board/ISO committee proceedings and procurement records would strengthen claims about why standards and state demand changed investment.

- **Original Indian railway concession and administration reports, Dalhousie’s 1853 minute, and Investors Monthly Manual pages.** The inspected scholarship supplies precise archival/data routes; this contribution does not claim a complete primary-record re-audit. The exact input and cleaning versions would be required for numerical replication.

- **Ghanaian railway records and the works of Luntinen, Hill, Ruf and Austin used by Jedwab/Moradi.** No independent verification of every colonial labour, forestry or financing statement was performed. Original sources are especially important for distributional outcomes outside the econometric outcome variables.

- **Smithsonian “Transforming the Waterfront.”** Search text was available but direct full-page access repeatedly failed. It is not included in the evidence ledger or used to establish an Oakland–San Francisco contrast. [Institutional locator](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/america-on-the-move/online/transforming-waterfront)

## Selection and geographic limits

The strand omits most aviation, bicycles, canals, urban rail and buses, highways, sailing-to-steam transitions, maritime networks in the Indian Ocean, and inland Chinese or Japanese systems. It should not stand in for all mobility. No pattern count can estimate how often a mechanism causes adoption. Strong rail and container evidence partly reflects accessible economic datasets and English-language scholarship. One German article and an author’s Atacama field research improve the comparison without curing that selection.

Non-European populations are substantive cases rather than labels on a European chronology, but their voices remain unevenly represented. Official records and aggregate economic results cannot substitute for workers’, indigenous communities’ or residents’ accounts. A next research wave should deliberately select refusal, durable older practice, non-exposure and unsuccessful infrastructure projects, rather than adding only celebrated networks.

## Structural validation boundary

Validation checks ID uniqueness, schema fields, enum values, internal references, case/source/claim/graph consistency and report word count. It does not validate the truth of an archaeological interpretation, colonial record or causal model. The most consequential review targets are MOB-K02, MOB-K06, MOB-K08–10, MOB-K12–14, MOB-K16–17 and MOB-K20–21, plus the especially weak welfare evidence in MOB-C07.
