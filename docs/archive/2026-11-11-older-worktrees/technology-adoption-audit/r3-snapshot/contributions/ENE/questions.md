# ENE limits, rivals and follow-up work

## Scope and excluded inferences

- Five purposively chosen cases do not measure the historical prevalence of any mechanism. Latin America, Africa, Russia, South Asia and manufacturing outside energy/electronics remain underrepresented in this strand. The parent project supplies complementary coverage; this contribution must not be called a global manufacturing census.
- All market shares are tied to their original observation years and denominators. No current production, price, safety, energy or market claim is offered.
- The original request required substantive historical mechanism tests, not forecasts. Prospective sections in Yoshino, Weinert and the cost-model papers were deliberately excluded.
- We distinguish a patent from repeatable production; a factory from a deployed energy system; construction cost from total finance; a laboratory result from qualification; sales from users; and emissions from health damages.

## Consequential uncertainties and rivals

1. **PV model sensitivity (ENE-K02).** Nemet assigns plant scale 43% of 1980–2001 decline; Kavlak et al. assign 8%. Reconstructing the equations and common raw observations would determine how much follows from new inputs, changed system boundaries or interactions. This contribution reports the disagreement rather than electing one percentage as truth. Their shared industry data preclude treating agreement elsewhere as two independent replications.
2. **Chinese entry and skill (ENE-K04).** Equipment, tacit skill, low wages, energy prices, finance and foreign demand are concurrent. A longitudinal comparison of successful and failed upstream/downstream entrants would discriminate them better. Supplier counts are an imperfect concentration proxy; patents are especially poor measures of secret process knowledge. The inspected preprint abstract calls the interview set ten companies, but Appendix 1 lists nine firms and one university institute. No exact ten-firm representative claim is made.
3. **Nuclear survival and accounting (ENE-K05–K08).** Completed-project cost studies omit cancellation losses and many unsuccessful experimental routes. Interest exclusion makes overnight costs useful for a narrower resource-cost question but incomplete for financiers. Assemble all ordered, cancelled, delayed and completed projects by start cohort before estimating adoption rates or financing risk. The inspected Lovering text says 26 Korean reactor records while its cohort table implies a different total; resolve against the supplement before reproducing subgroup counts.
4. **Nuclear rivals.** Standardization, project continuity, regulation, political authority, labour organization, safety scope and financial institutions covary. France shows deployment despite escalation; Korea challenges universality of escalation, without proving standardization alone caused decline. An indirect account may be generated by regulation or redesign; indirect spending is not a causal residual called mismanagement.
5. **Historical French cost lineage.** Grubler's 2012 chapter inherits his 2009/2010 reconstruction. Lovering uses later Cour des Comptes reactor records and different cohort definitions. Check the 1999 French parliamentary accounts, 2012 audit and their normalization directly before reconciling exact escalation magnitudes. They are not independent duplicate measurements.
6. **Apollo and attribution (ENE-K10).** NASA's retrospective estimate of 60% of microcircuit output in summer 1963 is narrowly scoped and comes through endnotes, not invoices inspected here. Retrieve the underlying note 34 record and other major customers before estimating procurement's share of industry improvement. The chapter's Block I/II labels and cycle-time units are not used as evidence here because they warrant separate technical checking.
7. **DRAM identification (ENE-K11–K12).** Price and shipments substitute for missing marginal cost and plant yields. Cournot competition, elasticity and instruments qualify estimates. Stronger within-Japan spillover estimates are inconclusive even though faster Japanese learning is not supported. Proprietary Dataquest data were not re-estimated. New generation entry is not proof that all labour or capital barriers disappeared.
8. **Battery development (ENE-K13).** Yoshino's first-person retrospective is valuable but selected. Test the narrative against original 1980s patents, laboratory records and Sony/Asahi Kasei qualification/manufacturing records. A comparative impact test does not certify all safety dimensions, configurations or later devices. Do not reproduce incidental numerical statements from the lecture without independent technical checking.
9. **Battery cost classification (ENE-K14–K15).** The model concerns representative high-energy 18650 cells and defines nonroutine pilot/process work as R&D. A small routine-learning share is not a small human contribution. Public/private finance effects and endogenous demand are not identified; proprietary electrolyte/process information and sparse early data remain constraints.
10. **Why lead-acid persisted (ENE-K16).** Upfront price, lifetime uncertainty, service networks, availability, theft risk, user mass/volume preferences and credit constraints are competing mechanisms. The contemporary studies establish dominance/coexistence and approximate prices, not a causal buyer choice model. A matched subsidy/credit or warranty comparison with actual vehicle configurations would discriminate explanations.
11. **Urban regulation (ENE-K17).** Bans correlate with congestion, pollution, enforcement and preferences. Find city timelines and pre-policy trends rather than treating the 10-city snapshot as a natural experiment. Distinguish removed options from voluntary adoption. The source's illustrative outlet-voltage statement is not reproduced as a fact about China's supply.
12. **Externality incidence (ENE-K18).** The lead inventory inherits old national flows, uses modeled service lives and omits several lifecycle processes. Obtain plant-specific modernized historical controls and actual exposure cohorts before claiming realized health damages. Counterfactual travel matters; replacing a bicycle or bus has a different implication from replacing a car.

## Labour and entry questions left unanswered

- Follow individual factory/assembly/construction workers rather than estimating welfare from unit costs. Need wages, conditions, gender, migration, credentials, unpaid maintenance and retraining duration.
- Examine who controlled access to transferable knowledge: PV vendor contracts and no-poach arrangements; semiconductor equipment/process licensing; battery trade secrets; qualified nuclear suppliers.
- Follow failed firms and cancelled plants as well as surviving productive assets. Exit from one DRAM line does not establish unemployment, company death or successful redeployment.
- Examine nonchooser rights in supply communities and electricity systems. France's concentrated decision process, Chinese city bans and upstream lead burdens cannot be collapsed into one measure of consent.
