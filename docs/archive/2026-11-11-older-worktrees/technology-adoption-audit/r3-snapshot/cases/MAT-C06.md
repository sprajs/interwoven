# MAT-C06 — West Mexican metals: sound, colour and selective technical transfer

**Period:** Approximately 600/800 CE to early sixteenth century; differing starting dates in inspected scholarship. **Region:** Americas; West Mexico; Central/South American comparison. **Domain:** copper alloys; bells; status objects; tools.

Studies identify technical uses beyond hardness and propose transmission with regional reinterpretation; this case is access-limited.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MAT/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No earliest inventor established; reported 600/800 CE dates are not merged into a single secure first.

**feasibility:** Alloy and fabrication knowledge is described by the original researcher; underlying object catalogue not independently reanalysed.

**deployment:** Tools, bells and sheet ornaments are attested in the studies.

**diffusion:** Proposed links with Ecuador, Colombia, lower Central America and later Peru; abstract-level access limits evaluation.

**scaling:** No verified production volume or consumer denominator in inspected material.

**non-adoption/niche:** No case is made that absence of a Eurasian-style iron transition meant failure or lack of sophisticated metallurgy.

**displacement/reversal:** Colonial-period change is outside the inspected evidence; no automatic technological replacement asserted.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** West Mexican copper-alloy bells, ornaments and tools, with proposed South/Central American technical links

**task:** Sound, visual appearance, status/ritual action and practical tool functions; meanings varied and require further audit

**population:** Artefacts and social settings discussed in Hosler’s studies; detailed collections and adopter population not independently inspected

**interval:** Approximately 600/800 CE to early sixteenth century; differing starting dates in inspected scholarship

**contemporary alternatives:** Different metals/alloys and fabrication routes named in the studies; Other sounding, ornamental and tool materials are possible alternatives, but comparative access/use not established here

**adoption measure:** Qualitative abstract-level descriptions; no production or adoption-rate measure. Circa AD 600 and AD 800 starting frameworks remain distinguished.

**observation limits:** Abstract access cannot support object-frequency claims, complete carrier routes or exact ethnographic/ethnohistoric interpretation; elite symbolism may be overrepresented.

## Actors

**chooser:** Smiths, patrons and ritual users are candidate choosers; roles cannot be audited from abstracts.

**payer:** Unknown; neither status associations nor technically elaborate objects reveal exact payer.

**operator maintainer:** Metalworkers and object users; mining, repair and maintenance organisation unavailable in inspected text.

**affected nonchooser:** Workers and non-elite households may be affected, but their experience is absent from inspected evidence.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown; symbolic importance does not establish voluntary participation or coercion.

## Labour

**tasks:** Casting/working copper alloys and producing sounding/visual objects; task list inferred, no employment series.

**entry barriers:** Knowledge and material access; institutional barriers unknown due limited access.

**bargaining ownership:** Unknown; elite/ritual consumption does not itself identify artisan power or ownership.

**transition:** No individual/cohort transition evidence; even initial chronology differs between abstracts.

**distribution:** No observed livelihood distribution.

**evidence limit:** Abstract-level evidence cannot sustain occupational displacement or social-welfare claims.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: sonic and visual performance; status and ritual demand; regional reinterpretation; transfer of know-how hypothesis

Only abstracts of Hosler 1988/1995 acquired; symbolic meanings, route and exact chronology require full-text follow-up.

## Evidence links

- [MAT-K19](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k19): Hosler proposed South/Central American transmission into West Mexico combined with local technical transformation; this is a claim about selective recombination, not passive copying.
