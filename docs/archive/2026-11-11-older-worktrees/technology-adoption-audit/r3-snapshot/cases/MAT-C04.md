# MAT-C04 — Meroe and Hamadab: workable ironmaking and long-lived fuel selection

**Period:** Iron production attested from at least seventh–sixth centuries BCE into the first millennium CE; experiments model later/post-Meroitic workshops. **Region:** Africa; Sudan: Meroe and Hamadab. **Domain:** iron; fuel; experimental archaeology.

Replica smelts reveal operating constraints, while charcoal identifies persistent fuel selection without resolving landscape sustainability.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MAT/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No claim about first African ironmaking or external versus independent origins.

**feasibility:** Archaeological waste proves ancient production; modern experiments identify one viable reconstructed operating range.

**deployment:** Workshops and slag accumulations demonstrate sustained local operation.

**diffusion:** Destination and social distribution of much output remain unresolved.

**scaling:** Large and variable accumulations imply substantial production but do not yield a verified annual series.

**non-adoption/niche:** Unsuitable experimental ores and operating combinations fail; ancient non-user groups are not measured.

**displacement/reversal:** Production extends beyond Kushite political transformations; final cessation and its causes are not established here.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Ancient Meroe/Hamadab ironmaking and selected fuels; separately modern replicas of later/post-Meroitic workshops

**task:** Producing iron/steel stock; ultimate ancient product destinations not established

**population:** Ancient producers represented by workshop/debris and charcoal samples; modern experimental teams provide operating evidence, not ancient actor testimony

**interval:** Iron production attested from at least seventh–sixth centuries BCE into the first millennium CE; experiments model later/post-Meroitic workshops

**contemporary alternatives:** Different local ores and charging/blast configurations tested experimentally; Alternative charcoal taxa occur in non-metallurgical contexts; ancient metallurgical substitution is not demonstrated; Imported iron or semi-products are potential consumer alternatives but unmeasured

**adoption measure:** Workshop/debris presence and charcoal composition; experimental success/failure and yields under stated modern conditions. No ancient annual output or adoption-population denominator.

**observation limits:** Long-lived deposits are time aggregates; modern experiments cannot substitute for ancient labour/demand evidence; charcoal may be imported.

## Actors

**chooser:** Ancient smelters demonstrably selected fuel; who controlled production targets is unknown. Modern experimenters chose trial parameters.

**payer:** Ancient institutional or household funding unknown; modern experiments are not evidence of ancient financing.

**operator maintainer:** Ore preparers, charcoal suppliers, furnace builders, smelters and bellows operators; later forging/repair labour incompletely traced.

**affected nonchooser:** Local or distant fuel-source communities and product users; no measured harms/benefit distribution.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown for ancient workers and suppliers; royal-centre context is not proof of coercion.

## Labour

**tasks:** Coordination of ore preparation, fuel production, furnace construction, bellows and smelting; ancient paid/unpaid status unknown.

**entry barriers:** Tacit operating skill and suitable ore/fuel; modern expert trial difficulty demonstrates process demands, not ancient social exclusivity.

**bargaining ownership:** Unknown; debris does not identify owners, wage setting or bargaining.

**transition:** Ancient multi-century continuity; modern trials are learning sequences, not ancient labour-transition evidence.

**distribution:** No group-level wages, exposure or welfare measures.

**evidence limit:** Cannot turn reconstructed yields into ancient employment, working hours or coercion rates.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: ore quality and preparation; bellows operation; tuyère quality; fuel selectivity; possible woodland management or imports

Experimental changes are not cleanly isolated; charcoal survival cannot rule out damage elsewhere in a supply catchment.
