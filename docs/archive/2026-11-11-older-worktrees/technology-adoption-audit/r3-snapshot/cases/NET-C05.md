# NET-C05 — Ottoman printed-book permissions and Müteferrika's market

**Period:** 1588–1748; documentary permission and 1720s–1740s press/ownership subepisodes. **Region:** Ottoman Empire; Istanbul. **Domain:** printing; regulation; book markets.

An alleged blanket printing ban is not established by the inspected documentary critique. Actual permissions were actor- and subject-specific. Müteferrika's reconstructed output, estate stock and selected probate ownership show production/circulation, but do not measure exact sales or mass readership.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/NET/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No invention claim; multiple Ottoman printing communities and imported books predate the particular press studied.

**feasibility:** Existing printed books and Müteferrika editions demonstrate production; legal permission and market viability remain distinct.

**deployment:** The 1588 firman protects specified imported-book trade; the 1727 grant authorises a particular enterprise with subject/correction conditions.

**diffusion:** Sixteen of 335 selected Istanbul askeri probate inventories with more than three books contain the studied prints, 1724–1748; not a population adoption rate.

**scaling:** Sabev reports 8,200 initial copies for thirteen known-run editions and estimates three other runs; 2,981 copies remain in the estate. Production minus stock is not observed sales.

**non-adoption/niche:** Title choices, subject limits, price and distribution constrain the observed market; motives and effects are not isolated.

**displacement/reversal:** Neither universal commercial failure nor replacement of manuscripts is demonstrated; residual stock and circulation coexist.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Three distinct evidential units: a 1588 protection for merchants' printed Arabic books; the 1727 permission/fatwa; Müteferrika's output/estate and selected Istanbul ownership records.

**task:** Authorise, reproduce, market and acquire particular texts with acceptable textual accuracy; not all Ottoman printing or religious literature.

**population:** Named protected merchants, the permitted press and 335 selected askeri decedents with more than three books in the studied Istanbul registers.

**interval:** 1588 permission; press activity examined mainly 1726–1746; ownership sample 1724–1748, with unequal title exposure times.

**contemporary alternatives:** Manuscript books; Imported printed books; Other Ottoman printing communities and title markets, not counted as failed exposure; Borrowing or gifts rather than purchase

**adoption measure:** Known-run output of 8,200 copies across thirteen editions, three estimated additional runs, 2,981 estate copies; sixteen positive selected probates among 335. None is a complete sales/reader rate.

**observation limits:** Original register not independently recounted; gifts, valuation/finishing differences, title ages, archive selection and living/non-owning readers prevent simple sell-through or literacy estimates.

## Actors

**chooser:** The press's petitioners/publishers choose titles subject to the grant; authorities delimit permission; buyers/recipients choose or receive available works.

**payer:** Enterprise resources and purchasers, with gifts removing stock without sales; original funding and profit accounts are incomplete.

**operator maintainer:** Printing workers and appointed learned correctors; source passages establish correction requirements more clearly than everyday staffing or remuneration.

**affected nonchooser:** Scribes, excluded authors/readers and workers may be affected; no numerical livelihood or suppression effect is established.

**ability to refuse:** Formal subject and correction conditions bind this project. Individual purchasers' alternatives and coercion are not inferred from ownership; absence of a blanket ban is not universal freedom.

## Labour

**tasks:** Text selection, printing, learned correction, finishing and distribution; manuscript copying remains an alternative.

**entry barriers:** Authorisation for this enterprise, resources, linguistic/craft competence and customers. The evidence does not establish uniform empire-wide entry rules.

**bargaining ownership:** Press ownership and official validation shape control, but workers' bargaining power and occupational earnings are not documented here.

**transition:** Estate and selected ownership snapshots do not track scribes becoming printers or individual livelihood transitions.

**distribution:** The sample includes some religious functionaries but excludes most ordinary/non-owning readers; no social-class or gender incidence estimate.

**evidence limit:** No exact profit, national literacy effect, wholesale scribal displacement or quantified welfare comparison between manuscripts and print.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: specific legal permission; proofreading authority; title selection; distribution and demand; evidence-mediated reputation

Missing blanket-ban documents do not establish an absence of narrower restrictions. Some owners were religious officials. Initial runs are partly estimated, gifts remove stock without sales, and probate inventories privilege particular deceased owners.
