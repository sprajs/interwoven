# AGR-C04 — Modern crop varieties, 1965–2010, and a bounded Indian groundwater contrast

**Period:** Variety effects, 1965–2010; distinct groundwater observation, August 2002–October 2008. **Region:** Developing-country crop sample; Rajasthan; Punjab; Haryana including Delhi; India. **Domain:** agriculture; crop breeding; irrigation; resource use.

Modern varieties spread with local adaptation and supporting systems. An econometric study estimates large direct yield gains, while a separately measured Indian groundwater episode demonstrates why output alone cannot describe resource costs or identify their exact technological cause.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/AGR/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Crop breeding and release are plural processes; no single invention date is assigned to the whole Green Revolution.

**feasibility:** Varieties' realised performance depends on crop, environment, adaptation and inputs; shorter maturity can alter multiple-cropping possibilities.

**deployment:** Release/adoption data cover food crops and countries in the specified research sample; regional Indian pumping is a different observed system.

**diffusion:** Historical country/crop adoption and release variation enter the Gollin study; no universal farmer-level adoption percentage is asserted here.

**scaling:** The direct yield estimate holds allocations fixed; reallocation and wider economic effects are distinct model components.

**non-adoption:** Lower uptake in marginal environments may reflect unsuitable complements, risk or access; it does not uniquely identify reluctance or ignorance.

**displacement:** New varieties and altered crop choices can coexist with older cultivars; this contribution does not reconstruct complete variety replacement or biodiversity loss.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: modern food-crop varieties with locally varying breeding, irrigation and input systems. B: northwestern Indian groundwater extraction within a farming/water-use system, independently measured rather than assigned as an effect of a particular seed.

**task:** A: increase crop yield and feasible production. B: provide water for irrigation and other uses while the study measures changes in groundwater stock.

**population:** A: crop-country observations and a 90-country baseline in Gollin et al., with broader retrospective context from Pingali. B: regional storage across Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana including Delhi; a geographic stock measure, not the same people as the yield sample.

**interval:** A: 1965–2010. B: August 2002–October 2008. These are linked conceptually for accounting, not a matched causal comparison.

**contemporary alternatives:** Existing locally adapted varieties and crop mixes; Alternative breeding/innovation paths excluded from the strict no-modern-varieties counterfactual; Rainfed or differently irrigated production where feasible; Different water-use/crop choices, not assumed equally accessible

**adoption measure:** A: historical modern-variety/release variation and modelled yield contribution, not an ownership census. B: satellite-derived groundwater stock trend; no adoption measure and no seed attribution.

**observation limits:** Country/crop aggregation obscures household distribution. Release timing needs identification assumptions; the groundwater estimate uses modelled storage subtraction. Domains, denominators and intervals differ.

## Actors

**chooser:** Breeders, national research programmes, public agencies and farmers choose different parts of the system; pumping/crop decisions and water policy need not be made by the same actors.

**payer:** Public/international research and local infrastructure finance coexist with farmer input expenditure. Costs and subsidies vary; exact incidence is not reconstructed across the sample.

**operator maintainer:** Farmers, workers, irrigation/pump operators and input/service suppliers sustain use; breeding requires continuing research and adaptation.

**affected nonchooser:** Consumers affected by food supply/prices, landless labourers, households with unequal access, downstream or future groundwater users; their effects are not all measured by the selected studies.

**ability to refuse:** Farmer adoption choices are observed under constrained land, water, price and policy conditions. These data do not establish free choice for every worker or allow future water users to refuse depletion.

## Labour

**tasks:** Changes in cropping frequency, input application, irrigation and crop allocation can reorganise work; the contribution does not estimate a uniform labour-saving or labour-using effect.

**entry barriers:** Locally suitable seed, water, knowledge, finance, land and input access; relative importance varies by crop and environment.

**bargaining ownership:** Land/water ownership and input/price policy can affect capture of productivity gains, but the selected aggregate yield estimate does not identify individual bargaining outcomes.

**transition:** The crop-country series spans decades, not individual occupational transitions; resource monitoring spans six years and does not trace household adjustment.

**distribution:** Average yield gains are not a measure of who gained income or control. Groundwater losses impose potential costs beyond current extracting households; exact household incidence is unmeasured here.

**evidence limit:** No individual employment forecast, universal farmer welfare claim or seed-specific depletion share follows from these studies.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: local agronomic adaptation; water and input complements; crop maturity/calendar; release timing and diffusion; resource stock accounting; private versus social outcomes

Large average yield estimates do not imply universal gains. Resource depletion is measured in a particular region and period and is not an identified seed-specific effect; different studies share some underlying crop-data traditions.

## Evidence links

- [AGR-K10](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k10): Pingali's retrospective describes modern crop varieties as part of an adaptation and input system, with uneven early benefits across crops and environments; shorter maturity, suitable water and local breeding could change the feasible cropping calendar.
- [AGR-K11](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k11): Gollin, Hansen and Wingender estimate a 44% direct yield gain in the average country from modern varieties during 1965–2010, holding allocations fixed, in their historical econometric framework; the baseline country sample contains 90 countries.
- [AGR-K12](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k12): Rodell and colleagues estimate groundwater depletion of 17.7 ± 4.5 km³ per year in Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana including Delhi during August 2002–October 2008 and judge irrigation and other human withdrawals the likely cause after examining other storage components.
- [AGR-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k24): Across these selected cases, testing a device or plot is often insufficient to evaluate the larger system that must operate, and gains in adoption, effort or output do not alone establish who benefits or whether welfare improves.
