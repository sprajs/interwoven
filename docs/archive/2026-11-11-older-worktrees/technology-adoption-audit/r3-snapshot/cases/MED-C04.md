# MED-C04 — Municipal safe water and sewerage with contested mortality attribution

**Period:** 1880–1940. **Region:** Massachusetts, United States; Major US cities. **Domain:** sanitation; public goods; urban infrastructure.

A Massachusetts regional design supports local water/sewer complementarity; broader city-panel contributions remain disputed.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MED/report.md).

## Stages

**deployment:** Regional water/sewer completion and major-city filtration/chlorination installations are distinct episodes.

**diffusion:** Dated municipal installations; dates need not equal universal household service.

**scaling:** Regional finance and engineering connected multiple jurisdictions.

**non-adoption/niche:** Some cities used different source-water configurations; the regional untreated and single-treatment municipalities serve as comparisons.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: protected piped water plus regional sewerage around Boston. B: filtration/chlorination and other interventions in a major-city panel; coefficients not pooled.

**task:** Supply usable water and remove sewage; protect health and address nuisance.

**population:** A: 60 Massachusetts municipalities excluding Boston in estimation, including 15 receiving both treatments. B: 25 major cities, 1900–1940.

**interval:** A 1880–1920; B 1900–1940.

**contemporary alternatives:** Single infrastructure intervention; Alternative water sources/aqueducts; Other public-health and housing changes; Unconnected or untreated municipalities

**adoption measure:** Municipal completion indicators and annual mortality; water/sewer interaction. Household connection/use not directly observed; city-panel rates use differing population denominators.

**observation limits:** Service dates, partial years, spillovers, population changes and cause-of-death coding; A and B have different populations and treatment configurations.

## Actors

**chooser:** Municipal and state authorities, engineering/health boards; household choices constrained by networks.

**payer:** Regional/municipal public financing; Massachusetts law compelled participation and payment in the sewerage district.

**operator maintainer:** Engineers, construction and operating crews, inspectors; household connection work not fully measured.

**affected nonchooser:** Downstream communities, children and residents unable to choose the network or pollution source.

**ability to refuse:** Jurisdictions within the mandated district could not simply opt out; household connection obligations/refusal rates not audited.

## Labour

**tasks:** Construction, pumping, treatment, maintenance, monitoring and records; occupational counts unavailable.

**entry barriers:** Capital, engineering skill, land/network rights and public authorization.

**bargaining ownership:** Public regional institutions controlled system choice; distribution of contracts, labour gains and ownership rents unexamined.

**transition:** Infrastructure phased across decades; no individual labour transition data.

**distribution:** Child mortality examined, including demographic heterogeneity; these are health outcomes, not wage or job distribution.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer equitable livelihood gains from aggregate mortality reduction.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: cross-boundary externalities; regional coordination; joint infrastructure; capital and operating capacity; measurement sensitivity

Other sanitary change, nutrition and population composition compete with health attribution; panel disagreements share records.

## Evidence links

- [MED-K09](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k09): Alsan and Goldin estimate that combined safe water and effective sewerage account for about one-third of the decline in log under-five mortality in their jointly treated Massachusetts municipalities, 1880–1920; the interventions are complementary in their preferred model.
- [MED-K10](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k10): Regional compulsory participation and payment formed part of the Massachusetts sewerage response to pollution crossing municipal boundaries.
- [MED-K11](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k11): The size of clean-water contributions to aggregate US mortality decline remains disputed across analyses using overlapping records and different coding choices.
