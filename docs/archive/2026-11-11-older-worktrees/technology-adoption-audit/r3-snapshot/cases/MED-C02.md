# MED-C02 — Ottoman vaccination law and uneven delivery

**Period:** 1840–1915. **Region:** Ottoman Empire; Anatolia; Ottoman southeastern Europe; Istanbul. **Domain:** health; vaccination; state capacity.

Free provision and legitimacy efforts coexisted with compulsion and supply constraints; mandates are not coverage.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MED/report.md).

## Stages

**deployment:** 1840–1846 free-provision, instructional and legitimacy projects; observed institutional provision separated from promises.

**diffusion:** Regulatory expansion in 1885, 1894, 1903 and 1915; actual coverage remained incomplete.

**scaling:** Training, production and registration were attempted complements; comprehensive delivery not demonstrated.

**non-adoption/niche:** Institutional populations more reachable than dispersed rural households; no comparable rates.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Smallpox vaccination organized through imperial medical institutions, local officials, supplies and certification.

**task:** Protect subjects and populations; also expand official responsibility and knowledge of residents.

**population:** Institutional groups named in law versus rural/general populations; their denominators are not reconstructed.

**interval:** 1840–1846 instruction/provision; 1885–1915 successive regulatory episodes.

**contemporary alternatives:** Continued variolation (later penalized); No prophylaxis; Limited local or travelling provision

**adoption measure:** Legal eligibility/obligation and qualitative delivery history, not achieved coverage percentages.

**observation limits:** Regulations are normative; enforcement, successful takes and complete population denominators are missing.

## Actors

**chooser:** Sultan, ministries and local officials set rules; medical staff administered; families acted within those conditions.

**payer:** Imperial/local funding intended; recipients entitled to free provision, but non-price costs not measured.

**operator maintainer:** Physicians, trained vaccinators, local clerics/leaders, registration personnel; household transport and return visits.

**affected nonchooser:** Children, migrants, students and workers whose access to institutions could depend on certification.

**ability to refuse:** 1885 school/service conditions and later fines/obligations documented. Actual enforcement and rural ability to evade unknown; compliance does not establish consent.

## Labour

**tasks:** Training, vaccination, inspection, records and local mobilization; household attendance required unpaid time.

**entry barriers:** Literacy and professional certification requirements appear in policy; rural personnel shortages documented.

**bargaining ownership:** State certification and salaries shaped recognized practice; livelihood effects on older practitioners not quantified.

**transition:** Decades-long institutional expansion documented; no worker cohort or wage panel.

**distribution:** Institutional versus rural delivery contrast; no reliable class/gender earnings distribution.

**evidence limit:** Official workforce proposals cannot be counted as jobs actually filled or sustained.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: delivery capacity; legitimate messengers; zero monetary price with residual access costs; certification and compulsion; population legibility

Refusal, war and instability also mattered; official accounts cannot rank them against supply problems.

## Evidence links

- [MED-K03](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k03): Ottoman vaccination policy combined free provision commitments, multilingual instruction and religious endorsement before and alongside later compulsory rules.
- [MED-K04](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k04): Ottoman universal-vaccination ambitions were constrained by vaccine production, personnel and rural delivery capacity; legal scope exceeded documented implementation.
- [MED-K05](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k05): The 1885 Ottoman rules conditioned school entry and state-linked roles on vaccination evidence; later rules enlarged obligations and penalties, so legally induced uptake cannot be equated with consent.
