# SKL-C06 — Early TEA-laser replication: contact, specifications and material failure

**Period:** 1968–1975, centred on the 1971 network study. **Region:** Britain; Canada; United States. **Domain:** scientific instruments; replication; knowledge transmission.

A contemporary network study reports failed written-only builds and successful contact-linked builds, but also failed visits, unnecessarily strict specifications and component breakdowns. It supports bounded transfer mechanisms rather than an untestable necessity of tacit knowledge.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/SKL/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Origin laboratory built early devices before public announcement; publication date is not the invention date.

**feasibility:** Operating beams supplied an observable success criterion for configurations in the study; reliability varied.

**deployment:** Seven identified British labs were building/using devices in summer 1971, with North American source-lab contacts investigated subsequently.

**diffusion:** Purposive problem-defined network with explicit searches for isolates; no total world-lab denominator.

**scaling:** Commercial variants and a British purchase after failed construction were reported; no manufacturing yield or sales series.

**non-adoption/niche:** Some attempts failed or were abandoned; alternative fabrication and buying a finished device supplied different routes.

**displacement/reversal:** No general displacement of publications or theory; detailed instructions improved and hardware failures persisted.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Early transversely excited atmospheric-pressure CO2 lasers, with pin-bar and double-discharge models distinguished where construction details differ.

**task:** Obtain an operating laser beam, either to develop the instrument or use it in other experiments.

**population:** Seven identified British labs and investigated North American source labs; a 1975 follow-up builder is a separately identified episode.

**interval:** 1968 antecedent operation; 1970 announcement; 1971 interviews and later follow-ups through 1975.

**contemporary alternatives:** Build using publications; Visits, telephone assistance or personnel movement; Alternative electrode fabrication; Purchase a commercial laser once available; Abandon/postpone the experiment

**adoption measure:** Named but pseudonymised labs' operating/nonoperating devices, interview network and one reported British commercial purchase; no general adoption percentage.

**observation limits:** Snowball selection checked by questionnaires, funding records and citations; unsuccessful isolates still may be missed. No randomised exposure, complete cost comparison or uniform performance threshold.

## Actors

**chooser:** Laboratory scientists/managers chose build routes and disclosures; instrument need differed between laser-development and downstream experiments.

**payer:** Government/university research resources and commercial licence customers; comparative full build/purchase costs not audited.

**operator maintainer:** Researchers, technical staff, machinists and commercial consultants; their paid/unpaid hours are not separated.

**affected nonchooser:** Students and staff allocated to build/support work; subsequent application impacts are outside this case.

**ability to refuse:** Labs could withhold details or buy/abandon, but grant/resource constraints mattered. Individual junior researchers' refusal options are unknown.

## Labour

**tasks:** Machining, assembly, debugging, consulting, visiting and instruction; improved specification can redirect work to suppliers as well as people learning in-house.

**entry barriers:** Prior laboratory expertise, equipment and access to experienced builders; contacts could be governed by reciprocity and competition.

**bargaining ownership:** Variants were patented/licensed while articles and assistance circulated; researchers also guarded unpatented practical information for reputational competition.

**transition:** Build attempts over weeks/months and transfers/placements are described, not occupational displacement or lifetime earnings.

**distribution:** Experienced gatekeepers held attention and information advantages; effects on junior staff, technicians or public benefit are not quantified.

**evidence limit:** Shows specific communication/material bottlenecks, not that more funding never helps or that all instrument knowledge is unwriteable.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: productive contact; prior expertise; selective disclosure; identifying relevant tolerances; component reliability

Contact was not randomised, some contacts failed, and inexperienced or poorly resourced labs may differ systematically. Collins explicitly allowed written-only success and acknowledged purposive selection of a favourable example.

## Evidence links

- [SKL-K16](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k16): Among the early TEA-laser laboratories Collins investigated, written-only attempts had not succeeded and working copies were associated with contact with successful builders; visits sometimes failed, and the author explicitly did not claim written-only success was impossible.
- [SKL-K17](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k17): For some double-discharge TEA builds, demanding electrode-profile tolerances obstructed one laboratory while roughly filed profiles and aluminium banister rail worked elsewhere; the episode limits a general 'more precision solves replication' explanation.
- [SKL-K18](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k18): Accessible footnotes to Collins and Harrison's1975 follow-up record simultaneous changes to two build variables and repeated power-unit failures; those observations leave any exclusive knowledge-transfer explanation of that episode's delays underdetermined.
