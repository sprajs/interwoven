# MED-C06 — Sri Lankan malaria suppression, resurgence and elimination

**Period:** 1945–2016. **Region:** Sri Lanka. **Domain:** health; vector control; surveillance; public services.

Successful suppression reversed when a maintained system weakened; later elimination combined continuing institutional functions.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MED/report.md).

## Stages

**deployment:** Postwar indoor residual spraying within the Anti-Malaria Campaign.

**scaling:** Suppression reached very low recorded local transmission by 1963.

**displacement/reversal:** 1964 relaxation followed by late-1960s resurgence; later resistance prompted insecticide replacement.

**diffusion:** Coverage cannot be recovered directly from infection counts.

**feasibility:** 2016 certification records sustained interrupted local transmission, not permanent absence of risk.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: DDT indoor residual spraying, case detection and treatment. B: later adapted vector-control and surveillance system culminating in certification.

**task:** Suppress and ultimately interrupt local malaria transmission.

**population:** National reported cases with specific local observations; later certification population is Sri Lanka, imported infections distinct.

**interval:** A 1945–1977 suppression/reversal/replacement; B later programme culminating in 2016.

**contemporary alternatives:** Larval/environmental control; Case detection and treatment; Alternative insecticides; Reduced-control consolidation phase

**adoption measure:** Reported indigenous/imported cases, programme chronology and certification; not a population adoption rate.

**observation limits:** Changing surveillance, overlapping retrospective source traditions and absent district-level exposure denominator.

## Actors

**chooser:** Health ministry/Anti-Malaria Campaign and regional teams; donors and households also affected deployment.

**payer:** Public health budgets with international support; complete finance series not inspected.

**operator maintainer:** Spray workers, diagnostic staff, clinicians, surveillance teams and participating households.

**affected nonchooser:** Residents exposed to others' infections, spray policies, land-use change and service disruption.

**ability to refuse:** Household spray acceptance could vary; detailed refusal rights and enforcement not audited. Conflict and movement constrained practical access.

## Labour

**tasks:** Repeated spraying, microscopy/diagnosis, surveillance, treatment and coordinated field response.

**entry barriers:** Technical training, stable employment funding, transport and supplies; exact credential requirements unexamined.

**bargaining ownership:** Public programme and donor resource control; household voice and worker bargaining not measured.

**transition:** Spraying teams were disbanded during relaxation; individual livelihood trajectories absent.

**distribution:** Health exposure differed geographically, including conflict regions; no occupational income distribution measured.

**evidence limit:** Service capacity and staff counts are not evidence of net lifetime employment gains.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: recurrent maintenance; surveillance; resource allocation after apparent success; ecological change; technical resistance; coordination despite conflict

Detection intensity, migration, ecological change and resistance interact; retrospective labels such as complacency are not independently observed motives.

## Evidence links

- [MED-K15](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k15): Sri Lankan records reported six indigenous and eleven imported malaria cases in 1963, followed by reduced control activity and a major late-1960s resurgence.
- [MED-K16](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k16): The inspected chronology does not justify attributing Sri Lanka’s initial late-1960s reversal solely to DDT resistance: relaxation began earlier, while the account reports resistance in 1969, which is not a demonstrated date of biological onset. The account reports replacement of DDT by malathion in 1977.
- [MED-K17](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k17): WHO announced Sri Lanka’s malaria-free certification in September 2016 after three consecutive years of documented zero transmission.
- [MED-K18](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#med-k18): Cohen et al. catalogued 75 malaria resurgence events; causes were reported attributions, and only 16% of suggested causes had substantial supporting analysis.
