# SKL-C01 — H4/K1: copying a sea watch and keeping it navigationally useful

**Period:** 1760s–1779, with navigation-system context to c. 1800. **Region:** Britain; Atlantic and Pacific voyages. **Domain:** horology; navigation; repair.

A commissioned copy separated Harrison's demonstration from another maker's execution; later cleaning, corrosion and astronomical checks separated precision manufacture from durable navigational service.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/SKL/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** H4 is treated as the antecedent device; no sole-inventor or first-chronometer claim.

**feasibility:** Kendall's K1 completed in 1769 and tested on Cook's second voyage; one replication, not a production yield.

**deployment:** Named Cook voyage trials and service, followed by cleaning and further use.

**diffusion:** Not estimated; these state-supported voyages cannot stand for merchant or naval fleets.

**scaling:** K1 replication does not establish cheap or rapid repeated production.

**non-adoption/niche:** Contemporary alternatives included astronomical methods and dead reckoning; simplified watch accounts do not identify a uniform performance gain.

**displacement/reversal:** K1 reportedly stopped and needed replacement parts; astronomical practice remained complementary.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Harrison H4 copied by Kendall as K1, operated with rate knowledge and astronomical observations aboard specified voyages.

**task:** Infer longitude from reference time compared with local time while preserving a usable timekeeper during long voyages.

**population:** Kendall, Board of Longitude evaluators and Cook expedition officers; broader shipping populations not measured.

**interval:** 1766 commission; 1769 completion; second and third Cook voyages in the 1770s; contextual practices to c.1800.

**contemporary alternatives:** Lunar-distance observations with instruments and tables; Dead reckoning and latitude sailing; Other makers' marine timekeepers

**adoption measure:** One named surviving copy and documented trial/service episodes; no fleet denominator or diffusion rate.

**observation limits:** Completion and approximate delivery dates differ between S01 and S02. Reports do not provide comparable staffing, costs, breakdown exposure or voyage outcomes.

## Actors

**chooser:** Board commissioners commissioned and allocated trials; naval expedition leadership used the instrument.

**payer:** Board/state supplied commissioned instruments and trial resources; no audited all-in cost is claimed.

**operator maintainer:** Skilled maker and service personnel; expedition astronomers/officers observed and compared times. Supporting crew labour is not quantified.

**affected nonchooser:** Crews and communities encountered by expeditions; this instrument evidence does not isolate consequences for them.

**ability to refuse:** Commission was a negotiated maker contract. The sources do not establish individual crew consent or ability to choose a different navigation system.

## Labour

**tasks:** Precision copying, adjustment, cleaning, part replacement, rating and observation; commissioned craft was paid, shipboard task allocation and unpaid support unquantified.

**entry barriers:** Established watchmaking ability, access to mechanism/explanation and state evaluation; no estimate of apprenticeship returns.

**bargaining ownership:** Commissioners controlled test acceptance and resource allocation; Kendall's copying obligation did not equal a guarantee of performance in the obituary's account.

**transition:** Several years from commission to completed copy, followed by multi-year voyages; no occupational cohort evidence.

**distribution:** Recognised makers and officers are visible; wages, workshops' gains and ordinary mariners' welfare are not established.

**evidence limit:** Demonstrates distinct tasks supporting practical precision, not net job creation, equal access or the invention's social return.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: commissioned replication; skilled adjustment; maintenance; complementary measurement

The contemporary obituary praises Kendall and disputes cannot be resolved without original trial data. Other chronometer designs and other navigation methods limit any H4-centred genealogy.

## Evidence links

- [SKL-K01](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k01): After H4's sea trials, the Board commissioned Kendall to copy it; K1's 1769 completion and Cook-voyage operation provide a named replication by another maker, not evidence of economical serial manufacture.
- [SKL-K02](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k02): Kendall's obituary reports cleaning K1 in 1775 and, after deterioration and stoppage on Cook's third voyage, replacing some corroded parts; a working precision artifact therefore did not remove the need for service during its useful life.
- [SKL-K03](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#skl-k03): In the late eighteenth-century navigation system described by Higgitt, marine timekeepers and astronomical observations were used together; chronometer deployment did not make local-time observation or independent checks dispensable.
