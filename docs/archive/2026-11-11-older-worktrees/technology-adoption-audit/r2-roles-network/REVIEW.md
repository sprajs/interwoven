# R2 — Future roles, chronology and network integration

Reviewed only the frozen `r2-snapshot`, captured 2026-11-05 14:55:49 UTC: 73 cases, 266 claims, 246 edges. PSY/SKL are unfinished and excluded without penalty. No programme files were modified, and no programme participant was contacted. Browser findings below are source-inspection findings; the coordinating reviewer is testing the UI. ## Judgment

The future-role document is a careful research framework, not an occupational forecast disguised as historical causality. Its distinction between productive contribution, bargaining/ownership and practical entry is substantive (`analysis/FUTURE_ROLES.md:7`). Its transition checklist explicitly includes assets, relationships, lost income, exclusion and incumbent versus entrant status (:77–83). Candidate-specific substitution scenarios and limits are real, not cosmetic. No major unsupported future-role conclusion was found in this sample.

The collection is much stronger as a critical comparative case library than as a connected history of technological development. It explicitly acknowledges this boundary (`README.md:7`, `analysis/GLOBAL_MAP.md:35–60`). The remaining shortfall should be reported as an integration limitation, not repaired by inventing genealogical arrows or pretending analogous cases independently establish a law.

## Three actual propositions challenged

**P1 — Cohort “recovery” is imprecise; the underlying distinction survives.** `FUTURE_ROLES.md:9` says incumbent harm coexisted with “employment recovery for later cohorts.” The original paper states that automation “did not reduce future cohorts’ overall employment” (Feigenbaum–Gross, p.1879, abstract); it also identifies adverse adjustment environments in manufacturing-intensive cities and severe Depression contractions (pp.1883–1884; heterogeneity discussion p.1933). This is not a demonstrated fall-and-recovery sequence for every cohort, nor successful retraining of incumbents. Use “employment resilience among the studied later cohorts,” identify the main white American-born women aged 16–25/small-city population, and carry weak substitute demand into future-role stress tests. `contributions/LBR/report.md:9–11` and LBR-K02–K04 already make most of these distinctions. **Minor wording repair, not a rejection of the proposition.** [Original study](https://www.dpgross.com/papers/answering-call-automation.pdf).

**P2 — Competence-building does not establish a durable paid teaching niche.** Candidate B (`FUTURE_ROLES.md:27–35`) uses ORT to motivate teaching/adaptation. The original study itself reports “an intergenerational transfer of the knowledge on ORT” (Chowdhury et al., p.58) alongside expansion of packaged-ORS distribution. These are within-case challenges: successful teaching may diffuse informally, and a simpler product/distribution route may change what must be taught. The observation does not isolate the marginal effect of paid instruction or identify instructors’ durable earnings. `MED-K20–K21` and `contributions/MED/report.md:77–83` correctly separate programme reach, preparation and use. Candidate B already warns about free instruction and compares access/interface interventions; add this concrete adverse case and measure paid versus unpaid teaching/care time and who funds remaining training. **Survives as a conditional study proposition; no correction required to its causal status.** [Original paper, pp.58–59 inspected](https://academic.oup.com/heapol/article-pdf/12/1/58/6749104/12-1-58.pdf).

**P3 — Coordination can protect incumbent claims by excluding other workers.** Candidate C (`FUTURE_ROLES.md:39–47`) cites container interfaces and proposes resolving disputes/credible commitments as useful work. The 1968 judicial account records the 1960 union/employer bargain and a $29 million mitigation fund; its fiscal incidence was disputed. The later original judicial record describes container work reserved to longshoremen at PMA facilities within fifty miles and exclusion of a non-PMA firm employing Teamsters (California Cartage, opening account). Thus productive interoperability, incumbent compensation and entry into work are separate outcomes, and one labour group’s bargaining gain can restrict another’s access. Existing caveats about rents, power and nonchoosers are correct. Make this the named negative case and require comparison of gains/losses across incumbent union members, other workers, entrants and payers; do not use aggregate trade gains as compensation evidence. **Survives conditionally; concrete distribution test recommended.** [1968 opinion, paragraphs 2–4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/390/261); [1987 opinion, opening account](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/822/1203/373792/).

## Prioritised integration findings

1. **P2 defect: the main chronological route omits completed additions.** `analysis/GLOBAL_MAP.md:9–16` has no routes for the 14 completed ENE/OCE/POL cases; :29 still describes Oceania expansion as underway. `analysis/SYNTHESIS.md:3` reports 59/210/129 rather than the frozen 73/266/170, while :93 speaks of the future-role document as eventual. These texts explicitly identify themselves as first-wave work, so their earlier observations are not false. But the current browser exposes them as its main history and human-role routes (`index.html:6`), without a sufficiently prominent coverage/version boundary. Refresh routes to completed cases, or visibly mark the first-wave scope and offer an expanded chronological index. Do not include unfinished PSY/SKL.

2. **P2 usability defect: the graph and comparison references do not support traversal.** `index.html:31` renders edge endpoints as plain labels and claim/source identifiers as plain text; :32 does the same for comparison case IDs. From an edge or SYN-X04, readers cannot directly follow the named node, claim, source or comparison case. The evidence section does provide real source links and locators (:29,33), and local case/dossier links work in source; preserve that strength. Link existing references and give a selected node’s incident relations and scoped case routes. This is possible without asserting new historical connections.

3. **P2 source-confirmed URL-state defect, pending live reproduction:** selected case and section navigation both use the single fragment. `index.html:22–25,35` stores `#MED-C07` but section links replace it with `#relations`; reopening that URL defaults to the first case. Use separate case and section URL state so a reader can share/reload the evidence they reached.

4. **Material limitation: almost no inter-case dependency history exists yet.** The actual graph has 347 nodes, 246 edges, 109 weak components, four isolated nodes and largest component size seven. Zero edges cross research strands; zero edges list multiple cases; only three components span multiple cases, through ENE-T01, IND-T01 and IND-T14. ENE-T01’s multi-case label alone does not establish a dependency path between its two cases. The 53 comparisons provide a better conceptual bridge, but only SYN-X01–X04 cross strands. Seven edges are labelled documented transmission, all within strands. Consequently the graph cannot currently trace a broad sequence such as material capability → precision machinery → industrial production → electricity → computing. This is an honest coverage limit, not evidence those sequences occurred. Add a separate navigable analogy/comparison layer and a date-aware reading layer; research cross-case transmission only where evidence warrants it.

5. **Chronology limitation, not erroneous ordering:** the browser retains the ledger’s strand/ID order (`index.html:23`), beginning with AGR-C01 then modern agriculture before ancient ANC cases. Dates are heterogeneous prose, frequently several episodes per case. The static map offers useful coarse historical navigation and resists a universal-stage story, but offers no time filtering or episode ordering within broad intervals. A future date index should separate operating dates, source dates and modern reconstructions, with explicit uncertainty, rather than sorting on the first number in a string. A broad chronological index does not require causal edges.
