# Technology, adoption and human opportunity — research charter

Edition: research edition 1, initiated 5 November 2026. Status: in progress.

## Purpose
Build a broad, inspectable account of how technologies become feasible, get deployed, spread, remain niche, fail, disappear or are displaced. Historical mechanisms come before implications for future human roles. The programme is a purposive comparative investigation, not an exhaustive history or a representative statistical sample.

## Questions
1. What constraints does a technology relax, for whom, compared with which contemporary alternatives?
2. Which material, energy, knowledge, labour, infrastructure, institutional and coordination complements does practical use require?
3. Who chooses, who pays, who maintains, who benefits, who is compelled, and who bears harms?
4. How do prices, wages, finance, scale, distribution, repair, trust, status, habit, bodily limits and switching costs interact?
5. What distinguishes invention, reproducible feasibility, deployment, diffusion, scaling, non-adoption and displacement?
6. Which rival explanations survive regional, temporal and negative-case contrasts? What evidence would disprove them?
7. What historically grounded questions should guide investigation of future human opportunities, without pretending to forecast winners?

## Commitments
- Generate candidate explanations from broad knowledge, then inspect evidence. Memory and plausible reasoning are not verified historical evidence.
- Treat cheapness, safety, convenience and psychological preferences as hypotheses whose actors, measures and periods must be specified.
- Include ancient and Roman engineering, metallurgy, medieval and Renaissance developments, industrialisation and digital systems, with substantial non-European cases.
- Treat technology as a branching, converging dependency network, with uncertain links and alternatives. Prerequisites are not inferred merely from chronology.
- Preserve contradictions, negative cases, missingness and contested scholarship. Do not invent firsts, diffusion denominators, prevalence or causal certainty.
- Separate observed evidence, historical interpretation, modelling assumptions, synthesis inference and speculation.
- Distinguish private desirability, buyer incentives, political power, state capacity and social welfare. Coercion and externalities are possible mechanisms, not incidental footnotes.
- Separate acquired full text from abstracts, metadata, search excerpts and inaccessible leads. Cite claim-specific locators wherever available.
- No external publication, pushing, correspondence or paid procurement. Work remains local.

## Execution
A first wave of eight independent Astra researchers covers complementary eras and domains. Subsequent waves fill geographic and causal gaps, develop rival mechanisms and conduct independent criticism. Synthesis is followed by source audits and substantive repairs, then re-review. Agent assignments and outcomes are recorded in PLAN.md and reviews/RESPONSE_LOG.md.

## Deliverables
Human-readable entry guide, methods, global chronological map, structured case library, claim/source ledgers, provenance-bearing dependency/adoption graph, mechanism and rival-explanation synthesis, rejected hypotheses, uncertainty register, explicitly conditional future-role implications, review records and reproducible structural validation.

## Stopping rule
Stop this edition only after: (a) major planned era/domain coverage is present; (b) synthesis has been challenged in at least two materially distinct review rounds; (c) critical defects are repaired or the affected claims withdrawn; (d) unresolved substantive limitations are visible; (e) source and data integrity checks pass. This is an edition boundary, not a claim that historical angles are exhausted.
