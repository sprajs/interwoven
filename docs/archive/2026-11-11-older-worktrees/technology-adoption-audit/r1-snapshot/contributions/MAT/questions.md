**MAT: unresolved questions, rival explanations, and access limits**

This contribution provides seven bounded cases, not a global chronology of all early materials. The questions below distinguish work needed to revise an existing claim from attractive additional coverage. No unresolved item licenses filling in missing actors, production rates or dates.

**Consequential claim audits**

1. **Canaan supply and quality, MAT-K05–K07.** The inspected 2015 synthesis explicitly labels parts of its 95-object bronze-composition and iron-metallography evidence unpublished. Locate the subsequent object-level publications, if any; audit context, preservation, selection and changes in tin content. Persistent alloy recipes do not measure price or access. Compare plausible input-shortage episodes against iron expansion and the development of urban production. A secure technical change preceding wider use would weaken the claim that no treatment threshold explains the transition. Conversely, changes in administrative installations after production expands would weaken an administrative-initiation account.

2. **Chinese complement and institution hypotheses, MAT-K08–K11.** The 1999 Wagner account is deliberately retained as a documented revision, not the latest inventory of early iron. The 2025 synthesis supplies recent discussion but makes sweeping China/West and monopoly claims that need separate testing. Inspect original Qin, Guo and other early assemblages, distinguishing meteoritic from smelted iron, casting from primary cast-iron smelting, and surface treatment from complete decarburisation. Seek production under claimed restrictions. Do not infer a continuous monopoly from one policy date or assume that all regions had identical craft organisation.

3. **Meroe environment and organisation, MAT-K12–K14.** Charcoal selection is strong evidence; its source radius and annual demand are not. A further test needs dated vegetation history, fuel provenance, ore mass balance, repeatable experiments and actual production intensities. Local depletion plus imported acacia could reproduce stable charcoal composition. Modern replica improvements altered several variables, so isolate ore, draft, tuyère position and charging in further trials before assigning their individual effects. Existing experimental learning is not evidence about ancient wages, worker dependence or apprenticeship.

4. **Andean political distribution, MAT-K17.** Read the underlying mine/production-site histories on which Lechtman’s imperial interpretation rests, preferably paired before/after incorporation and official/local contexts. Continued local arsenical bronze might reflect autonomous demand, geology, transport costs or permitted coexistence. It need not prove either total imperial control or no imperial influence. Separate the mechanical experiments, which are directly inspected, from political interpretation inherited through that paper.

5. **Ile-Ife primary manufacture and origins, MAT-K20–K22.** Raw-batch reaction products strongly support primary production. The precise chronology of museum-store specimens, local raw-material source and transfer of ideas are less resolved. Independent field sequences and additional potential production centres could distinguish workshop relocation, continuing manufacture, later rediscovery and recurrence from similar geological resources. The authors’ autonomous-origin conclusion is stronger than the narrow evidential claim adopted here. Related beads elsewhere do not uniquely identify trade direction or demonstrate that their makers learned the process at Ile-Ife.

**Important access boundaries and leads**

- **MAT-S10, Hosler 1995:** only the publisher abstract was available; the attempted full-article URL failed. The full paper is needed to inspect the laboratory–ethnohistoric connection, historical passages and object prevalence. [Publisher record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00438243.1995.9980295).
- **MAT-S11, Hosler 1988:** the PDF link redirected to the abstract. Transmission phases and local transformation are recorded as the author’s proposal. Detailed diagnostic techniques, transport evidence and archaeological sequence were not audited. [Publisher record](https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1525/aa.1988.90.4.02a00040).
- **MAT-S12, Ile-Ife crucibles:** opening PDF pages were inspected, but repeated body retrievals timed out or returned access errors. Do not present the whole crucible dataset as independently checked. The reported 1–7 L capacity is not annual throughput. [Repository copy](https://repository.cyi.ac.cy/bitstream/CyI/1939/1/Babalola%20et%20al%202018%20Glassmaking%20crucibles%20Ile-Ife%20JAA%2016.pdf).
- **Additional Chinese primary-study lead, not evidence used:** *Coexistence of bloomery iron and cast iron in a Qin state cemetery dating to the mid Warring States period* (2025), [publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352226725000054). Search result only; investigate rather than treat it as proof of the magnitude of continued bloomery use.
- **Additional technical lead, not evidence used:** *Invention of cast iron smelting in early China: Archaeological survey and numerical simulation* (2021), [publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667136021000017). Not independently inspected beyond search results. Simulation assumptions need separate audit from archaeological chronology.
- **Additional Meroe lead, not evidence used:** *The ancient iron mines of Meroe*, [publisher page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0067270X.2018.1515922). The experimental paper describes the ore hypothesis, but this mining investigation was not acquired/read. It could materially improve raw-material sourcing.
- **Xianrendong supporting files:** the main paper was inspected through an author upload; the supplementary micromorphology and complete dating methods were not independently acquired. A targeted replication of context integrity and calibration would be needed before treating the date as beyond revision. No social-media critique is used as evidence.

**Missing contrasts that could change the synthesis**

Sub-Saharan Africa’s earliest iron chronologies, including arguments for and against independent invention, are not adjudicated by a case about Meroe’s later operating tradition. West/Central African sequences, East African production and different furnace systems need original dating/context audits. Neither a diffusionist continental centre nor continent-wide independent invention should be inferred from the two African cases included here.

South Asian crucible steel, Southeast Asian bronze/iron coexistence, Near Eastern glass routes, early ceramic traditions in Africa and the Americas, and American non-smelting copper traditions would add materially different production systems. They are coverage gaps, not negative adoption cases. Their absence from this contribution does not imply lack of historical capability.

The present cases overrepresent visible workshops, mineral residues and durable objects. They underrepresent successful recycling, non-metal substitutes, low-status users, bodily labour, exposure, women's work, apprenticeship and groups unable to choose. Reports of unusual or prestigious artefacts can amplify novelty while ordinary tools disappear. A sample of non-adopters must distinguish rejection from non-exposure, lack of access and loss of evidence.

**Labour and welfare boundaries**

Task descriptions are better supported than occupations. Several process steps may belong to one practitioner, and one archaeological installation may be shared. No inspected case provides individual or cohort trajectories through technological change. Thus the structured labour fields preserve unknown pay, ownership, bargaining, refusal and distribution. No estimate of jobs displaced, new incomes, reduced effort or common prosperity follows from a better experimental yield, greater object count or specialised facility.

**Comparison discipline**

The four entries in `comparisons.json` are consequential contrasts, not independent votes for a general mechanism. Multiple Ile-Ife papers share sites, researchers and a production theory; the two Meroe papers share project chronology; the Chinese syntheses reuse earlier finds. Distinct specimens can strengthen a narrow inference while leaving shared provenance or historical interpretation untested. `sources.json` records these lineages without claiming a complete genealogy.

Do not compute a success rate from seven purposively selected cases. Do not count the 24 claims as 24 independent observations. No transitive causal closure across the early Chinese and Song–Yuan subepisodes is warranted. Graph entries marked hypothetical remain hypothetical even when all their nodes have archaeological evidence.
