**Independent review of the technology-adoption research design — 5 November 2026**

**Assessment: proceed with research, but tighten the contribution contract before integrating the first wave.** The written design is substantially stronger than a collection of technological success stories. It explicitly rejects representative-sample claims, chronology-as-dependency, outcome-inferred preferences and universal necessities; it requires rival explanations, distinguishes source access, and treats coercion and welfare separately. These are genuine strengths, not omissions to rediscover.

This is an early design audit, not approval of historical findings. Priority P1 means repair before integrated causal conclusions or graph export. P2 means resolve before the edition handoff. “Present” describes an ambiguity or omission in the written contract; “risk” describes a possible defect in work that has not arrived.

1. **D01 — P1, present: the structured case contract does not preserve its own unit of analysis or the meaning of adoption.**

   [METHODS.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/METHODS.md:6), lines 6–18, requires a population, contemporary choice set and differentiated stages. Yet its case fields at line 36 provide region, period, outcomes and a free-form stages object without defined population, comparator or observation fields. Modern claims explicitly require denominators; historical qualitative proxies lack an equivalent recording rule. Narrative compliance could therefore disappear from machine-readable comparison.

   **Repair:** define an episode record, or equivalent required subfields, containing technology configuration/task, population, interval, contemporary alternative, outcome measure or qualitative proxy, and observation limits. Distinguish installations, production share, ownership and sustained use. Identify chooser, payer, operator and affected population where they differ; attach evidence of mandates, sanctions, dependency or unavailable alternatives. Permit “unknown” with a reason. Coercion can vary by actor and episode; it should not be one binary label for a whole society.

   **Later acceptance:** a query can recover who adopted what, relative to what alternative, measured how, and whether affected people could refuse. A purchaser's choice cannot stand in for workers' consent or population welfare. Changes in the surviving record must not automatically become diffusion curves. Wilson's watermill study shows why: documentary frequency changes with genres of surviving texts, while archaeological evidence has different preservation problems. This supports an observation-process check, not a definitive adoption estimate. [Wilson, 2007, body paragraphs on documentary and archaeological evidence](https://oxrep.classics.ox.ac.uk/working_papers/uptake_mechanical_technology_ancient_world_watermill/).

2. **D02 — P1, present: graph semantics do not yet distinguish historical transmission from feasibility, or necessity from contribution.**

   [CHARTER.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/CHARTER.md:21), line 21, correctly rejects chronological prerequisites. [METHODS.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/METHODS.md:39), lines 39–40, supplies useful typed edges, case/claim links and qualifications. However, “enables” can still mean an engineer's reconstruction, documented local use, or an actual transmission pathway. “Depends-on” lacks an explicit standard for necessity. Multiple incoming edges do not specify whether inputs are jointly required or substitutable. Qualifications could express these distinctions, but their interpretation is not specified.

   **Repair:** define edge admissibility and meaning. Resolve every substantive edge to a particular episode/stage and claim; distinguish technical requirement, observed contribution, documented/inferred transmission and hypothesis. Record relevant alternative routes and joint-input bundles when they affect the explanation. Necessity needs a bounded counterfactual rationale. Preserve claim uncertainty and dispute status in exports rather than displaying confidence alone. Do not infer transitive dependencies across different places or periods.

   **Later acceptance:** reviewers can distinguish “B uses A,” “B could use A,” “B acquired A from this source,” and “B required A under these conditions.” Chronology without pathway evidence remains chronology. An alternative route, joint requirement or adoption-to-cost feedback can be represented without falsely implying a sufficient cause, a universal prerequisite or a time-travelling causal loop.

3. **D03 — P1, present: a potential falsifier is required, but an actual discriminating evidence record is not.**

   [METHODS.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/METHODS.md:17), lines 17–18, already calls for pathways, rivals and potential falsifiers. The claim contract at line 38 stores counterevidence and limits, but not the rival's predictions, what was sought, or how an observation changes the comparison. A plausible list of costs, institutions and preferences could satisfy the fields without showing which explanation survives scrutiny. This is a contract weakness, not evidence that the unwritten cases contain unfalsifiable claims.

   **Repair:** for claims carrying the synthesis, preserve a compact mechanism record: initial conditions → actor action → intermediate change → adoption outcome; strongest credible rival; discriminating observation; search/result; remaining ambiguity. Record whether the hypothesis preceded the search or emerged from it. Treat exploratory revisions as legitimate, but visible. Check whether falling costs, reliability, legitimacy or infrastructure preceded uptake or resulted from it; allow reciprocal causation. Psychological explanations need evidence about beliefs or behaviour beyond the adoption outcome itself.

   **Later acceptance:** a reviewer can explain why the cited observation favours one account over another. If both predict it, the claim remains underdetermined. Missing evidence is disconfirming only when that evidence would plausibly survive and be found. This implements the distinction between diagnostic evidence and a compatible narrative discussed in [Collier, 2011, “Process Tracing, Prior Knowledge, and Diagnostic Evidence”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ps-political-science-and-politics/article/understanding-process-tracing/183A057AD6A36783E678CB37440346D1).

4. **D04 — P2, unresolved design choice: domain breadth is specified; comparison selection is not yet operational.**

   [METHODS.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/METHODS.md:4), line 4, deliberately includes varied outcomes. [PLAN.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/PLAN.md:5), lines 5–23, lists promising topics and a genuine subsequent coverage audit. It does not yet say why particular contrasts discriminate mechanisms. More countries or more failures alone would not fix selection bias. Non-European cases could become illustrations of a European periodisation rather than explanations in their own terms; that is a risk to check, not an established defect.

   **Repair:** add a comparison ledger naming the question, selected episodes, useful similarities/differences, evidence comparability, source/language limits and plausible omitted countercases. Include stable niches, continued older practices, non-exposure and abandonment where they test the claim. Record connections between comparison sites: trade, empire, migration and shared standards can make apparent replications parts of one process. Give the cross-domain coverage audit an owner; it should examine military power, resource extraction, maintenance and institutional alternatives where cases make them relevant. No geographic or failure quotas are needed.

   **Later acceptance:** each major comparative conclusion explains its comparison logic and limits. “Slow,” “failed” and “resistant” have a defensible benchmark. Schwartz's critique of Ottoman printing is a useful stress test: she challenges European print history as the expected endpoint and asks what particular presses were established to accomplish. This is a scholarly challenge to investigate, not a replacement master explanation. [Schwartz, 2017, pp. 1–3](https://religionculturesociety.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/s1__schwartz_did-ottoman-sultans-ban-print.pdf).

5. **D05 — P2, present: corroboration and review independence lack an evidence-lineage rule.**

   [METHODS.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/METHODS.md:23), lines 23 and 43, requires corroboration for high confidence and protects reviewer independence through prompts and disconfirmation. It also commendably requires audit denominators and selection methods. But several publications can repeat one account, and separate researchers can retrieve the same canonical interpretation. Bibliographic multiplicity is not independent evidence.

   **Repair:** for decisive and contested claims, track shared underlying records, datasets or inherited assertions. State what corroboration contributes rather than counting links. Select audits for consequence, controversy, access weakness and graph importance, alongside a disclosed check of ordinary claims; do not audit only convenient or already strong examples. Have reviewers reconstruct selected claims from the cited passages and an independently sought rival source. Record the inspected version and close findings against changed artifacts. This can be done by the requested Astra reviewers; changing models is not the remedy.

   **Later acceptance:** the audit distinguishes citation entailment, source reliability and causal adequacy. A repeated assertion cannot acquire high confidence merely through additional citations. Schwartz's tracing of the printing-ban story through later scholarship illustrates this concern; her argument should itself receive source scrutiny. [Schwartz, 2017, pp. 27–28](https://religionculturesociety.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/s1__schwartz_did-ottoman-sultans-ban-print.pdf).

6. **D06 — P2, risk to check: evidence for adoption may not answer the user's question about future human roles.**

   [CHARTER.md](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/CHARTER.md:15), lines 15 and 32, wisely promises conditional implications. The case fields and [domain plan](/Users/szymon/.codex/worktrees/4b39/big_idea/research/technology-adoption-audit/design-snapshot/PLAN.md:8) do not yet assign collection of task changes, livelihood transitions or who could enter new roles. Aggregate productivity, a new occupation and improvement for displaced people are different propositions.

   **Repair:** where evidence permits, record tasks displaced, reorganised or created; paid and unpaid work; skills and entry costs; ownership and bargaining power; transition duration; and whose outcomes are observed. Assign synthesis responsibility for these links. Preserve unknowns rather than requiring labour reconstruction for every ancient case. Future-facing claims should identify the historical mechanism, its boundary conditions, the proposed modern analogue and what would break the analogy.

   **Later acceptance:** no inference from net employment or cheaper goods alone to accessible, desirable roles for displaced individuals. Subgroup evidence matters: Humphries and Schneider's abstract reports hand-spinning evidence on women and children that challenges a high-wage explanation of mechanisation. I inspected the abstract only and did not adjudicate that debate. [Humphries and Schneider, 2019, abstract; first published online 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ehr.12693).

The immediate repair can be a short, versioned addendum to the contribution contract, followed by migration of initial records before synthesis. It need not delay exploratory research or produce a large ontology. Later audits should inspect completed episodes and graph paths for substantive compliance; structural validation alone cannot establish any acceptance condition above.

Questions that remain genuinely unresolved:

- Which comparisons and causal claims will carry the synthesis? Their identity should determine where the deepest source and mechanism audits are spent.
- What languages, specialist scholarship and inaccessible records constrain the first wave, and which claims must consequently remain provisional?
- How will canonical graph nodes preserve locally different technology configurations, episode scopes and contradictory claims when contributions merge?
- Who will own cross-domain connections and occupational-transition evidence, and how will unresolved objections remain visible in the final HTML and data exports?
