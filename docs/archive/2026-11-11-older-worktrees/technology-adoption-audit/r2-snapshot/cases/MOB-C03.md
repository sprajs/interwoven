# MOB-C03 — Indian railways: trade gains, guarantees and labour under colonial ownership

**Period:** 1853–1930; ownership analysis 1874–1912 and guaranteed-security comparison 1880–1908. **Region:** British India; South Asia; Britain (finance). **Domain:** railways; colonial finance; trade; labour.

Distinct studies show trade/income gains, fiscal risk allocation and lower operating costs after state purchases; none is a sufficient statistic for the welfare or consent of all colonial subjects.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MOB/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Locomotive invention occurred outside this bounded case; the question is deployment of a transferred railway system.

**feasibility:** Operational lines from 1853; engineering, capital and recurring labour/fuel supply complemented rails.

**deployment:** Early privately owned companies operated under colonial concession and guarantee arrangements.

**diffusion:** Network expansion and district connection are measured, not universal household access or use.

**scaling:** Growth to a large interregional network accompanied expanding trade; timing and ownership differed across companies.

**non-adoption/niche:** More than 40,000 km of surveyed/planned lines remained unbuilt in Donaldson’s placebo exercise; these are plans, not failed operational services.

**displacement/reversal:** State purchases changed ownership and incentives; this was institutional reversal within rail, not displacement of rail technology.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Colonial steam railway network with regulated companies and later state ownership; three linked observational subepisodes retained separately.

**task:** Transport freight/passengers, connect markets and project state power; finance railway capital and operate services.

**population:** Donaldson: 192 districts’ agricultural outcomes. Bogart/Chaudhary cost study: railway companies and workers. Investor study: holders of listed securities. No pooling of these outcomes.

**interval:** Deployment from 1853; district panel 1870–1930; company panel 1874–1912; guaranteed-security return figure 1880–1908.

**contemporary alternatives:** bullock pack/cart transport; river and coastal shipping; unbuilt railway proposals; public borrowing/ownership versus guaranteed private concessions

**adoption measure:** District connected-to-rail indicator, route network, company ownership and operating costs; no population-wide modal share or voluntary adoption denominator.

**observation limits:** Colonial records reflect administrative priorities; agricultural output per area is not household income; railway wage/salary data unavailable; secondary-market returns omit many other transfers.

## Actors

**chooser:** Colonial Government of India and Secretary of State controlled concessions/placement; company boards made operational choices; users chose shipments under available constraints.

**payer:** British investors supplied much early capital; Indian tax revenues covered guarantee shortfalls; users paid fares/freight; state purchased companies.

**operator maintainer:** Indian and European railway employees; racial segmentation of occupations reported in the cost study.

**affected nonchooser:** Taxpayers, railway workers, route-adjacent households and unconnected regions; land losers are not directly sampled here.

**ability to refuse:** No evidence of colonial subjects consenting to guarantees or route plans. Users’ choice among transport modes differs from taxpayers’ exposure; employee exit/refusal and land-acquisition resistance are not directly measured.

## Labour

**tasks:** Construction, track maintenance, driving, station operation, handling and administration; ownership change reduced employment relative to comparison paths.

**entry barriers:** Study describes Europeans concentrated in more highly paid technical/managerial jobs and Indians in lower-skill work; individual recruitment and training pathways not reconstructed.

**bargaining ownership:** Colonial government sought treasury returns and did not depend on workers’ votes; guarantees weakened some private cost incentives.

**transition:** Company-panel employment change over years around takeovers; no linked displaced-worker cohort or destination-occupation data.

**distribution:** Operating savings and agricultural gains are observed at different units. Direct wages unavailable; no average person-level gain can be inferred from them.

**evidence limit:** Cannot determine household net welfare, fiscal opportunity costs, land compensation, individual unemployment duration or overall colonial extraction.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: government guarantees; military planning; trade integration; contractual ownership change; fiscal incentives; labour-cost reduction

Aggregate agricultural gains do not establish worker gains or refute all colonial extraction. Moderate financial returns do not prove absence of extraction through other channels.

## Evidence links

- [MOB-K07](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mob-k07): Early private Indian railway expansion combined British capital with colonial guarantees, while government line-placement priorities included military control; observed construction was not simply a market vote by passengers or farmers.
- [MOB-K08](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mob-k08): Donaldson’s published study finds lower trade costs and higher trade, and estimates a 0.164 log-point rise in real agricultural income per unit area following rail access in its district panel; the paper summarizes the income effect as about 16%.
- [MOB-K09](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mob-k09): Bogart and Chaudhary find lower working expenses after colonial state takeovers of Indian railways, with employment reductions supporting a labour-cost channel; they do not directly observe railway wages.
- [MOB-K10](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mob-k10): The April 2019 accepted manuscript by Bogart and Chaudhary estimates average annual total returns of 3.7% on Indian guaranteed railway securities for 1880–1908 and finds no average abnormal returns in the three years before state purchases.
