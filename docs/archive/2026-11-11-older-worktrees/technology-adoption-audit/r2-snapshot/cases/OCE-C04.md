# OCE-C04 — Kuk Phase 3 drainage: variable use and unresolved abandonment

**Period:** c.4350–2500 cal BP Phase 3; c.2000 cal BP Phase 4 resumption. **Region:** Wahgi Valley, Papua New Guinea; comparator highland valleys. **Domain:** wetland cultivation; drainage; field systems.

Ditch networks at Kuk extend earlier cultivation configurations but are neither a linear intensification curve nor an archaeological transcript of social organization. Wetland use ceased before a later configuration emerged; reasons remain unresolved.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/OCE/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Origins and immediate motivations not established; chronology alone is not proof of independent invention.

**deployment:** Ditch networks and maintenance traces demonstrate local modification for cultivation, interpreted jointly with environmental evidence.

**diffusion:** Other valleys have dated ditches but thinner sampling; no direction or rate of transfer inferred.

**non-adoption/niche:** Wetter northern portions appear undrained; dryland gardening was another option.

**displacement/reversal:** Several-hundred-year Phase 3–4 gap before later grid systems; no demonstrated total knowledge loss.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Phase 3 rectilinear, dendritic and other ditch arrangements at Kuk wetland margins; Phase 2 mounds and Phase 4 grids are distinct comparators.

**task:** Drain cultivation surfaces; possible boundary/risk functions remain hypotheses.

**population:** Users/builders of excavated Kuk fields, population and household counts unknown; other valley sites are limited comparators.

**interval:** Phase 3 starts c.4350–3980 cal BP; later stage c.2700–2500 years ago; Phase 4 begins around 2000 years ago. Dates approximate, BP convention anchored to 1950 where calibrated.

**contemporary alternatives:** Dryland cultivation on neighboring slopes; Earlier mound-based wetland cultivation; Hunting/gathering and mixed land-use portfolios, quantities unknown; Leaving excessively wet locations uncultivated

**adoption measure:** Dated stratigraphic features, plan extent and different networks; no operator, hectare-use or production denominator through time.

**observation limits:** Wetland channels preserve differently from dryland practice; resolution cannot establish every continuous interval or simultaneous regional abandonment.

## Actors

**chooser:** Ancient individuals/groups unknown; do not infer chiefs or state command from ditch scale.

**payer:** Labor/material providers unknown; manual earthmoving evident but contribution rules unrecorded.

**operator maintainer:** Diggers and cultivators; maintenance represented by tools/features, demographic identities unknown.

**affected nonchooser:** Other water/land users plausibly affected, but impact and status not recoverable from selected sources.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown: no direct evidence of work obligations, displacement or voluntary abandonment for this episode.

## Labour

**tasks:** Excavation, cleaning/repair, planting, harvesting and fallowing; labor quantities and paid/unpaid categories cannot be reconstructed reliably.

**entry barriers:** Land access and cultivation/drainage skills plausible requirements; actual allocation, exclusion and training unknown.

**bargaining ownership:** Ditches may mark tenure, but control of land and output remains an interpretation rather than documented rights.

**transition:** Long material sequence with discontinuity; no individual or cohort career evidence.

**distribution:** No defensible household, gender or status distribution of gains.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer egalitarianism, coerced labor, occupational specialization or livelihood improvement from landscape investment.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: hydrology; maintenance labor; possible plot demarcation; portfolio of wetland/dryland tasks; contested abandonment mechanisms

Hydrological, agronomic and social explanations can fit the same surviving sequence; modern property analogies cannot fill absent ancient institutional data.

## Evidence links

- [OCE-K13](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k13): Kuk’s published sequence differentiates mounded cultivation around 6,950–6,440 cal BP from ditch cultivation beginning around 4,350–3,980 cal BP; a later account maps multiple, locally varying Phase 3 ditch configurations.
- [OCE-K14](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k14): Kuk Phase 3 ditches plausibly combined drainage with social functions such as plot marking, while wetland use coexisted with nearby dryland cultivation; the sources cannot separate hydrological, tenure and risk-management drivers.
- [OCE-K15](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k15): The Phase 3–4 sequence at Kuk contains a several-hundred-year gap in wetland cultivation, while the reason remains unresolved among hydrological change, dryland alternatives and social disruption; it does not demonstrate disappearance of cultivation knowledge.
