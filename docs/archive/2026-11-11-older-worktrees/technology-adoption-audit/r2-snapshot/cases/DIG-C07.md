# DIG-C07 — Microsoft phone hardware: sales coexist with strategic retrenchment

**Period:** FY 2014–FY 2015. **Region:** Worldwide Microsoft operations. **Domain:** smartphone platforms; consumer hardware; corporate strategy.

An already deployed phone business had large unit sales while expected business value was written down; platform coordination remains a plausible, unisolated explanation.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/DIG/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Not evaluated.

**feasibility:** Operational Lumia and non-Lumia products sold.

**deployment:** 36.8 million Lumia and 126.8 million non-Lumia sales FY 2015.

**diffusion:** Sales are a flow; installed base and active use not measured here.

**scaling:** Full-year revenue includes acquired business, not comparable organic growth.

**non-adoption/niche:** Narrower strategic focus announced; market-specific non-adoption unmeasured.

**displacement/reversal:** 2015 impairment/retrenchment, without an inferred terminal platform date.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Post-acquisition Microsoft Phone Hardware portfolio including Lumia and other phones; company platform strategy.

**task:** Sell communication/computing devices sustainably and build a common application ecosystem.

**population:** Company worldwide sales and accounts; consumer population denominator unavailable.

**interval:** FY 2015 with partial post-acquisition FY 2014 comparator; acquisition 25 Apr 2014.

**contemporary alternatives:** Android/iOS phones as broad platform alternatives named in company business context; Other non-Lumia phone portfolio; Existing PC/tablet services; not identical communication substitutes

**adoption measure:** FY 2015 reported unit sales plus $7.5 billion impairment and management retrenchment statement; not active users or retention.

**observation limits:** Partial FY 2014 comparator; no consistent global smartphone market denominator, app catalogue comparison or carrier-incentive data.

## Actors

**chooser:** Microsoft executives and developers decide investment; distributors and buyers choose stocking/use; their decisions not separately observed.

**payer:** Corporate capital and purchasers; financing/subsidy allocations unknown.

**operator maintainer:** Microsoft and partner manufacturing/software/channel workers; product maintenance conditions not inspected.

**affected nonchooser:** Employees facing restructuring and existing users dependent on ecosystem continuity.

**ability to refuse:** Company portfolio changes are not selected by affected employees/users; individual bargaining/refusal evidence absent.

## Labour

**tasks:** Engineering, manufacturing, distribution and support reorganised; report records restructuring expenses including severance but no causal net-employment effect.

**entry barriers:** Skills, assets, distribution access and developer audience; individual entry barriers not directly researched.

**bargaining ownership:** Company controls portfolio and acquisition capital; worker bargaining outcomes not described.

**transition:** Accounting/strategy episode FY 2014–2015; no longitudinal worker destination/retraining evidence.

**distribution:** Severance/restructuring costs reported; distribution of gains/losses across workers or countries unknown.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer an occupational decline or net social loss solely from corporate impairments.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Possible app/developer/distributor coordination; Acquisition valuation and execution as strong rivals; Total business economics differ from device feasibility

Large sales demonstrate users; an impairment is not a user-preference experiment or proof of specific ecosystem causality.

## Evidence links

- [DIG-K22](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k22): Microsoft reported 36.8 million Lumia and 126.8 million other non-Lumia phone sales in FY 2015 alongside $7.5 billion phone-business impairments and strategic retrenchment; FY 2014 sales covered only the post-acquisition period.
- [DIG-K23](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k23): Software/content coordination is a plausible contributor to the Microsoft phone episode, but its 2015 stated unified-application strategy does not establish that app shortages caused the retrenchment.
