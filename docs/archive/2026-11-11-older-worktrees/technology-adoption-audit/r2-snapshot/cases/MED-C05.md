# MED-C05 — Penicillin from research material to industrial supply

**Period:** 1941–1945. **Region:** Britain; United States; Peoria; Brooklyn. **Domain:** health; pharmaceutical production; industrial biotechnology.

Production complements and coordination help explain the gap between a promising drug and usable supply.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MED/report.md).

## Stages

**feasibility:** Clinical promise and stable material were antecedents; this case concerns industrial reproducibility.

**deployment:** Laboratory-to-company process transfer and plant investment.

**scaling:** Media, strain, tank, extraction and sterile finishing improvements enabled much larger supply.

**diffusion:** Clinical population coverage and allocation not quantified here.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Specified US fermentation and downstream-processing route linked to British penicillin research.

**task:** Produce usable quantities for wartime treatment.

**population:** NRRL collaborators and participating manufacturers, with Pfizer process detail; not all producers or recipients.

**interval:** 1941–1945.

**contemporary alternatives:** Surface/flask fermentation expanded in parallel; Other antibacterial treatments, with clinical substitutability not assessed; Potential alternative industrial routes not evaluated

**adoption measure:** Qualitative process and plant scaling; no verified common production series or treatment-population denominator.

**observation limits:** Company/public-laboratory retrospectives; no batch logs, primary contracts or allocation dataset inspected.

## Actors

**chooser:** Research teams, government coordinators and company management chose process and capital commitments.

**payer:** Public research resources and company investment; exact procurement guarantees and shares unknown.

**operator maintainer:** Microbiologists, chemical engineers, plant operators and sterile-processing workers.

**affected nonchooser:** Patients facing scarce supply and workers whose capacity was diverted from other products.

**ability to refuse:** Firms made investments under wartime pressures; precise contracting autonomy unknown. Patients could not choose unavailable supply.

## Labour

**tasks:** Culture development, fermentation control, extraction, purification and sterile finishing; transfer of existing industrial expertise.

**entry barriers:** Laboratories, capital, technical skill and controlled production environments.

**bargaining ownership:** Firm/state authority over production; patents, wage bargaining and distribution of returns not audited.

**transition:** Rapid wartime scaling within several years; individual retraining and displacement unknown.

**distribution:** Scarce supply and Allied wartime priorities visible, but recipient-class or worker outcomes unquantified.

**evidence limit:** No evidence-based net job count, wage effect or equitable-access estimate.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: yield bottlenecks; complementary industrial knowledge; capital opportunity cost; public-private coordination

Commemorative sources may overstate own institutions; counterfactual production routes and procurement effects remain unresolved.
