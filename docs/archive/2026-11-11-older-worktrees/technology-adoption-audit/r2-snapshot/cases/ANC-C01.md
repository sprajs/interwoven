# ANC-C01 — Hero's steam-driven rotating sphere and its uncertain purpose

**Period:** Roman-period textual tradition, conventionally c. first century CE but exact authorial date uncertain; 1851/2023 reception used only to interpret the evidence. **Region:** Alexandria, Egypt (Roman-period intellectual context). **Domain:** mechanics; steam; technical knowledge.

The transmitted device specifies rotation. Productive deployment and economic comparisons are absent from the inspected passage.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/ANC/report.md).

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Heated cauldron feeding a pivoted sphere with opposing bent outlets, as described in Pneumatica II.11.

**task:** Visible rotation; intellectual, demonstrative or performative purpose contested.

**population:** Authorial/readership tradition; historical builders and users are not identified.

**interval:** Ancient textual episode only; subsequent editorial reception is a separate interpretive subepisode.

**contemporary alternatives:** Other mechanical demonstrations for teaching/display; productive water or muscle power only if the task is instead grinding or pumping (analytical comparison, not a documented purchaser choice).

**adoption measure:** One transmitted technical description; no adoption denominator or prevalence window.

**observation limits:** No original apparatus, user log, or output series; later translation and images mediate the record.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Purpose determines the relevant performance criterion.; Moving from rotation to productive service requires additional evidence about loads, duration and buyers.

Other Heronian devices are practical; absence of one application in a transmitted text is weak evidence about all ancient uses.
