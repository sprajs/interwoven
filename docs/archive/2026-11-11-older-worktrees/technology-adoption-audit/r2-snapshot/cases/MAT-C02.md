# MAT-C02 — Southern Levant bronze–iron transition without a single scarcity or hardness trigger

**Period:** Approximately 1200–800 BCE, with earlier prestige-object background. **Region:** Southwest Asia; southern Levant. **Domain:** iron; bronze; craft organisation.

Iron dominance in sampled utilitarian metal assemblages lagged initial use and coincided with production changes; bronze survived.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/MAT/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Local first invention is not established; earlier iron objects and possible imported knowledge exist.

**feasibility:** Metallography and workshop debris establish production capability; different smelting/smithing stages must be separated.

**deployment:** Iron I tools and weapons occur before the Iron IIA expansion.

**diffusion:** Changing shares of classified excavated metal objects; no household denominator.

**scaling:** Systematic Iron IIA production with urban smelting/forging is supported; annual tonnage unknown.

**non-adoption/niche:** Earlier iron remains limited beside a viable bronze industry.

**displacement/reversal:** Iron gains utilitarian predominance; bronze production continues.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Bloomery-derived iron objects and urban smelting/forging compared with bronze production in surveyed southern Levant sites

**task:** Tools, weapons, ornaments, and supplying metalwork; object categories do not identify every actual use

**population:** Excavated metal assemblages and workshops in surveyed settlements, with some funerary contexts; actual buyer/household population unknown

**interval:** Approximately 1200–800 BCE, with earlier prestige-object background

**contemporary alternatives:** Tin-bronze objects and continuing bronze workshops are observed alternatives; Imported iron objects or semi-products could substitute for local smelting; not established for every phase; Stone/wood tools may be task alternatives but were not counted in the metal denominator

**adoption measure:** Changes in shares of classified metal tools/weapons and workshop evidence across Iron I/IIA; no population adoption denominator or price series.

**observation limits:** Corrosion, recycling and deposition differ by material. Manufacturing location does not determine user identity or access.

## Actors

**chooser:** Metalworkers and buyers/administrations are plausible decision-makers; workshop contexts support involvement but not exact decision rights.

**payer:** Unknown; administration-associated spaces do not identify who paid or supplied ore/fuel.

**operator maintainer:** Smelters, smiths and furnace workers; repair practitioners likely relevant but local roles not established.

**affected nonchooser:** Ore/fuel suppliers and households using or lacking metal goods; group outcomes not measured.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown; no direct contracts, mandates or worker testimony inspected.

## Labour

**tasks:** Smelting and forging may reorganise existing metalwork; bronze continues. Job displacement not established.

**entry barriers:** Technical skill, furnaces, fuel and material access; formal status or entry rules unknown.

**bargaining ownership:** Administrative association is suggestive, not proof of property rights or rent extraction.

**transition:** Archaeological phase sequence spans centuries; no worker cohort histories.

**distribution:** No wage, household-consumption or occupational distribution measures.

**evidence limit:** Cannot infer smith redundancy, universal labour saving or improved livelihood from iron dominance.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: craft adaptation; administrative support hypothesis; ore supply; tacit smithing skill; recycling and preservation filters

Tin composition evidence cannot measure prices; workshop location is not proof of state ownership; corrosion obscures treatment.

## Evidence links

- [MAT-K07](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k07): Iron work associated with older bronze-working areas and public architecture makes local craft adaptation and administrative support plausible contributors to systematic production.
- [MAT-K23](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#mat-k23): Recycling and composite manufacture complicate inferences from surviving iron objects to initial production, replacement rates or material scarcity.
