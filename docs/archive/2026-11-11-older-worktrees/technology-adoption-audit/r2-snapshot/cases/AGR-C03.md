# AGR-C03 — Jahaly-Pacharr irrigation: rice output, rights and labour in The Gambia

**Period:** 1984–1990 project use, with prior-rights context and 1983–1984 fieldwork. **Region:** Jahaly-Pacharr; The Gambia; West Africa. **Domain:** agriculture; irrigation; gender and labour; contract farming.

A technical and credit package expanded rice opportunities, while project land reclassification and household authority altered who controlled returns. Early income benefits coexist with crop substitution, uneven individual incentives and later operating problems.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/AGR/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** The project deploys existing pumps, land preparation, improved rice and credit; it is not an invention-priority case.

**feasibility:** Irrigated rice production is demonstrated, conditional on water control, inputs, machinery and labour calendars.

**deployment:** Project participants cultivate irrigated plots from 1984; fieldwork/evaluation describes households and farmers in participating and comparison villages.

**diffusion:** No complete regional participation/time-series denominator is reconstructed. Nominal registration is not control or actual labour supplied.

**scaling:** Continued operations involve fuel, parts, input prices, credit and marketing alongside labour mobilisation.

**non-adoption:** Some women with alternatives resist uncompensated obligations or negotiate wages/shares; low utilisation can also reflect competing rainfed returns.

**displacement:** Labour shifts between irrigated and other crops and toward paid groups; no simple complete replacement of older rice systems is established.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Pumped irrigation, water control, mechanical land preparation, short-duration rice, purchased inputs, credit and prescribed cultivation schedules, embedded in local household/land institutions.

**task:** Raise rice output and income; women and household heads may separately seek control over crop returns and labour time.

**population:** Carney's fieldwork and follow-ups in project communities, and IFPRI's 200-household/about-900-farmer survey in ten villages. These are related project populations but not a reconciled panel of identical people and years.

**interval:** Early 1984–1986 evaluation and field follow-ups through 1990, with pre-project cultivation-rights context.

**contemporary alternatives:** Existing swamp, tidal and rainfed rice; Upland crops and independently controlled plots; Paid labour groups or negotiated family labour; Different seasonal allocation when rainfall is favourable

**adoption measure:** Project access, plot/crop production, labour participation and qualitative rights/control reports. Plot registration is not effective ownership; yields alone are not household welfare.

**observation limits:** Largely observational comparisons with different study dates and purposes; Carney cites some IFPRI results, so all findings are not independent corroboration. Input/price/repair changes overlap rights conflicts.

## Actors

**chooser:** Donors and project managers choose the package/schedules; household heads, women cultivators and labour groups negotiate or contest land and work decisions.

**payer:** Donor/project resources and credit support infrastructure/inputs; cultivators and households bear repayment, labour and operating costs, distributed unevenly.

**operator maintainer:** Women perform much rice work alongside household/project labour arrangements; machinery/pumps require operating and repair personnel. Paid groups coexist with uncompensated family obligations.

**affected nonchooser:** Women without matching crop control, other household workers, children affected by care/nutrition, and producers whose other crops lose labour.

**ability to refuse:** Observed labour withdrawal, renegotiation and wage groups demonstrate some alternatives, especially with independent plots or political connections. Others face household obligations and weaker outside options; formal registration does not prove consent.

## Labour

**tasks:** Rice preparation, planting, weeding and harvesting are reorganised around tighter water/crop schedules; women adapt groups toward paid work, and other crops lose labour.

**entry barriers:** Access to irrigated plots, credit, inputs and local authority; independent upland rights and political connections shape alternatives and bargaining.

**bargaining ownership:** Reclassification can transfer control to household heads despite women being registered. Wages, crop shares and independent plots alter who captures returns.

**transition:** Fieldwork spans initial rollout and later follow-ups through 1990; it is not a complete worker panel or estimate of time needed to replace lost income.

**distribution:** Household income gains can coincide with weaker individual control and greater maternal work burdens. Ethnic/community and household contrasts matter; averages do not identify each woman's gain.

**evidence limit:** No credible single percentage of project decline is assigned to labour resistance, and household totals cannot establish individual welfare or consent.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: control of returns; household bargaining; outside options; competing crop calendars; maintenance and supply systems; income versus nutrition pathways

IFPRI reports substantial early production/income benefits; Carney's later problems do not erase them. Neither observational design fully separates project selection, rights, operational failures and reallocation.

## Evidence links

- [AGR-K07](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k07): Carney documents Jahaly-Pacharr land registered to women being reclassified as household rice fields under male control; nominal allocation and the irrigation package did not fix who controlled output and labour.
- [AGR-K08](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k08): Carney links women's withdrawal from uncompensated project labour and their use of paid labour groups to control of returns and available crop alternatives, while documenting concurrent repair, input-price, diesel and marketing problems.
- [AGR-K09](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k09): The IFPRI evaluation associates project access with production and income gains but reports substitution away from other crops and nutrition pathways involving illness, water and maternal work; lower irrigation yields can coexist with better household allocation.
- [AGR-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k24): Across these selected cases, testing a device or plot is often insufficient to evaluate the larger system that must operate, and gains in adoption, effort or output do not alone establish who benefits or whether welfare improves.
