# AGR-C01 — Raised fields in the Titicaca basin: ancient use and modern rehabilitation

**Period:** Ancient local use from approximately three millennia before 1992; separate rehabilitation episode, 1980s–2000. **Region:** Lake Titicaca basin; Peru; Bolivia; Andes. **Domain:** agriculture; water management; landscape engineering.

Raised fields altered water and crop microclimates. Ancient archaeological persistence and modern reconstructed-plot results do not prove universal labour superiority or durable revival; complete rotations and local institutions change the comparison.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/AGR/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No precise invention or single origin assigned. Erickson's local chronology indicates early use roughly three millennia before his 1992 study.

**feasibility:** Twentieth-century experimental blocks demonstrate operating configurations and local thermal effects; ancient conditions are inferred rather than reproduced exactly.

**deployment:** Archaeological earthworks and rebuilding establish ancient deployment; promoted projects rebuilt fields from the 1980s.

**diffusion:** Neither ancient simultaneous area nor modern participating-household share is established here. Reconstruction totals are not a denominator of sustained use.

**scaling:** A productive experimental plot does not measure complete-rotation labour, maintenance or organisation across a landscape.

**non-adoption:** Bandy reports disappointing persistence in specified revived fields, alongside other continuing reconstruction.

**displacement:** Ancient disuse and twentieth-century project abandonment are separate events. No single climatic, political or labour explanation is assigned to both.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: ancient elevated planting platforms with intervening canals, varying locally. B: experimental and promoted reconstructions beginning in the 1980s, with modern crops, labour and project support.

**task:** Produce crops in a high-altitude lakeshore environment while managing water, frost exposure and seasonal labour; the relative importance of these goals is contested.

**population:** A: cultivators inferred from northern-basin archaeology; no enumerated ancient population. B: experimental blocks and named rehabilitation programmes reviewed by Erickson and Bandy; Swartley's revisited fields are a limited subset.

**interval:** A: long ancient sequence, with early local use approximately three millennia before 1992 and no uniform basin-wide endpoints. B: research/revival from the 1980s, revisits in 1996–1997 and reconstruction reports through 2000.

**contemporary alternatives:** Dryland potato cultivation and rotations with fallow; Other water/land management suited to local sites; Modern households' alternative crops and livelihoods; exact menus vary and are incompletely observed

**adoption measure:** A: surviving field morphology and excavated rebuilding are deployment proxies, not a diffusion rate. B: local reports of reconstruction and cessation; no common denominator of sustained users or maintained area.

**observation limits:** Preservation and dated excavation are uneven; experimental blocks are small. Modern labour comparisons use heterogeneous studies; important programme originals were not acquired. The subepisodes cannot be treated as one longitudinal experiment.

## Actors

**chooser:** A: ancient household/community/elite decision authority remains uncertain. B: project promoters and cultivators both shape construction and continued cultivation; their decisions need not share objectives.

**payer:** A: labour and resource contributions are archaeologically uncertain. B: projects supply some resources while participating cultivators bear labour and maintenance; precise subsidy incidence differs by programme and is not fully reconstructed.

**operator maintainer:** Cultivators build/repair platforms and canals and perform cropping. Ancient division of work and modern paid versus unpaid shares are not adequately measured.

**affected nonchooser:** Household members and neighbours sharing land, water and labour demands; their individual consent and benefits are not recoverable from the main measures.

**ability to refuse:** Ancient compulsion versus voluntary cooperation is unresolved. Modern cessation demonstrates an ability to cease use in observed projects, but does not reveal every participant's constraints or all entry conditions.

## Labour

**tasks:** Earthmoving, canal/platform maintenance and crop work; a larger initial harvest may involve more construction or recurring work than the dryland comparator.

**entry barriers:** Access to suitable land/water, labour at the required season and local agronomic/maintenance knowledge; programme access adds a modern institutional barrier.

**bargaining ownership:** Ancient control and political mobilisation are disputed. Modern project totals do not identify who owns improvements or captures each household member's return.

**transition:** Bandy reviews revival and later cessations; no complete person-level livelihood trajectory or common duration-to-exit distribution is available.

**distribution:** Per-hectare and per-workday outcomes can rank systems differently. Gender, status and household distributions are insufficiently observed for aggregate claims.

**evidence limit:** Experimental yields and schematic labour accounting do not estimate employment creation, wage effects or welfare for ancient or modern populations.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: drainage and local microclimate; land versus labour productivity; rotation and maintenance accounting; seasonal labour opportunity cost; organisation of reconstruction

Some raised-field comparisons are favourable and communities differ. Bandy's critique inherits earlier measurements and does not supply a basin-wide abandonment census or prove ancient farmers inefficient.

## Evidence links

- [AGR-K01](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k01): Erickson's northern Titicaca investigation documents an engineered raised-field landscape and reconstructed blocks with warmer, shorter frost exposure on two reported frost nights; it does not measure an ancient basin-wide frost advantage.
- [AGR-K02](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k02): Bandy's reassessment reports abandonment in specified 1980s–1990s raised-field rehabilitation programmes while also recording reconstruction elsewhere through 2000; the evidence does not supply a basin-wide abandonment rate.
- [AGR-K03](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k03): Bandy challenges claims of universally superior raised-field labour productivity by incorporating crop rotation, fallow and maintenance labour; whether raised fields outperform dryland alternatives depends on the comparison and accounting assumptions.
- [AGR-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k24): Across these selected cases, testing a device or plot is often insufficient to evaluate the larger system that must operate, and gains in adoption, effort or output do not alone establish who benefits or whether welfare improves.
