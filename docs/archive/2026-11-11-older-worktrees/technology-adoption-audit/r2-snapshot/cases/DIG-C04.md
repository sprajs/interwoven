# DIG-C04 — India: mobile data expansion and unequal educational access

**Period:** 2014–2024; education comparison 2018–2021. **Region:** India; Rural India. **Domain:** telecommunications; remote education.

Falling nominal revenue per GB accompanies greater service spending; household phone availability still fails to imply child access for study.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/DIG/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Outside episode.

**feasibility:** Mobile internet and smartphone learning were available but require complements not captured by subscriptions.

**deployment:** Telecom subscription/revenue returns and household smartphone availability.

**diffusion:** ASER restricted 2018–2021 availability comparison; rural/urban subscription density 2024.

**scaling:** Large fall in average revenue/GB, without a causal supply explanation.

**non-adoption/niche:** Children in phone-owning homes can lack study access; rural subscription density lower.

**displacement/reversal:** No claim that physical schooling or basic phones were displaced.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** National wireless data service and subscription accounts; separate rural household smartphone access for education during pandemic disruption.

**task:** Access internet services; children obtain study materials or participate remotely.

**population:** Telecom subscriptions/data subscribers, not unique people; ASER enrolled rural children 5–16 in a phone-reachable sampling frame.

**interval:** Calendar 2014 vs FY 2023–24 accounts; March 2024 density; 2018–2021 ASER comparison.

**contemporary alternatives:** Basic phone/SMS or voice support; Shared smartphone; Printed study materials and in-person teaching when available; Computer/tablet, availability not established for all sampled children

**adoption measure:** Official nominal revenue per GB and per-data-subscriber/month; subscriptions per 100 inhabitants. ASER smartphone-at-home and conditional study-access shares, with no-phone households excluded from 2018 comparator.

**observation limits:** Do not infer unique use from subscriptions; survey mode/reachability differs; children’s access is not measured learning gain.

## Actors

**chooser:** Household adults decide acquisition/allocation; telecom providers decide offers; schools shape delivery. Individual decisions not directly reconstructed.

**payer:** Households, service providers and educational institutions; payment/incidence data unavailable.

**operator maintainer:** Network operators; household device managers; teachers and caregivers supporting access. Unpaid burden not quantified in selected tables.

**affected nonchooser:** Children who neither purchase nor control phones; households excluded from telephone sampling.

**ability to refuse:** Children cannot necessarily control use of household phones; school closure constrains in-person alternatives, but individual compulsion is not measured.

## Labour

**tasks:** Teachers’ delivery and caregivers’ support potentially reorganised; study-access allocation documented, not hours of unpaid work.

**entry barriers:** Device/control, connectivity, language and literacy require separate measures; no labour-market entry estimates.

**bargaining ownership:** Household ownership and provider service terms can constrain pupils; intra-household bargaining not identified.

**transition:** 2018–2021 availability contrast, not a longitudinal occupational transition.

**distribution:** ASER reports unequal access by grade and parental education; neither income effect nor causal learning distribution established.

**evidence limit:** Do not translate telecom scale or household smartphone availability into school achievement, employment or equal opportunity.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Total affordability differs from per-GB prices; Household resource control and scheduling; Teacher/content/language/coverage/energy complements remain candidates

Lower unit revenue coexists with higher per-subscriber monthly revenue; phone survey excludes important non-exposure.

## Evidence links

- [DIG-K10](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k10): India’s official compilation reports nominal average wireless-data revenue realisation falling from ₹268.97/GB in calendar 2014 to ₹9.12/GB in FY 2023–24, while monthly data revenue per data subscriber rose from ₹71.25 to ₹211.36.
- [DIG-K11](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k11): At March 2024 the official Indian compilation recorded 111.75 urban and 44.16 rural internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants.
- [DIG-K12](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k12): ASER’s rural enrolled-child comparison reports a smartphone at home for 36.5% in 2018 and 67.6% in 2021; the 2018 comparison excludes households without mobile phones to match telephone-survey coverage more closely.
- [DIG-K13](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k13): Among ASER 2021 enrolled children with a smartphone at home, 26.1% had no access to it for studies and 27.0% had access at all times.
- [DIG-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#dig-k24): Pocket internet, performance, ergonomics and cost cannot be causally ranked from these observations; the claim that most laptops cost less than most smartphones is unestablished by this source set.
