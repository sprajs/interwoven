# ANC-C04 — Early southeast Arabian falaj with an Iranian textual contrast

**Period:** Arabian first millennium BCE; Iranian channels described for a late third-century BCE campaign; later revival mentioned only as a contrast. **Region:** Southeast Arabia, especially al-Madam and Al Ain (present UAE); Northern Iranian plateau/Parthia (Polybius contrast). **Domain:** groundwater; irrigation; settlement; water rights.

Local galleries show adaptation and limits. A separate Iranian narrative reports a long benefit horizon, without establishing a shared origin or universal governance system.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/ANC/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Multiple-origin and diffusion hypotheses remain contested.

**feasibility:** Groundwater interception plus suitable gradient differs from mere underground conveyance.

**deployment:** Excavated Arabian installations associated with settlements; Iranian account is textual.

**diffusion:** No universal direction or adoption denominator established.

**scaling:** Regional settlement associations are not a quantified output expansion.

**non-adoption/niche:** Conditions and source depths vary; wells remain a named alternative in the synthesis.

**displacement/reversal:** Deepening and later silting/abandonment at al-Madam; later deep galleries are not assumed a direct continuous lineage.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: early Arabian shafts-and-gallery water catchment, with al-Madam alteration sequence. B: northern Iranian underground channels in Polybius, water source not specified.

**task:** Bring water to settlement/cultivation; in Polybius also water access on a military route.

**population:** A: excavated settlements' users. B: people reported as beneficiaries of earlier Persian cultivation grants; no census.

**interval:** Distinct regional episodes within the first millennium BCE.

**contemporary alternatives:** Wells in the Arabian record.; Surface-water channels where an appropriate source exists; unavailable at some sites rather than demonstrably rejected.

**adoption measure:** Archaeological occurrence and local relative sequence, plus one narrative report. No number of eligible settlements or diffusion rate.

**observation limits:** Many galleries undated or repeatedly altered; collecting and conveying functions can be confused; written and excavated populations differ.

## Actors

**chooser:** Arabian builders and allocation authorities incompletely identified; Persian rulers reported as grantors in a separate literary story.

**payer:** Arabian financing unknown; Polybius reports substantial expense by builders but gives no accounts.

**operator maintainer:** Diggers, cleaners and water users required; named people, remuneration and labour status unknown in these ancient episodes.

**affected nonchooser:** Dependent cultivators and downstream/competing users are plausible affected groups, not measured participants.

**ability to refuse:** Unknown: later communal water rules cannot be projected onto first-millennium BCE settlements.

## Labour

**tasks:** Excavation, depth adjustment, clearing and water distribution; reduced recurring lift at a suitable gravity outlet is a physical inference, not measured net labour saving.

**entry barriers:** Local water-location, gradient-setting and excavation knowledge; access rights and capital may matter but are not quantified.

**bargaining ownership:** Polybius's cultivation right is reported; actual title, inheritance conditions and division of benefits unknown.

**transition:** Physical adaptation sequence known through synthesis; no individual livelihood transitions.

**distribution:** No ancient distribution of work, water, land or surplus by group established.

**evidence limit:** Do not infer egalitarian community organisation, free work or indefinite sustainability from a gravity-fed design.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: Groundwater and gradient constrain feasible operation.; Maintenance and local hydrology interact.; Durable benefit rights could encourage investment, but that causal reading is untested.

Poor dating and excavation in Iran can distort apparent priority; similar gallery forms fit diffusion plus adaptation as well as independent innovation.

## Evidence links

- [ANC-K12](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k12): Excavated early Arabian galleries complicate a single Iranian origin narrative; Boucharlat's polycentric account remains a hypothesis under uneven dating and disputed water-source identifications.
- [ANC-K13](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k13): The al-Madam sequence of deepening and later silting is consistent with declining water availability followed by discontinued maintenance, but does not isolate environmental change from settlement reorganisation.
- [ANC-K14](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k14): Polybius reports that earlier Persian rulers offered five generations of cultivation rights for bringing water to unirrigated land; the inspected translation does not explicitly state a tax exemption.
- [ANC-K15](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k15): A gravity-fed groundwater gallery can supply its outlet only when water is available at a compatible elevation and the channel remains passable; knowing the technique alone does not establish local feasibility.
- [ANC-K22](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#anc-k22): These cases show why cessation of an installation, substitution of its service, and loss of technical knowledge must be tested separately; survival of a named system likewise does not establish unchanged operation.
