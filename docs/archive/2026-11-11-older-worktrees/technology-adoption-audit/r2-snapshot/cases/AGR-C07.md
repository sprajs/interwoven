# AGR-C07 — Cooking systems: Odisha biomass stoves and a distinct supported LPG trial

**Period:** Odisha: starting 2006–2007, up to four-year follow-up; HAPIN: randomisation 2018–2020 and pregnancy follow-up. **Region:** Odisha; India; Guatemala; Peru; Rwanda. **Domain:** household technology; cooking; health; fuel supply.

The Odisha programme's initial exposure gain fades as real cooking and maintenance mediate performance. HAPIN achieves high adherence and lower exposure under free ongoing support but no statistically significant birth-weight increase, distinguishing equipment, use, exposure and health links.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/AGR/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** Neither study establishes an invention first; each tests a specified delivered configuration.

**feasibility:** Laboratory promise is distinct from long-run programme performance. Odisha measures real use over years; HAPIN supplies equipment plus recurring fuel and support.

**deployment:** Odisha uses phased random assignment across 44 villages/2,651 households. HAPIN randomises eligible pregnant women at selected four-country sites.

**diffusion:** Neither supported trial establishes market diffusion. Older-stove use persists in Odisha; HAPIN reports high adherence during provision.

**scaling:** Maintenance, delivery and recurring fuel costs are system components; the trials do not evaluate universal affordable rollout.

**non-adoption:** Odisha ownership does not guarantee consistent use or repair; constraints cannot be inferred as ignorance. HAPIN's free-fuel context limits willingness-to-pay inference.

**displacement:** Mixed use/stacking differs from effective biomass displacement. HAPIN's lower exposure does not imply an effect on every health endpoint.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** A: inexpensive improved biomass stove with training and household responsibility for operation/maintenance in Odisha. B: LPG stove plus free continuing home-delivered fuel, instruction and adherence support in HAPIN.

**task:** Cook everyday meals while reducing harmful exposure; fuel/time savings and particular health outcomes require separate measurements.

**population:** A: 2,651 households in 44 Odisha villages, with primary cooks' outcomes. B: eligible pregnant women and households in selected sites in Guatemala, India, Peru and Rwanda. The populations and clinical endpoints are not interchangeable.

**interval:** A: phased programme starting 2006–2007 and followed up to four years. B: randomisation from May 2018 to February 2020 with pregnancy/birth follow-up in the 2022 report.

**contemporary alternatives:** Continued traditional biomass cooking; Mixed use of older and improved stoves; Supported LPG during HAPIN; Other commercial cooking systems not directly compared in either experiment

**adoption measure:** A: randomised offer, reported/observed use and maintenance, personal CO exposure, fuel/time and health outcomes. B: randomised package, adherence, personal particulate exposure and birth weight. Ownership is distinct from use; clinical endpoints differ.

**observation limits:** Odisha's full 2012 working paper and final 2016 abstract are separate editions of one experiment. HAPIN's free support and specific pregnant population limit transfer to commercial demand; this contribution inspects one HAPIN outcome paper only.

## Actors

**chooser:** Programmes select devices and support; households/cooks decide daily use within fuel, meal and family constraints. Trial assignment is not a household preference vote.

**payer:** Odisha's programme and household contributions/support differ from HAPIN's free stove and continuing fuel provision; household time and repair costs remain real costs. No unified cost-effectiveness comparison is made.

**operator maintainer:** Primary cooks operate devices, typically within unpaid household work. Odisha households undertake upkeep/repair; HAPIN staff provide recurring fuel and adherence support.

**affected nonchooser:** Other household members and children exposed to cooking pollution, and fetuses in HAPIN's birth-weight outcome; primary cooks need not control every purchase or fuel decision.

**ability to refuse:** Observed continued older-stove use shows some daily choice, while resource and meal constraints can limit options. HAPIN high adherence under support is not proof of unconstrained commercial willingness to pay.

## Labour

**tasks:** Cooking, fuel acquisition/handling, cleaning, chimney/device upkeep and repair; Odisha records additional repair time and does not realise expected fuel savings.

**entry barriers:** Suitable device and pots/meals, fuel access, recurring affordability, maintenance and instruction; the precise importance of each component was not separately randomised.

**bargaining ownership:** Cooks bear operation and exposure burdens but may not control purchases or cash. The studies do not fully identify intra-household bargaining effects.

**transition:** Odisha follows operation up to four years; HAPIN follows a supported pregnancy intervention. Neither tracks permanent occupation or livelihood changes after full support ends.

**distribution:** Primary cooks' exposure, household repair time and infant birth weight are distinct outcomes; a null in one does not establish equal effects for all people or all health outcomes.

**evidence limit:** No universal time-saving, health, fuel-saving or market-demand claim, and no head-to-head causal estimate of biomass versus LPG devices alone.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: continued-use and maintenance; fuel and service complements; device–meal fit; exposure displacement; trial support versus market demand; health-outcome specificity

The trials differ in technology, populations, dates, subsidy and outcomes, so they are not head-to-head product tests. HAPIN's null birth-weight result cannot simply inherit Odisha's low-use explanation.

## Evidence links

- [AGR-K17](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k17): The Odisha improved-biomass-stove experiment found an initial reduction in primary cooks' carbon-monoxide exposure that disappeared by the following year; the inspected 2012 working paper follows 2,651 households in 44 villages for up to four years.
- [AGR-K18](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k18): The Odisha working paper documents continued use of older stoves, breakage and maintenance problems alongside the loss of exposure gains, no expected fuel savings or clear health improvements, and additional repair time.
- [AGR-K19](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k19): The HAPIN trial's package of free LPG stoves, continuing home-delivered fuel, instruction and adherence support achieved high use and markedly lower measured personal particulate exposure among pregnant women in selected sites in Guatemala, India, Peru and Rwanda.
- [AGR-K20](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k20): Despite high adherence and lower exposure, HAPIN's adjusted birth-weight difference was 19.6 g with a 95% confidence interval of −10.1 to 49.2 g; this is a null finding for that specified outcome, not evidence of no health benefits of any kind.
- [AGR-K24](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#agr-k24): Across these selected cases, testing a device or plot is often insufficient to evaluate the larger system that must operate, and gains in adoption, effort or output do not alone establish who benefits or whether welfare improves.
