# OCE-C01 — Hōkūleʻa: supported voyaging and inter-island renewal

**Period:** 1976–2007; participant accounts published through 2017. **Region:** Hawaiʻi; Tahiti; Satawal, Micronesia. **Domain:** navigation; boatbuilding; knowledge transmission.

A modern voyaging programme combined a reconstructed canoe, living Micronesian instruction, Hawaiian purposes and research infrastructure. Operational success and formal transmission are documented; prehistoric route reconstruction and general cultural causation remain separate questions.

[Read the substantive research dossier](../contributions/OCE/report.md).

## Stages

**invention:** No first-invention claim; canoe and navigation practices drew on multiple bodies of knowledge.

**feasibility:** 1976 and 1980 voyages; 1980 participant record explicitly describes supported non-instrument navigation.

**deployment:** Repeated staffed voyages, later pupils and March 2007 initiation.

**diffusion:** Named canoes and pupils provide qualitative spread within a network; no population denominator.

**displacement/reversal:** 1978 capsize interrupted the programme; 1980 voyage followed. No commercial displacement claim.

## Episode

**technology configuration:** Hōkūleʻa programme: double-hulled sailing canoe, skilled non-instrument navigator, trained crew, research/escort supports; 2007 Pwo transmission is a later subepisode.

**task:** Make planned ocean landfalls and renew knowledge, responsibility and collective identity.

**population:** Named PVS voyage crews and navigators; later eleven Satawal and five Hawaiʻi initiates; purposively interviewed women voyagers are a separate qualitative sample.

**interval:** 1976–1980 operational sequence; 1979–2007 transmission sequence; women interviewed by 2010, including a dated 2007 interview.

**contemporary alternatives:** Instrument-assisted sailing and modern marine transport, available for transport but not equivalent to the cultural/learning task; Not joining or discontinuing the voluntary programme; individual opportunity costs incompletely recorded

**adoption measure:** Recorded voyages, named pupils and a specific initiation cohort; no share of Pacific navigators, crews or households.

**observation limits:** Survivor/commemoration bias; formal recognition is not a standardized proficiency denominator; no ancient-voyage uptake inferred.

## Actors

**chooser:** Mau chose to teach; learners and PVS organized participation; interviewed women explain their own purposes.

**payer:** PVS and supporting institutions supplied canoe/programme resources; full finance and donor allocation not inspected; volunteers supplied time.

**operator maintainer:** Navigators, crews, canoe builders and volunteers; named teaching and voyage roles observed, maintenance payroll unknown.

**affected nonchooser:** Families bearing time commitments and communities represented by voyages; distribution not measured.

**ability to refuse:** Mau’s agreement and women’s voluntary time are documented; some interviewees withheld genealogy. Crew access and gendered selection rules are not established.

## Labour

**tasks:** Navigation, weather/sea observation, crew coordination, teaching, canoe work and volunteering; wages largely unobserved.

**entry barriers:** Hundreds of hours of preparation in the 1980 account, sea experience, access to teachers and capacity to volunteer; neither universal equality nor a quantified gender barrier is established.

**bargaining ownership:** PVS organizes vessels; teachers control transmission and recognition; no complete ownership or decision-rights audit.

**transition:** Named learner Nainoa trained before 1980; later 2007 initiates show cohort transmission, not cohort wage mobility.

**distribution:** Women’s accounts establish participation and purposes; no comparative income or opportunity distribution.

**evidence limit:** Cannot estimate jobs created, returns to credentials, household costs or safety rates.

## Mechanisms and counterevidence

Candidate/supported mechanisms: apprenticeship and embodied environmental interpretation; hybrid training infrastructure; cultural purpose; volunteer labor and institutional coordination

Support craft and modern training matter; PVS commemorative accounts are not independent impact evaluations. Women’s accounts complicate a solely male-master narrative.

## Evidence links

- [OCE-K01](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k01): Kyselka’s contemporaneous account records Hōkūleʻa’s 1980 Hawaiʻi–Tahiti return voyage under Nainoa Thompson’s non-instrument navigation, with planetarium training, Mau Piailug’s teaching and escort-based documentation explicitly present.
- [OCE-K02](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k02): The 1979–1980 apprenticeship and March 2007 Satawal initiation support an intergenerational, inter-island knowledge-transmission pathway in voyaging renewal; they do not establish that one navigator alone caused a Pacific-wide cultural revival.
- [OCE-K03](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k03): PVS records a 1978 capsize and loss of Eddie Aikau, followed by renewed voyages including 1980; revival included severe operational failure, not an uninterrupted sequence of successful demonstrations.
- [OCE-K04](../data/CLAIM_LEDGER.md#oce-k04): Wilson-Hokowhitu’s interviews with women voyagers document ancestral responsibility and identity as expressed reasons for participation, alongside sacrifices of volunteer time; several interviewees declined or did not know personal genealogies.
