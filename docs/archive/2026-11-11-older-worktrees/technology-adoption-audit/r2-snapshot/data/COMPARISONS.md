# Comparison ledger

Comparisons preserve useful similarities, important differences, source limits and non-independent settings. They are not a matched statistical sample.

## AGR-X01 — When is a successful agricultural plot the wrong unit for judging a durable system?

**case ids:** AGR-C01; AGR-C02

**useful similarities:** Raised fields and Balinese rice both change the environment of cultivation; maintenance or coordination beyond one isolated planting season can alter performance.

**important differences:** Titicaca combines archaeology and modern reconstruction/rotation debates; Bali combines modern observations and ecological simulation. Frost/water management and pest–water coordination are different mechanisms, and their populations and historical periods differ.

**evidence comparability:** Use for comparison of evaluation boundaries, not pooled effect sizes: measured small-block temperatures, inherited labour/yield studies and simulated watershed harvests have different evidential status.

**connections nonindependence:** Bandy reuses earlier raised-field studies including Erickson; Janssen reanalyses Lansing–Kremer's model and inherits its historical narrative. These are critical analyses rather than four independent measurements of one mechanism.

**omitted countercases:** Standalone field improvements that remain beneficial without intensive maintenance or neighbour coordination; independent-schedule systems with weaker pest/water interactions; successful long-lived modern raised-field programmes not fully enumerated.

**conclusion limit:** Supports testing the scale and duration over which a system must operate. It does not show every innovation requires central coordination or that traditional arrangements are always superior.

## AGR-X02 — How can control over returns change investment or labour even when agronomic feasibility is established?

**case ids:** AGR-C03; AGR-C05

**useful similarities:** Both cases distinguish nominal household/land labels from an individual's practical control; local power and alternatives can affect whether a person captures a return from work or waiting.

**important differences:** Jahaly-Pacharr introduces a project package with gendered work obligations and operating failures; Akwapim concerns established fallow investment under tenure risk. Countries, crops, survey designs and relevant time horizons differ.

**evidence comparability:** Carney supplies qualitative institutional/labour histories and IFPRI an observational evaluation; Goldstein–Udry supplies repeated plot/household analysis and IV arguments. Neither is random assignment of political authority.

**connections nonindependence:** Carney cites some IFPRI project findings, so these are partially shared evidence. The Akwapim plot data are distinct; thematic similarity is not independent causal identification of a universal control mechanism.

**omitted countercases:** Securely controlled low-investment plots where soil, risk or cash dominates; irrigation projects where women retain returns and operating systems function; other ways to secure delayed returns without individual title.

**conclusion limit:** A reason to measure practical control, bargaining and alternatives before interpreting refusal or short fallow. It does not establish a universal gender effect, rights-policy recipe or share of output loss due to institutions.

## AGR-X03 — Do two Ghanaian studies identify the same binding constraint?

**case ids:** AGR-C05; AGR-C06

**useful similarities:** Both examine agricultural investment that may be limited even when farmers understand potential gains; anticipated capture of future returns matters.

**important differences:** Akwapim's land/fallow setting and late-twentieth-century observational survey differ from northern Ghana's maize/rainfall setting and 2009–2011 randomised insurance/cash interventions. Tenure loss and rainfall risk are distinct hazards.

**evidence comparability:** The insurance offer effect has stronger experimental identification for that package; Akwapim's institutional mechanism uses within-person/plot contrasts and exclusions. Neither gives the other's treatment effect.

**connections nonindependence:** Christopher Udry is an author of both, but the datasets, regions and interventions differ. Shared country and author do not make the settings a matched comparison or guarantee independence of conceptual framing.

**omitted countercases:** Farmers with secure tenure and reliable insurance who remain cash-constrained; uninsured farmers who invest heavily; land/investment settings with low expropriation risk but poor agronomy.

**conclusion limit:** Different constraints can rationally produce similar low-investment outcomes. No Ghana-wide ranking of tenure, risk and credit constraints follows, and one programme should not be transferred on country label alone.

## AGR-X04 — Does continued use explain all null cooking-health results?

**case ids:** AGR-C07

**useful similarities:** Both Odisha and HAPIN randomise a cooking intervention and measure exposure and downstream outcomes, allowing ownership/use/exposure/health links to be distinguished.

**important differences:** Odisha uses a low-cost biomass stove with household upkeep and multiyear follow-up; HAPIN provides LPG, continuing free fuel and support to selected pregnant women in four countries. Pollutants, health endpoints, periods and populations differ.

**evidence comparability:** A conceptual negative-case comparison, not a head-to-head stove trial. Odisha's fading CO result and HAPIN's high-adherence/lower-particulate birth-weight result cannot yield a common efficacy ranking or cost-effectiveness ratio.

**connections nonindependence:** AGR-S12 and AGR-S16 are editions of one Odisha experiment, not replication. HAPIN is a distinct trial; its background citations to earlier studies are not new measurements of their outcomes.

**omitted countercases:** Later HAPIN health outcomes not inspected; commercial LPG uptake after support ends; well-maintained improved biomass configurations; settings with different ambient pollution and fuel reliability.

**conclusion limit:** Low sustained use can limit a programme, but high use and exposure reduction do not guarantee a change in a specified endpoint. No blanket finding of no cooking-health benefit or universal maintenance explanation is supported.

## AGR-X05 — Can a rise in agricultural effort or output stand in for a welfare result?

**case ids:** AGR-C03; AGR-C04; AGR-C06

**useful similarities:** Irrigation, modern varieties and rainfall insurance can expand production possibilities or investment. Each raises a separate question about who captures benefits, what other production is displaced and which costs are omitted.

**important differences:** Project-level gendered labour and income, country/crop yield effects, regional groundwater stocks and experimental farm investment are different outcomes with different boundaries and time horizons.

**evidence comparability:** Do not pool magnitudes. The studies supply complementary outcome checks: observational income/nutrition, historical econometrics, satellite resource measurement and randomised investment responses.

**connections nonindependence:** Some project findings are shared by Carney and IFPRI, and crop reviews/econometrics may share national data. Groundwater measurement and the Ghana experiment use materially different observations, but neither independently validates all crop-yield effects.

**omitted countercases:** Interventions with measured positive net consumption, risk and distributional benefits; resource-neutral yield improvements; input increases that fail to raise profits; crop allocation changes that lower a project's yield while raising household returns.

**conclusion limit:** Requires explicit welfare accounting; it does not negate measured productive benefits or establish that costs exceed gains. Groundwater depletion is not assigned as a causal effect of a particular seed.

## AGR-X06 — What can household work measures reveal about the value of a new device?

**case ids:** AGR-C07; AGR-C08

**useful similarities:** Cooking and other domestic machines must fit repeated tasks, handling, maintenance and unpaid work; purchase or ownership omits operating time and service quality.

**important differences:** Modern cooking trials randomly assign specific packages with personal exposure measures; US appliance history and national time reconstructions span heterogeneous devices, services and decades without appliance assignment.

**evidence comparability:** The cases motivate a common measurement checklist of output, effort, maintenance and distribution, but they do not support pooled time-saving or health effects.

**connections nonindependence:** No shared participant data across these studies. Cowan and Ramey may overlap in historical time-study traditions; the working/final Odisha editions remain one experiment.

**omitted countercases:** Devices with durable direct savings at fixed service quality, households retaining older tools for valued tasks, poorer US households without utilities, and paid domestic workers' transition histories.

**conclusion limit:** Neither persistent hours nor a time decline alone measures all benefits or identifies their cause. The argument must not be converted into a claim that household machines never save time.

## ANC-X01 — What distinguishes a described mechanism from productive deployment?

**case ids:** ANC-C01; ANC-C02

**useful similarities:** Both concern rotary motion within the Roman-period technological world and invite later industrial analogies.

**important differences:** Hero's purpose is contested and evidence is textual; Barbegal concerns grinding with material operational deposits and infrastructure.

**evidence comparability:** Suitable for comparing evidentiary stages, not mechanical efficiency or adopter preference.

**connections nonindependence:** No transmission link demonstrated. Both were selected partly because they feature in the same modern debate about ancient technology.

**omitted countercases:** Other practical Heronian machines; ordinary hand/animal mills; many smaller watermills; non-European mechanical traditions.

**conclusion limit:** Shows why proof standards differ; cannot show which device had a higher return or that one caused the other.

## ANC-X02 — How far can recurrent upkeep explain long-lived water service?

**case ids:** ANC-C03; ANC-C05

**useful similarities:** Official involvement, labour organisation and maintenance procedures are visible in both settings.

**important differences:** Urban conduit distribution differs from river diversion and sediment management. Frontinus supplies administrative prescriptions; late Han evidence is mediated epigraphy, and the Chinese model is modern.

**evidence comparability:** Good for identifying candidate tasks and authority arrangements; weak for comparing burdens, continuity or effectiveness.

**connections nonindependence:** No diffusion link established. Within each case, modern analyses reuse ancient accounts; multiple citations are not multiple performance series.

**omitted countercases:** Locally managed irrigation with little state involvement; comparable failed Chinese schemes; ordinary Roman wells and small provincial supplies.

**conclusion limit:** No inference that centralized command is necessary, that coercion was efficient, or that different ancient systems had equal maintenance costs.

## ANC-X03 — Does a successful water technology imply a universal best design or permanent reliability?

**case ids:** ANC-C04; ANC-C06

**useful similarities:** Water-limited settlements invest in durable infrastructure; later modification or non-restoration is visible.

**important differences:** Groundwater galleries exploit an elevation-compatible resource; runoff storage buffers episodic inflow. Chronologies, political evidence and archaeological recovery differ.

**evidence comparability:** Physical constraints can be compared; household service levels and comparative rates of adoption cannot.

**connections nonindependence:** No genealogy asserted. Regional contacts alone would not prove a shared technological origin; excavation syntheses and newer interpretations can share evidence.

**omitted countercases:** Successful continued wells and surface canals, settlements that never encountered a suitable gallery source, Egyptian and Central Asian gallery variants.

**conclusion limit:** Supports locally conditional feasibility, not a universal environmental explanation of settlement decline.

## ANC-X04 — When does cessation count as loss of technology rather than loss or substitution of service?

**case ids:** ANC-C02; ANC-C03; ANC-C04

**useful similarities:** Each has a bounded interruption or abandonment sequence rather than a single invention date.

**important differences:** Barbegal's equipment histories are geochemical reconstructions; Rome offers a narrative of emergency substitution; Arabian galleries have settlement and hydrological ambiguity.

**evidence comparability:** Useful negative contrast to a uniform decline narrative; not a matched causal design.

**connections nonindependence:** The Roman cases share wider institutions but are separated in time and task; no direct transmission chain is established. Barbegal papers share specimens.

**omitted countercases:** Instances where workshops, specialist communities and texts demonstrably disappear together; continued handmills or wells; comparable surviving installations.

**conclusion limit:** Calls for separate tests of knowledge, infrastructure and service. It does not imply ancient knowledge was never lost.

## DIG-X01 — Does smartphone preference imply that handhelds displaced computers because they are universally more convenient?

**case ids:** DIG-C02; DIG-C03

**useful similarities:** Consumer computing/internet devices with overlapping functions; explicit computer and handheld categories.

**important differences:** US 2015 household joint access versus UK 2015 individual importance; later UK 2023 task perceptions and US 2014 constrained dependence. Different countries, samples and dates.

**evidence comparability:** Qualitative contrast only: co-access, importance and perceived difficulty cannot be pooled. Household stock is not active individual task use.

**connections nonindependence:** Same broad device/platform development connects markets; repeated Ofcom questions share institution but not one independent causal experiment. DIG-S15/S 16 are one dataset.

**omitted countercases:** Households with no device; accessibility-specific use; workplaces with mandated computers; low-cost feature-phone users; detailed retention/disposal records.

**conclusion limit:** Task complementarity is plausible but underdetermined; larger screens/input can remain useful while pocket access is valued. No price or processor-speed causal ranking.

## DIG-X02 — Does obtaining a device or cheap connection establish usable access to a desired service?

**case ids:** DIG-C03; DIG-C04; DIG-C05

**useful similarities:** Each separates possession or technical access from being able to complete a task when needed.

**important differences:** US adult service suspension, rural Indian child access to shared hardware, and Kenyan agent cash redemption constrain different actors and tasks.

**evidence comparability:** Compare mechanisms only; percentages have incompatible denominators and recall windows. Indian revenue/GB is an administrative average, not a person-level price.

**connections nonindependence:** Uses distinct household surveys/administrative data, but all systems rely on mobile networks. Kenyan early papers share a panel; 2024 FinAccess is a different sample.

**omitted countercases:** Users with reliable autonomous access; free public internet; successful offline education; mobile-money deployments outside Kenya with weak uptake.

**conclusion limit:** Possession is not sufficient for task access; no general estimate of which bottleneck dominates across societies.

## DIG-X03 — What evidence supports calling VR a minority rather than mass computing medium?

**case ids:** DIG-C03; DIG-C06

**useful similarities:** UK 2023G 1 compares gaming devices with the same population/question; the VR work experiment compares two interfaces for the same participants.

**important differences:** Gaming participation is not all-day work; UK representative-target quota survey is not German university-worker experiment; Meta accounts have no person denominator.

**evidence comparability:** High within G 1 for reported participation, though devices serve different games; controlled within-person work comparison for the tested setup. No legitimate pooled global adoption rate.

**connections nonindependence:** DIG-S11 summary, S 15 tables and S 16 methods describe the same Ofcom survey. The experiment is distinct; its authors include a commercial research affiliation.

**omitted countercases:** Stable specialist training/design uses, arcades/shared headsets, people lacking exposure, longitudinal returning users, other countries, newer/optimised headsets.

**conclusion limit:** Minority UK 2023 VR gaming relative to stated devices is supported; ownership, recent activity, retention, universal uselessness and inevitable future outcomes remain unestablished.

## DIG-X04 — Do digital systems require the same institutional complement to diffuse?

**case ids:** DIG-C01; DIG-C05

**useful similarities:** Both join networked devices to practical permissions and organisational arrangements.

**important differences:** CERN code reuse and information linking differ from money redemption, identification and banking safeguards; no common adoption denominator or matched historical period.

**evidence comparability:** Design/release records versus operational surveys and conditional welfare estimates. Analogy is a mechanism-generation device, not causal replication.

**connections nonindependence:** Both exploit pre-existing communications/computing; no direct transmission link between these specific institutions is established.

**omitted countercases:** Proprietary information systems that spread; open systems that remained unused; mobile-money services with permissive regulation but low uptake.

**conclusion limit:** Institutions should be examined configuration by configuration; neither openness nor strict identification is a universal adoption prerequisite.

## DIG-X05 — Can company revenue, investment or accounting loss be used as adoption measures?

**case ids:** DIG-C06; DIG-C07

**useful similarities:** Large firms disclose resources and performance in consumer-device businesses.

**important differences:** Microsoft phone impairment reassesses assets of an acquired portfolio; Meta RL operating loss mixes AR/MR/VRhardware, content and research. Different accounting measures and periods.

**evidence comparability:** Both are primary company disclosures, but revenue/loss/impairment are not commensurate with users or retained use.

**connections nonindependence:** Shared platform competition context; sources originate with interested firms and are not independent consumer surveys.

**omitted countercases:** Profitable niche hardware; low-margin high-use systems; open/community software with few commercial revenues.

**conclusion limit:** Deployment and sales can coexist with a failed expansion strategy; segment losses cannot prove no consumer utility or predict eventual success.

## ENE-X01 — Does more production necessarily make a technology cheaper?

**case ids:** ENE-C01; ENE-C02; ENE-C03; ENE-C04

**useful similarities:** All require capital, repeatable processes and accumulated operational knowledge; all have historical cost evidence.

**important differences:** PV and cell factory output are not site-built nuclear projects; DRAM costs are structurally inferred; product performance and cohort intervals differ.

**evidence comparability:** Compare proposed mechanisms and measurement boundaries, not numerical learning rates. Nuclear OCC omits finance; battery USD/Wh differs from module USD/W.

**connections nonindependence:** PV, nuclear decomposition and battery studies share some authors/methods; DRAM and PV share semiconductor inputs; nuclear datasets reuse reactor records.

**omitted countercases:** Wind turbines, chemical plants, cement and shipbuilding would test granularity and complexity beyond this selection.

**conclusion limit:** Evidence rejects a universal automatic-cost-decline rule; it does not estimate how often learning dominates or prove modularity is sufficient.

## ENE-X02 — Why does a newer chemistry coexist with an older one?

**case ids:** ENE-C04; ENE-C05

**useful similarities:** Rechargeable storage serves portable/mobile loads; both combine cells, product design and user charging/maintenance.

**important differences:** High-energy electronics prize volume and mass; low-speed two-wheelers face upfront purchase and road-access constraints. Historical comparison uses different services/windows.

**evidence comparability:** Contemporary e-bike accounts directly document availability and dominance; cell-cost reconstruction is not an e-bike lifetime-cost panel.

**connections nonindependence:** Shared battery producers/knowledge possible but not individually traced; Weinert coauthors both e-bike sources and reuses survey evidence.

**omitted countercases:** Japanese/European premium e-bikes, off-grid storage and lead-acid vehicle starter batteries would test whether price, serviceability or performance dominates.

**conclusion limit:** Continued lead-acid use contradicts inevitable immediate replacement; household credit constraints and lifetime expectations remain hypotheses.

## ENE-X03 — Does state involvement have one consistent effect on diffusion?

**case ids:** ENE-C01; ENE-C02; ENE-C03; ENE-C05

**useful similarities:** Procurement, permissions and finance alter feasible choices rather than merely respond to a preexisting price.

**important differences:** Apollo purchased specialized components; French utilities organized construction; PV demand support crossed borders; cities both favored and prohibited e-bikes.

**evidence comparability:** Different actors, interventions and outcomes prevent pooling as a single treatment called government.

**connections nonindependence:** International trade connects national PV policies to Chinese output; licensed reactor designs connect programme histories.

**omitted countercases:** Comparable unsupported firms/cities and abandoned procurement programmes not systematically sampled.

**conclusion limit:** Useful institutional distinctions survive; no estimate of net state-versus-market contribution or welfare follows.

## ENE-X04 — When is specialized knowledge an entry barrier and when can it travel?

**case ids:** ENE-C01; ENE-C03; ENE-C05

**useful similarities:** Manufacturing relies on complementary equipment, routines, skilled people and component interfaces.

**important differences:** PV upstream secrecy contrasts with downstream turnkey supply; DRAM generation changes can erode prior learning; e-bike assembly uses prefabricated components.

**evidence comparability:** Interview histories, structural estimates and factory visits reveal different aspects; they do not measure the same worker or firm entry probability.

**connections nonindependence:** PV inherits semiconductor materials/process knowledge; mobility of staff and vendor feedback can link nominally separate firms.

**omitted countercases:** Failed entrants, women/migrant production workers, financing-constrained firms and proprietary chemical processes remain undersampled.

**conclusion limit:** Observed transferable know-how and lower assembly requirements do not establish equitable access, high wages or absence of capital barriers.

## IND-X01 — Does cheap energy generally explain steam adoption?

**case ids:** IND-C01; IND-C02; IND-C03

**useful similarities:** All concern obtaining mechanical work with geographically constrained power resources and capital equipment.

**important differences:** Mine drainage differs from rotary textile production; Cornwall imported dear coal, while early coalfield users had cheap fuel. Central European rod systems and Manchester factories are separated in time and industrial location.

**evidence comparability:** Descriptions, engine-duty records, reconstructed horsepower and modelled hydrology have different denominators; no pooled adoption rate is warranted.

**connections nonindependence:** British steam studies share earlier engine histories. Technological transfer and later technical improvements connect cases, so they are not independent experiments.

**omitted countercases:** French/Belgian mines, Scottish reservoir successes, American water-powered factories and systematic non-coalfield Newcomen installations.

**conclusion limit:** Cheap coal can support a fuel-intensive niche while dear coal motivates fuel-saving adoption. Neither proves coal irrelevant or identifies a single universal energy threshold.

## IND-X02 — Do high wages uniquely predict mechanisation or a particular spinning machine?

**case ids:** IND-C04; IND-C05; IND-C06

**useful similarities:** Cotton/yarn production creates linked comparisons of labour, capital, materials and product quality.

**important differences:** Early mixed-fibre hand-spinning data differ from later mill-machine orders; international silver wages differ from local female earnings, and machine technologies evolve over a century.

**evidence comparability:** Daily pay construction, cost models and machinery specifications are not directly commensurable productivity measures.

**connections nonindependence:** Lancashire machinery and cotton markets directly connect the cases; Broadberry/Gupta and Clingingsmith/Williamson share data, and all studies engage overlapping debates.

**omitted countercases:** French/continental commercial spinner accounts, underfinanced Japanese/Indian nonbuyers, independent mill wage/hour and contract records.

**conclusion limit:** Evidence rejects a fixed wage-to-machine mapping and requires task-specific comparisons; it does not reject every induced-innovation mechanism or prove that low wages caused Japanese success.

## IND-X03 — Does factory production necessarily displace all household textile tasks?

**case ids:** IND-C04; IND-C05; IND-C07

**useful similarities:** Spinning and weaving are separable tasks connected by tradable yarn, and households combine paid and unpaid labour.

**important differences:** Periods, cloth qualities, gender regimes and trade politics differ; China’s pre-1949 hybrid is not the same event as early British spinning mechanisation.

**evidence comparability:** Qualitative household displacement, trade data and a reported national handloom-output share cannot be pooled as a common decline rate.

**connections nonindependence:** British, Indian and Japanese yarn exports directly affected Chinese household options; these observations are parts of a connected trading system.

**omitted countercases:** Indian handloom districts with long persistence, regional Chinese non-cotton textiles, Japanese rural weaving and matched household time budgets.

**conclusion limit:** Industrial intermediates can complement older tasks while displacing others. Persistence alone does not prove freely chosen preference, efficiency or welfare improvement.

## IND-X04 — Is delay evidence of irrational resistance when a new power or production system is technically feasible?

**case ids:** IND-C03; IND-C06; IND-C08

**useful similarities:** Investors choose between configurations requiring different complements and facing installed-capital costs.

**important differences:** A non-built reservoir, purchases of improving mules and retrofits of existing factory power systems are different decisions; public coordination and private replacement costs cannot be equated.

**evidence comparability:** Reservoir account is abstract-only, machinery orders quantitatively richer, electric configurations inferred partly from technical literature.

**connections nonindependence:** Common engineering and capitalist-market contexts link some mechanisms, but the studies do not form a single matched sample.

**omitted countercases:** Documented profitable projects rejected despite available complements; factory failures after enthusiastic electrification; reservoirs with successful governance.

**conclusion limit:** Delay has several empirically distinguishable candidates. These cases require investigation of complements, product niches and coordination before assigning irrationality.

## LBR-X01 — Does stable aggregate employment imply displaced workers benefit?

**case ids:** LBR-C01; LBR-C02

**useful similarities:** Both distinguish changing work from a static stock of jobs.

**important differences:** LBR-C01 links incumbents and cohorts around a specific shock; LBR-C02 measures broad title/patent patterns over decades.

**evidence comparability:** Not a pooled treatment effect. Useful as a warning about incompatible units of analysis.

**connections nonindependence:** Both use US census-based evidence and related task-model literature; different constructed measures do not make independent global replications.

**omitted countercases:** Other countries and racialised labour markets; Transitions without growing substitute sectors; Unpaid work and worker-controlled technology

**conclusion limit:** Historical creation of roles does not prove access, desirable conditions or compensation for any displaced individual.

## MAT-X01 — Does an intrinsically superior metal explain substitution?

**case ids:** MAT-C02; MAT-C05

**useful similarities:** Both have competing copper-alloy/material traditions and continued older practice; properties depend on processing and task.

**important differences:** Canaan compares bronze with iron using archaeological sequences; Andean comparison includes controlled modern arsenical/tin-bronze tests and an imperial distribution interpretation.

**evidence comparability:** Mechanical tests and archaeological assemblage frequencies answer different questions; neither supplies comparable prices or household adoption denominators.

**connections nonindependence:** The two regional empirical programmes differ, but share scholarly critiques of hardness ranking. Several claims within each case derive from the same paper and must not count as separate replications.

**omitted countercases:** Contexts where improved treatment demonstrably precedes task-specific replacement; arsenical/tin-bronze transitions in other regions.

**conclusion limit:** Rejects a universal scalar material ranking; does not show performance never causes substitution.

## MAT-X02 — Does sustained ironmaking imply fuel depletion and inevitable mineral-fuel adoption?

**case ids:** MAT-C03; MAT-C04

**useful similarities:** Ironmaking requires substantial, suitable fuel supplies and coordinated operation; both retain uncertainty about production intensity.

**important differences:** Song–Yuan evidence includes mineral fuel and documentary information; Meroe/Hamadab evidence centres on selected charcoal and modern replicas. Ecology, chronology and product mix differ.

**evidence comparability:** Text/chemical fuel evidence and anthracology measure different things. Neither gives matched local forest inventories, prices and output per year.

**connections nonindependence:** Meroe experimental and charcoal papers share project chronology. Wagner supplies both technical and output critiques for the Chinese subepisode; no direct historical link is claimed.

**omitted countercases:** Measured charcoal woodland management elsewhere; iron regions with accessible coal that continued charcoal; periods of demonstrated fuel shortage.

**conclusion limit:** Resource substitution and sustainability are contingent; continued charcoal is a meaningful countercase to inevitability, not proof of no damage.

## MAT-X03 — Can distinctive local production establish independent invention?

**case ids:** MAT-C03; MAT-C07

**useful similarities:** Both require separating finished-object occurrence from local transformation and combine multiple pyrotechnical operations.

**important differences:** Chinese iron involves contested early chronological/route reconstruction; Ile-Ife has distinctive chemistry and raw-batch residues within an exchange setting.

**evidence comparability:** Shared labels such as casting conceal different processes. Recipe uniqueness, kiln presence and distribution are not equivalent measures of knowledge transmission.

**connections nonindependence:** Chinese syntheses reuse earlier finds. The three Ile-Ife articles overlap sites, authors and production theory, although specimens/analyses differ.

**omitted countercases:** Documented craft migration that produces novel local recipes; independent inventions with secure developmental sequences; other African glass production sites.

**conclusion limit:** Local primary production is not logically equivalent to isolation; transfer is not demonstrated merely by earlier external examples.

## MAT-X04 — How can continuing functions or valued sensory properties constrain adoption explanations?

**case ids:** MAT-C01; MAT-C06

**useful similarities:** The task performed matters more than a generic claim that a new material is better; both cases resist a purely mechanical or subsistence-only interpretation.

**important differences:** Japanese culinary association is directly chemically measured; West Mexican meaning is an abstract-level report of laboratory and ethnohistoric interpretation.

**evidence comparability:** Evidence is deliberately unequal. Residue continuity does not independently demonstrate preference; reported ritual meanings need the full underlying texts.

**connections nonindependence:** No historical connection inferred. Both are selected for interpretive contrast and therefore cannot estimate prevalence of cultural mechanisms.

**omitted countercases:** Ceramic traditions with changing terrestrial-food use; metalworkers prioritising ordinary cutting tools; non-users who lacked exposure rather than rejected use.

**conclusion limit:** Specifies a question about experienced utility; does not causally identify psychology or universal symbolic conservatism.

## MED-X01 — When does legal compulsion create delivery, and when does it exceed delivery capacity?

**case ids:** MED-C02; MED-C04

**useful similarities:** Both involve positive health objectives, cross-person effects and public authority altering private choices.

**important differences:** Ottoman vaccination required recurrent household contact and biological supplies; Massachusetts sewerage pooled capital and governed a spatial network. They are not equivalent coercive acts.

**evidence comparability:** Regulations/qualitative provision versus municipal construction records and mortality panel. No common coverage, consent or welfare metric.

**connections nonindependence:** Separate core sources, but both selected for institutional mechanisms. Shared interpretation is not independent causal confirmation.

**omitted countercases:** Voluntary well-supplied vaccination; compulsory poorly maintained sewerage; successful intermunicipal bargains without equivalent mandates.

**conclusion limit:** Rules can alter incentives and obligations; only accompanying evidence establishes realized service. Neither case proves compulsion uniquely necessary.

## MED-X02 — Does simple material composition remove the need for embodied skills and organized teaching?

**case ids:** MED-C03; MED-C07

**useful similarities:** Both require competent performance and transfer practical responsibility beyond a written formula.

**important differences:** Surgery is specialized invasive care; ORT includes household administration and nonprofessional teaching. Their task risks and skill thresholds differ.

**evidence comparability:** Historical surgical publications/interviews versus programme survey and preparation tests; no matched training experiment across cases.

**connections nonindependence:** Both historical narratives feature successful advocates; insiders and later success can shape interpretation.

**omitted countercases:** Continued cleanliness practice in surgery and continuing non-use/home preparation in the ORT survey; further cases with adequate competence but absent use needed.

**conclusion limit:** Material simplicity is insufficient to infer cheap or immediate implementation. The quantitative contribution of teaching is not identified by recurrence.

## MED-X03 — How can supply economics produce too little access in one antibiotic episode and excessive use in another?

**case ids:** MED-C05; MED-C08

**useful similarities:** Both concern pharmaceuticals and actors who control access while responding to resource and revenue incentives.

**important differences:** 1940s manufacturing bottleneck versus 2011–2012 prescribing of established medicines; different products, populations and clinical tasks.

**evidence comparability:** Commemorative production history versus randomized encounter scripts. Stronger mechanism discrimination in the audit does not validate the production counterfactual.

**connections nonindependence:** Broad antibiotic category connects episodes; no direct genealogical or causal path from Pfizer production to the audited city is asserted.

**omitted countercases:** Efficient production with equitable access; incentive-linked care without overprescribing; contemporary antibiotic under-access.

**conclusion limit:** Adoption volume is not a sufficient welfare metric. Increased supply and appropriate use must be evaluated separately.

## MED-X04 — Can initial success make continued intervention less visible while a recurring service remains necessary?

**case ids:** MED-C06; MED-C07

**useful similarities:** Both require repeatable action beyond a demonstration, and measured outcomes depend on observation/recognition.

**important differences:** Malaria suppression changes population transmission over time; ORT responds to individual episodes and does not itself eliminate diarrhoeal pathogens.

**evidence comparability:** Reported infections and control history versus recalled treatment episodes and chemical preparation tests. No shared outcome scale or direct recurrence probability.

**connections nonindependence:** Both draw on health-programme participants' histories; source count cannot neutralize institutional selection.

**omitted countercases:** Malaria elimination with stable maintenance; places where environmental changes allow less intensive control; ORT knowledge without product access.

**conclusion limit:** Maintaining a service is analytically distinct from achieving first success, but no universal maintenance cost or failure probability follows.

## MED-X05 — Is refusal best explained by ignorance when knowledge, supply and decision authority are separated?

**case ids:** MED-C01; MED-C02; MED-C07; MED-C08

**useful similarities:** Information passes through trusted or powerful intermediaries and decisions may be made for dependents.

**important differences:** Induced infection, vaccination, rehydration and prescription each have different immediate risks, objectives and power relations.

**evidence comparability:** Sparse correspondence and official text are weak on beliefs; survey reasons are self-reports; the audit manipulates explicit demand. Psychological claims have unequal support.

**connections nonindependence:** MED-C01/C02 share aspects of the Montagu narrative; they are not independent origin evidence. The other datasets are distinct but purposively chosen.

**omitted countercases:** Independent observations of well-informed refusal with reliable supply; communities already served through older practices; non-exposed populations.

**conclusion limit:** Observed non-use does not identify a preference or an ignorance mechanism. In the audit, demand effects depend on dispensing conditions; that is not a timeless result about patients.

## MOB-X01 — Does improving the line-haul vehicle determine the best complete transport system?

**case ids:** MOB-C01; MOB-C06

**useful similarities:** Both expose costs and coordination at interfaces between land and water transport.

**important differences:** Roman evidence is mainly a model and pottery distribution; postwar containers have industrial capital, standards and trade panels. No continuity or equivalence of institutions is assumed.

**evidence comparability:** Mechanism comparison only, not a pooled cost-ratio estimate or measured common outcome.

**connections nonindependence:** Both research traditions use generalized transport-cost reasoning; container scholarship is not corroboration of a particular Roman parameter.

**omitted countercases:** Bulk water transport requiring few transfers; local road journeys; ports where containers remained marginal.

**conclusion limit:** Supports asking about the entire chain, not claiming transfer costs always dominate.

## MOB-X02 — Can imperial infrastructure create local benefits without being voluntarily chosen or uniformly beneficial?

**case ids:** MOB-C02; MOB-C03; MOB-C04

**useful similarities:** Political power structures initial access and resources; local production responds and can differ from rulers’ intentions.

**important differences:** Inka archaeology, colonial Indian district/company/financial panels and Ghanaian spatial data have different institutions, labour relations and scales.

**evidence comparability:** No common welfare or coercion metric. The cases jointly prevent equating producer response with political consent.

**connections nonindependence:** Indian/Ghana studies share a transport-economics research conversation and use planned-route tests; repeated findings are not independent randomized replications.

**omitted countercases:** Infrastructure imposing costs without observed local production gains; districts refusing or bypassing projects; merchant-funded networks outside empires.

**conclusion limit:** Neither gains nor coercion can be inferred for every person from an aggregate infrastructure outcome.

## MOB-X03 — Does displacement mean all functions and effects of an older system disappear?

**case ids:** MOB-C04; MOB-C05

**useful similarities:** Older configurations lose dominance while selected benefits, practices or spatial consequences survive.

**important differences:** Ghana’s persistence concerns places served by replacement roads; electric niches concern continued operation of vehicles under selected duty cycles.

**evidence comparability:** Different outcomes deliberately retained; there is no common survival rate.

**connections nonindependence:** The Ghana evidence is largely one original spatial study; the EV sources share parts of the scholarly debate. They are not multiple independent tests of a single latent law.

**omitted countercases:** Complete abandonment after loss of transport access; electric fleets with suitable routes but unaffordable maintenance; tasks never exposed to either system.

**conclusion limit:** Supports disaggregating displacement, not a universal claim that every failed technology leaves a valuable niche.

## MOB-X04 — Does lower operating cost imply a favourable occupational transition?

**case ids:** MOB-C03; MOB-C06; MOB-C07

**useful similarities:** Labour and organization are integral to cost changes, with institutions controlling investment and surplus.

**important differences:** Colonial India’s state could cut employment without electoral dependence on workers; US dock bargaining created a compensation fund; Singapore’s official speech supplies no independent labour-outcome series.

**evidence comparability:** Expense/employment panels, litigated contracts and political speech differ sharply. Singapore is an explicit evidence gap, not a positive welfare observation.

**connections nonindependence:** Two US court records concern connected agreements and disputes. Similar language of efficiency is not independent evidence of workers’ outcomes.

**omitted countercases:** Unorganized port workers, informal carriers, unpaid household adjustments and projects without compensatory bargaining.

**conclusion limit:** Requires person/cohort transition evidence before making claims about new human opportunities or net livelihood gains.

## NET-X01 — Does a writing system determine the adopted reproduction method, or do task/workflow differences also matter?

**case ids:** NET-C02; NET-C03; NET-C04

**useful similarities:** Each setting combines copying/reproduction tasks, skilled preparation and material inventories; movable type is technically known in all three comparisons at some point.

**important differences:** Scripts and languages, patronage, title demand, printing configurations, periods and labour markets differ. Korean Jikji is an object episode, not equivalent to European city adoption.

**evidence comparability:** Chinese/Korean qualitative workflows and surviving objects can test categorical impossibility or automatic replacement; they cannot be ranked against European city/edition statistics as equivalent rates.

**connections nonindependence:** McDermott/Burke and Park share book-history literature; European accounts also inherit some of it. Potential transmission is not proven by chronology and creates neither automatic identity nor independent observations.

**omitted countercases:** Japanese publishing and later type experiments; Indian lithography; Other East Asian genres and officially subsidised projects

**conclusion limit:** Within-script niches and long coexistence reject a sufficient-cause script-only theory. They do not identify the weight of character inventory, wages, subsidies, demand or repression.

## NET-X02 — Do religious and state institutions consistently suppress these practices, or do particular interventions enable and constrain different tasks?

**case ids:** NET-C01; NET-C02; NET-C03; NET-C05

**useful similarities:** Authorities/patrons affect material acceptance, finance, expertise or permission in each case; concrete documents and production episodes make their actions inspectable.

**important differences:** A 1409 ruling, Buddhist sponsorship, Korean official resources and an Ottoman licence are different interventions with different jurisdictions and actors.

**evidence comparability:** They discriminate a timeless uniformly hostile account, but do not form a common measure of openness or net technological effect. Norms, surviving objects and retrospective scholarship have different strengths.

**connections nonindependence:** Cases sit in connected textual/material worlds; scholarship also repeats inherited narratives. Schwartz and Sabev overlap on Ottoman records but do not supply independent confirmations of every quantity.

**omitted countercases:** Documented censorship and prosecutions in particular European/Ottoman settings; Denied publishing applications; Religious/administrative production beyond elite canonical texts

**conclusion limit:** Institutional direction must be specified by act, actor and subject. Enabling examples do not estimate whether an entire regime's net effect was positive.

## NET-X03 — Does continued manuscript use establish a failed printing transition?

**case ids:** NET-C01; NET-C02; NET-C03; NET-C05

**useful similarities:** Writing/copying remains part of book systems alongside paper trade and printing; ownership, circulation and mechanical reproduction are distinct outcomes.

**important differences:** The paper case concerns inputs, Chinese evidence covers print-method choices, Korean rental manuscripts form commercial circulation, and Ottoman inventory data sample particular titles/owners.

**evidence comparability:** The comparison can identify distinct tasks and reject automatic displacement, but cannot calculate a common manuscript share or profitability ranking.

**connections nonindependence:** Printed and copied texts often move through the same trade/reading networks; coexistence units are therefore not independent technology trials.

**omitted countercases:** Tasks where standardised printed forms rapidly displaced copying; Low-demand texts never commercially printed; Ordinary readers/nonreaders absent from surviving catalogues

**conclusion limit:** Older practice can remain useful, profitable or imposed by constraints. Persistence alone proves neither preference nor efficiency, and absence of print is not always exposure followed by rejection.

## NET-X04 — Can device availability explain deployment without tracing how skilled people and working knowledge are obtained?

**case ids:** NET-C01; NET-C04; NET-C06

**useful similarities:** Papermaking, press operation and navigation all require practices beyond awareness that a technique exists, including material handling or interpretive knowledge.

**important differences:** A fixed craft workshop differs from a moving vessel; printer entry data and one coerced pilot-acquisition episode are not comparable labour markets. The paper evidence is partly later craft reconstruction.

**evidence comparability:** Process detail and people moving into an episode support complementarity. They do not identify equivalent skill elasticities, training times or universal necessities.

**connections nonindependence:** Trade/migration networks transfer inputs and people together; observed skill movement can respond to demand. Staples/Tolmacheva share texts, and Staples partly overlaps the voyage tradition inspected in translation.

**omitted countercases:** Projects where untrained users successfully operated standardised equipment; Training failures and non-surviving workshops; Indian Ocean crew traditions beyond the Arabic/Turkish and Portuguese record

**conclusion limit:** A contribution by trained people is supported in bounded episodes; neither an all-purpose tacit-knowledge explanation nor a quantified future occupational prediction follows. Coercion changes whose gains and losses must be considered.

## OCE-X01 — When does demonstrated operation establish historical adoption?

**case ids:** OCE-C01; OCE-C05

**useful similarities:** Voyaging and crop trials reconstruct practical competence and expose material/skill constraints.

**important differences:** Hōkūleʻa also served directly chosen contemporary cultural purposes; Cook Strait crop trials principally test feasibility near archaeological sites. Their support systems and users differ.

**evidence comparability:** Experiments and participant records can document what happened under their conditions; neither gives a prehistoric adoption denominator.

**connections nonindependence:** Neither is independent proof of a shared ancient Polynesian institutional configuration; experimental practice is a shared research approach, not shared outcome evidence.

**omitted countercases:** Unsuccessful or unpublished reconstructions; Other canoe configurations, untended/untreated crop trials and storage experiments

**conclusion limit:** Retain modern deployment as a genuine outcome while refusing to use it as direct proof of ancient routes, farm yields or unchanged tradition.

## OCE-X02 — Does cessation mean a superior substitute won or knowledge disappeared?

**case ids:** OCE-C02; OCE-C04; OCE-C05

**useful similarities:** Observed local interruption in technically workable systems can be related to altered environmental or institutional conditions.

**important differences:** Tae Rak drainage/opposition and modern Pōhatu land disconnection have documented institutional histories; Kuk’s prehistoric interruption has competing unresolved causes.

**evidence comparability:** Mechanism evidence is stronger for the modern hydraulic intervention than for prehistoric disappearance of features; these cannot be pooled as equally identified coercion cases.

**connections nonindependence:** S05 and S06 share a restoration lineage; S09 and S10 share Kuk evidence. Repetition across their texts adds no independent confirmation.

**omitted countercases:** Voluntary shifts to higher-yielding imported crops with surviving household records; Undisturbed comparable wetlands with continued operation

**conclusion limit:** A chooser-access-water distinction belongs in the general model; one cannot impute colonial coercion to Kuk or infer voluntary technological rejection at Tae Rak.

## OCE-X03 — Must maintenance of a landscape-scale system imply centralized coordination?

**case ids:** OCE-C02; OCE-C03; OCE-C04

**useful similarities:** Recurring human work modifies water or fuel conditions and has effects beyond a single immediate action.

**important differences:** Martu burning is directly observed in everyday, gendered foraging; Tae Rak restoration has formal organizations and contractors; Kuk institutions are archaeologically unresolved.

**evidence comparability:** Behavioral/field comparisons versus project records versus archaeological traces provide unequal leverage on authority and division of labor.

**connections nonindependence:** Martu papers overlap population and data programme; they are not two independent regional replications.

**omitted countercases:** Indigenous centralized field/water systems; Places where burning produced harmful outcomes or immediate incentives failed to sustain shared benefit

**conclusion limit:** Physical coordination can emerge through repeated local practice, but this does not imply absence of customary institutions or universal benign ecological outcomes.

## OCE-X04 — What persists when a technology’s purpose or configuration changes?

**case ids:** OCE-C01; OCE-C02; OCE-C03; OCE-C05

**useful similarities:** People combine inherited knowledge with contemporary tools, institutions or scientific methods.

**important differences:** Navigation renewal, restoration hydraulics, daily foraging and soil-history reconstruction are different purposes; living practice, recollection and experimental inference have different standing.

**evidence comparability:** Specific documented acts and expressed goals are comparable; authenticity, cultural continuity and community welfare are not a single measurable variable.

**connections nonindependence:** PVS witnesses and interviewees share a movement; knowledge histories in the Māori paper partly use coauthor and community sources rather than independent textual corroboration.

**omitted countercases:** Explicit community refusals to share/revive a practice; Technologies retained for necessity under disadvantage rather than chosen renewal

**conclusion limit:** Hybrid practice is not evidence of failed authenticity; nor does honoring persistence establish unchanged technique, unrestricted knowledge access or equal distribution of benefits.

## POL-X01 — Does a state rule establish technological disappearance or universal use?

**case ids:** POL-C01; POL-C04

**useful similarities:** Both contain formal rules plus differentiated continuing practice; categorical abandonment/adoption stories are falsified by scoped counterexamples.

**important differences:** Firearms regulate violence, status and crop protection; metrication regulates commensuration. Domain pluralism differs from successive French national mandates.

**evidence comparability:** Japanese local counts are inherited through historians; French evidence combines direct decree with local archive synthesis. Neither supplies national use shares.

**connections nonindependence:** Comparison is conceptual; no transfer between cases asserted. Japanese sources overlap Howell/Fujiki; French legal sources share the same decrees.

**omitted countercases:** Other Tokugawa domains; French regions with rapid compliance; occupational groups lacking alternatives.

**conclusion limit:** Demonstrates law/use distinction, not a general causal effect of regulation or a prevalence estimate.

## POL-X02 — Can strategic state demand both advance deployment and impair performance?

**case ids:** POL-C02; POL-C03

**useful similarities:** State purchasing/administrative demand and financing arrangements shape practical systems; formal reach need not equal reliable service or household welfare.

**important differences:** Monetary issue creates liabilities/value risks; telegraph is an information network. Centuries, regimes, evidence and outside options differ.

**evidence comparability:** Yuan reconstructed annual issues/prices versus Qing prefectural office/price panel; no comparable adoption or welfare metric.

**connections nonindependence:** Both concern parts of China but no direct institutional transmission is established. Some cited Chinese historical compilations recur within cases, not independent causal replications.

**omitted countercases:** Other Yuan regional payments, nonstrategic Qing lines, networks funded without merchant shareholders, and successful fiscal systems elsewhere.

**conclusion limit:** Supports examining who funds and controls complements; does not prove a state-versus-market ranking or a timeless Chinese capacity trait.

## POL-X03 — Did telegraph access stabilize every connected market?

**case ids:** POL-C03

**useful similarities:** Within the same historical panel, connected markets share technology and estimated specification.

**important differences:** Preexisting volatility and exposure to local/distant shocks differ; connection timing and market roles differ.

**evidence comparability:** Common rice-price reporting framework; baseline-risk subgrouping and weather weights are model choices.

**connections nonindependence:** K13 and K14 use one Gao–Lei study and underlying panel, not independent replications.

**omitted countercases:** Unreported grains, household consumption prices, remote nonrice provinces, unsuccessful/delayed messages and nonusers.

**conclusion limit:** Risk redistribution is more accurate than uniform stabilization; cannot rank household welfare without net-buyer/seller and income evidence.

## POL-X04 — Can coerced standardization coexist with convenience and continued alternatives?

**case ids:** POL-C02; POL-C04

**useful similarities:** Both impose a common official language of value/quantity while requiring practical infrastructures and user adaptation.

**important differences:** Currency valuation and redemption differ from measurement calibration; inflation is not analogous to a changed measuring unit.

**evidence comparability:** Polo is rhetorical traveler testimony; French decree is prescriptive law; both require practice evidence before assessing consent.

**connections nonindependence:** No monetary-to-metric transmission claim; analogy explicitly limited.

**omitted countercases:** Private monetary standards, voluntary standards with broad uptake, local rejection without state enforcement.

**conclusion limit:** Legal uptake may combine compulsion and usefulness. Neither compliance nor persistence of alternatives alone reveals preference.

## SYN-X01 — When does equipment presence fail to establish useful service?

**case ids:** ANC-C01; AGR-C07; DIG-C04; MED-C05

**useful similarities:** All distinguish an available object or principle from the user’s relevant outcome.

**important differences:** Textual description, field trial, survey and institutional production history observe different stages and populations.

**evidence comparability:** Diagnostic counterexamples to a universal sufficiency claim; not matched causal estimates or a failure-rate sample.

**connections nonindependence:** Some complements and reporting traditions recur; modern programme research is better observed than ancient use.

**omitted countercases:** Simple tools usable with few external complements; Adoption after a single price/quality change; Non-users with adequate complements

**conclusion limit:** Names stage distinctions; does not show that complements are always the binding constraint.

## SYN-X02 — Does a newer technology necessarily displace older practice?

**case ids:** MAT-C05; NET-C02; NET-C03; IND-C07; MOB-C05

**useful similarities:** Observable coexistence, hybrid workflows or stable task-specific niches.

**important differences:** Alloy properties, text workflows, textile inputs and vehicle duty cycles differ.

**evidence comparability:** Can disprove a universal replacement narrative; cannot estimate how often coexistence is efficient.

**connections nonindependence:** Printing cases share scholarship and linked East Asian histories; industrial cases belong to connected trade systems.

**omitted countercases:** Rapid near-complete replacement in a well-defined task; Persistence solely through enforced exclusion

**conclusion limit:** Coexistence does not establish optimality, autonomy or unchanged use intensity.

## SYN-X03 — Can uptake or output gains represent all actors’ welfare?

**case ids:** AGR-C03; ANC-C03; MED-C08; MOB-C03

**useful similarities:** Choice, payment, operation and exposure to harm can belong to different actors.

**important differences:** Household gender relations, slavery, provider incentives and colonial finance have distinct institutions.

**evidence comparability:** Actor decomposition is portable; institutional effects and welfare magnitudes are not pooled.

**connections nonindependence:** Some cases lie within imperial/state systems, not isolated independent experiments.

**omitted countercases:** Worker-owned adoption; Voluntary collective provision with measured distribution; Effective veto/compensation by affected users

**conclusion limit:** No general net-benefit ranking or single sign for state/religious/market institutions.

## SYN-X04 — Does new work or aggregate recovery compensate displaced individuals?

**case ids:** LBR-C01; LBR-C02; MOB-C06; IND-C04; AGR-C08

**useful similarities:** Technological change reallocates tasks, control and measured work.

**important differences:** Linked individuals, occupational-title aggregates, legal bargaining records and household histories have different units.

**evidence comparability:** Only some sources directly observe transitions; absence of such evidence is retained.

**connections nonindependence:** US studies share census traditions; industrial and household evidence may inherit overlapping scholarship.

**omitted countercases:** Non-US linked workers; Unpaid carers; Transitions with strong worker ownership or no growing substitute sector

**conclusion limit:** Historical tasks are candidate research directions, not forecasts of human comparative advantage or wages.
