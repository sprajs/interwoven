Primary comparison of the chemistry and composition of artifacts and geological source samples. Shares Nyayanga contributors and archaeological material with Plummer et al. (2023), whose field stratigraphy and tool observations are separate evidence. Some source data build on previous surveys. The new study samples more deposits and makes a new artifact-to-source comparison.

Results, Sources accessed by hominins and Stone transport distance, Figures 4–5
The sampled local conglomerates lack suitable naturally occurring Bukoban quartzite; quartzite artifacts most closely match the Oboro source samples in the Awach Tende drainage about 13 km away. Younger eastern conglomerates are geological analogues for older sources, and the precise ancient pickup location remains unknown.

Results, Stone transport behavior and Figure 6; Discussion
Nonlocal raw materials account for most of the assemblage mass. Outer surfaces on flakes and cores and other reduction indicators support transport of relatively little-worked raw material and on-site work. The study interprets probable travels exceeding 10 km; it does not directly observe a route or purpose.

Materials and Methods, Nyayanga, Kenya
The study assigns the NY-1 archaeological context a bracket of 3.040–2.581 Ma. The 2023 field report uses 3.032 Ma for the older bound in its final summary. The artifact deposits supply the date, with no direct date of stone collection.
