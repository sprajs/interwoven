Arsenical copper tools of Old Kingdom Giza craftsmen: First data
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2021.102868
Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 36, article 102868
Inspected 2026-11-17. Version of record inspected in the author-posted facsimile at https://www.academia.edu/45502657/Arsenical_copper_tools_of_Old_Kingdom_Giza_craftsmen_First_data. The material is from an independent archaeological context relative to the earlier mortuary assemblage.

Abstract and §§1.1, 2.1: Fifteen finished artefacts were analysed from the Kromer settlement debris dump at Giza. Sealings associate the material with the Khufu and Khafra reigns (c. 2500–2450 BCE in the authors' chronology). The deposit combined discarded material, and the finds cannot be assigned separately to either reign.
Highlights and conclusion: The examined functional full-size chisel and two probable axe-blade fragments were arsenical copper. The authors infer carpentry among the activities represented in the debris.
