§3, San Bartolo site context, p. 4: “The use of both chultunes is most likely to date within the c. 770–895 cal CE”
The context is two pits sealed below construction at Grupo Zacatal, associated with Structures 192 and 195 B/C. Section 3 combines the first two charcoal dates from Structure 192: 707–725 (6.5%) or 773–881 (89.0%) cal CE. For Structure 195 B/C the reported intervals are 772–896 (86.6%) or 926–950 (8.9%) cal CE. The preferred interval for pit use integrates this evidence with the construction sequence. The conclusion’s broader “seventh or eighth century CE” wording differs from the explicit synthesis in section 3.

§§4.1 and 5, pp. 5–8; Table 1 and Figs. 5–10: microscopy identified starch spherulites in both pits. Scanning electron microscopy showed crystalline structures radiating from a central core. Elemental analysis on one particle was consistent with a laboratory lime-treated maize reference. Smaller differences in optical appearance and central pitting were discussed as possible preservation effects.

§6.1, pp. 8–10: the authors consider and argue against animal-fecal spherulites and starch altered by lime after burial. Combined evidence rather than spherulite shape alone supports their identification. Spherulites without intact maize starch granules fit disposal of wastewater from lime treatment, although preservation could also explain the missing intact granules.

§6.2, pp. 10–11, and Fig. 11: the authors interpret helminth eggs as evidence that the pits also received human waste. The residential refuse deposits document household waste handling. The pits do not independently identify the cook, exact cooking location, recipe, or first application of the technique.
