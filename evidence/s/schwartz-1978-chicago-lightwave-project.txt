Attributable reading notes (paraphrased).

Printed p. 1881 / PDF p. 165, §I
The team reports February 1977 Chicago installation after trials of placement methods in New Jersey. Much Atlanta equipment was modified, replaced or repackaged and sent to Chicago. The team distinguishes reported initial traffic on 1 April from full cutover on 11 May.

Printed pp. 1882–1883 / PDF pp. 166–167, §II and Fig. 1
The diagram and system account locate M13 multiplexes at the optical links, providing 44.7 Mb/s streams. The Brunswick customer voice/data link used SLC-40 terminals and video required additional terminal equipment. The Wabash–Franklin interoffice link used standard T1 inputs.

Printed pp. 1882–1885 / PDF pp. 166–169, §III and Figs. 3–5
Bell Laboratories made new cables with Western Electric Atlanta fibers and used the Atlanta cable design except for reducing the stack to two twelve-fiber ribbons. Fig. 5 dates the compared Atlanta experimental cable to 1975 and Chicago’s new cable to 1976. Its distribution counts fewer transmitting fibers than nominal installed fiber positions.

Printed pp. 1885–1886 / PDF pp. 169–170, §IV and Fig. 6
The cable was spliced and installed along the Brunswick Building, Franklin Central Office and Wabash Central Office route. Construction involved older crowded tile ducts and protective inner ducts.

Printed pp. 1887–1888 / PDF pp. 171–172, §§V–VI and Fig. 7
The authors report installed-fiber loss measurements and date full cutover to 11 May 1977. Trial service included interoffice voice, customer voice, analog and digital data, and Picturephone Meeting Service.
