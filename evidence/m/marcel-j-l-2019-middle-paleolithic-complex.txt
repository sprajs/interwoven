Inspected locations: Abstract; Late Pleistocene Adhesives and table 1; The Zandmotor Find; Procedural Complexity and Hafting Practices.

Campitello: the paper reports two unretouched flakes partially covered with chemically identified birch-bark tar and gives a greater-than-191-ka age from associated micromammal biochronostratigraphy, citing Mazza et al. (2006). It states that no clear organic handle is evident on these examples, allowing the tar to have formed backing rather than attachment to a separate handle. The 2019 paper is a secondary account of Campitello.

Zandmotor: the authors examined a flint flake embedded in birch tar; a direct AMS radiocarbon result is described as approximately 50 ka calibrated BP. The item reached an artificial beach in dredged North Sea sediment. The paper states that no separate organic handle is clearly evidenced, so a backing or grip is possible. This is the 2019 paper's primary specimen analysis.

Comparative material: birch bark is transformed into tar by heating, unlike collecting a naturally sticky plant exudate. Neither passage proves the first-ever making of tar, a particular ancient production apparatus, a direct connection between sites, or an ancient end-of-use date.
