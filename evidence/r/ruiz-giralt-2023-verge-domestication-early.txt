Whereas domesticated barley was probably introduced from eastern Sudan or Saudi Arabia by at least 3,500 years ago (during the Initial phase of the Pre-Aksumite period, ca. 1600 to 900 BCE), the origin and time of arrival of C 4 cereals remain uncertain. In this regard, Lyons and D’Andrea ( 6 ) argued that because of the emphasis on griddle vs. oven baking technology in the Ethiopian highlands, nonglutenous local C 4 grains were likely adopted before domestic C 3 plants. T’ef is considered to have been domesticated locally, but it does not appear in the macrobotanical record until ca. 400 BCE at Mezber (Tigrai, Ethiopia) and Mai Chiot (Asmara, Eritrea) ( 5 , 7 ). Later on, finger millet is recorded for the first time at Bieta Giyorgis (Tigrai, Ethiopia) during the Early Aksumite period (ca. 50 BCE to 150 CE) ( 8 ), although in this case the original area of domestication is still unclear ( 9 , 10 ). Finally, the earliest available evidence of sorghum dates from Aksum (Tigrai, Ethiopia) at ca. 500 CE ( 11 , 12 ) and it is thought to have been introduced from the Sudanese lowlands where it was likely domesticated (see refs. 13 and 14 ).
