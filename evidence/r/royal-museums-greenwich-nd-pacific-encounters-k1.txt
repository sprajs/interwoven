L155: # Pacific Encounters
L156: 
L157: Discover the histories and cultures of the world's largest ocean – visit the Pacific Encounters gallery at the National Maritime Museum

L162: ## Essential information
L163: 
L164: Location  | 【15†National Maritime Museum】
L165: Price  | Free
L166:  |

L170: The Pacific is the world’s largest ocean, covering one third of the globe. Its vast sea connects thousands of islands and many diverse cultures.
L171: For thousands of years Tangata Moana – the peoples of the Pacific – have navigated across this mighty ocean, using the skilled art of wayfinding to guide them on their journeys. Not until the 16th century did Europeans begin to explore the region.

L178: The Pacific Encounters gallery at the 【15†National Maritime Museum】 examines the early encounters between Pacific communities and European explorers, and explores the legacies of those encounters today.
L179: 
L180: 【77†Image: Inside the Pacific Encounters gallery at the National Maritime Museum】
L181: ## Inside the gallery
L182: 
L183: This gallery was created in collaboration with all communities from across the Pacific Ocean, based both in the UK and the Pacific.
L184: 
L185: For Tangata Moana (peoples of the Pacific Ocean), many ‘objects’ housed or displayed in European museums are precious cultural and ancestral treasures; powerful, sacred, breathing and connected. Respecting these treasures acknowledges their mana, or power.
L186: European voyages to the Pacific began to increase during the 18th century. This was prompted by an interest in science and exploration – to map the region’s coasts and islands and record its wildlife and cultures – but also to identify land and resources for trade and exploitation.
L187: In Britain, many of these expeditions were commissioned by the Admiralty. Between 1768 and 1780, Captain James Cook embarked on three voyages to the Pacific. Cook’s voyages brought new information that aided Western understanding of the world. However, they also brought lasting consequences for Pacific communities.
L188: In the wake of Cook’s voyages, European missionaries, settlers and administrators arrived across the Pacific. While some interactions between Europeans and peoples of the Pacific were positive, many were violent, resulting in the loss of life, land and cultural heritage. The gallery aims to be a space to address these difficult legacies.
L189: More recently, there has been a revival of the arts, heritage, customs and cultures of the Pacific – a source of pride and a cause for celebration across the ocean and its diverse communities.

L196: ## Adi Yeta
L197: 
L198: Come and say hello to Adi Yeta, a Fijian canoe known as a drua.
L199: 
L200: Adi Yeta was built in 2014-15 using traditional methods. The canoe is bound together by magimagi (coconut husk fibres) and has no nails in its construction.

L207: ## Marshall Islands stick chart
L208: 
L209: Made from wooden sticks and cowrie shells, 【78†this personal map from Majel (the Marshall Islands】) was used by navigators to remember areas of ocean. This chart would have been an important tool to teach the art of wayfinding. The curved sticks represent currents and wave patterns, while the shells mark specific islands.
L210: 
L211: 【79†Image: An image for 'Marshall Islands stick chart'】
L212: ## K1
L213: 
L214: 【80†This elaborate timekeeper from 1769】 has a fascinating story to tell. It was used during Captain James Cook’s second voyage to the Pacific (1772-75) to help calculate longitude (the position located east to west). The clock performed exceptionally well, and was even described by Cook as ‘our trusty friend’. The clock is based on 【81†H4, a prize-winning timekeeper created by clockmaker John Harrison】.
L215: 
L216: 【82†Image: An image for 'K1'】
L217: ## Paintings of Kangaroo and Dingo, George Stubbs
L218: 
L219: Following his work on Cook’s first Pacific voyage (1768-71), scientist Sir Joseph Banks commissioned 【83†artist George Stubbs to create two paintings of a kangaroo and a dingo】.
L220: 
L221: Despite never having seen the animals in person, Stubbs created these artworks based on the sketches, descriptions and animal skins brought back from the voyage. Stubbs’s paintings are the first depictions of the Australian animals in Western art.
L222: 【84†Image: An image for 'Paintings of Kangaroo and Dingo, George Stubbs'】
L223: ## Bark cloth
L224: 
L225: Bark cloth is a textile made by beating the inner bark of trees, such as mulberry. 【85†This piece dates from the 19th century】 and has a bold design, incorporating shell patterns and symbols representing centipedes.
L226: 
L227: While it's unknown where this bark cloth originated, suggestions include Niue, Rarotonga or Samoa.

L232: ## Pasifika Now photography display
L233: 
L234: Powerful photographs by young artists from across Te Moananui-a-Kiwa are now on display in the Pacific Encounters gallery: 【87†Pasifika Now】.
L235: 
L236: Through the lens of these photographers, experience intimate reflections on life, culture and resilience – encompassing island landscapes, portraits, moments of celebration and everyday life across the Pacific.

L239: ## The War Boats of Tahiti by William Hodges
L240: 
L241: Artist William Hodges accompanied Cook on his voyages to the Pacific. He turned his sketches into vibrant paintings, such as this one, titled The War Boats of Tahiti. This artwork depicts Tahitian chiefs preparing for battle with the neighbouring Mo’orea. In this video, see how Hodges’ largest surviving painting was prepared for display at the Museum.

L247: ## Captain Bligh’s coconut bowl
L248: 
L249: Signed and dated ‘W Bligh / April 1789’, 【89†this coconut bowl was used by Captain William Bligh】 on his endeavour from Tofua to Timor. Captain Bligh commanded HMS Bounty, a ship that set out on an expedition from Tahiti in 1787 to collect breadfruit to transport to the West Indies.
L250: The voyage was beset with difficulties and culminated in a mutiny. Bligh and 18 men were cast adrift in an open boat. Bligh navigated from memory to sail to Timor, an undertaking that took 47 days. This cup was used by Bligh during this turbulent time.
L251: 
L252: 【90†Image: An image for 'Captain Bligh’s coconut bowl'】
L253: ## Mai
L254: 
L255: 【91†Mai became the first Pacific Islander to visit Britain】 when he joined HMS Adventure, a vessel that accompanied Cook on his second voyage to the Pacific.
L256: 
L257: During his time in Britain, Mai was seen as a celebrity – and also an object of curiosity. He was introduced to King George III, taken to the theatre and painted by leading artists. Mai returned to Tahiti two years later with European gifts and weapons.

L268:   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   * ## Visiting Pacific Encounters
L269: 
L270: Where is the gallery?
L271: Pacific Encounters is a permanent gallery at the 【75†National Maritime Museum】 in Greenwich, located on the first floor. Entry to the Museum is free: 【96†book tickets online in advance†tickets.rmg.co.uk】 to guarantee entry and receive updates before you visit.

L277: Tours and audio guides
L278: Pacific Encounters features as part of the 【99†range of audio guides available at the National Maritime Museum】. Listen to a free introduction to the gallery 【100†here†app.smartify.org】, or find out more about objects on display by 【94†ordering a special Treasures Tour.】
L279: 
L280: Accessibility information
L281: There are two ways into the Pacific Encounters gallery. One is through automatic doors from the south end of the gallery (from the 【101†Sea Things gallery】) and the
L282: other is over a sloped bridge which slopes down from the 【102†Ocean Map】 to the gallery.
L283: 
L284: This slope has several level changes in it and we advise taking your time when accessing the gallery from this point. There are railings along each side of the bridge.
L285: 
L286: The slope flattens out part way down the first section, then again at the corner.
L287: At each end of the Pacific Encounters gallery is a circular map at a height of 78cm at the south end and 93cm at north end. The north map has a set of two drawers in the cabinet. The drawers are quite heavy but pull out. Each contains a map. One is at 66cm and the other at 78cm. Paintings are displayed at a range of heights starting at 55cm to above head height. Display cabinet heights range from 66cm to 78cm, and some from near floor level.
L288: There are several tactile objects in the Pacific Encounters gallery. These are:
L289: 
L290:   1. a ship's gun from the Endeavor (which has a raised emblem on),
L291:   2. a statuette of Matthew Finders and cat
L292:   3. a telescope.
L293: 
L294: There is also a tactile interactive about the Mutiny on the Bounty at 85cm height.
L295: 
L296: The Transit of Venus telescope is displayed at 125cm high, but the film can be seen from a seated distance, the button for the film is at 90cm. There is also a touch screen AV.
L297: There are five touch screens in the gallery at 79cm high. One features video, audio, BSL and subtitles; three with text and images; one is a map-building game. On the south west wall is a wall mounted screen ‘Continuing Traditions’ showing a video with audio, subtitles and BSL. There are four benches in the gallery.
L298: There are four screens on the south-east wall displaying photographs. At each end of the gallery are automatic glass doors. There is a ramp at the South door leading down to a stone floor in the rotunda area.
L299: 
L300: BSL is included as part of the 【103†National Maritime Museum audio guide†app.smartify.org】. There are four chapters about the Pacific Encounters gallery in the audio guide. Large print guides are also available inside the gallery.
