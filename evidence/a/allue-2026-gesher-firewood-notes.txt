Research notes
Paleoenvironmental and behavioral insights into firewood selection by early Middle Pleistocene hominins
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.109973
Quaternary Science Reviews 382, 109973. Publisher abstract and version of record transcribed on ResearchGate.
Inspected 2026-11-17. New charcoal analysis of excavated Level 1 specimens, compared against earlier published wood, flint and fish material from the same horizon. Two authors also worked on the fish study. Inspected transcription at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405546157_Paleoenvironmental_and_behavioral_insights_into_firewood_selection_by_early_Middle_Pleistocene_hominins.

Sections 1–3 and Table 1: The study identifies 188 of 266 charcoal specimens recovered from Layer II-6 Level 1 (MIS 18). Ash and olive predominate in both charcoal and the comparison wood assemblage.
Fig. 2B–C and section 4: The largest charcoal cluster overlaps the main concentration of fish teeth in Level 1. Charcoal and burnt flint distributions do not precisely coincide. The maps include 190 charcoal fragments with full spatial records, 754 burnt flint microartifacts and 2471 fish remains.
Section 4, pp. 5–7: The authors infer that hominins gathered available wood for fuel, with lakeshore deadwood or driftwood the likely source. They leave the significance of particular taxa in the cluster open.
