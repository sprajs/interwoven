L102: Basin
L103: 1699 - channel on west side of Upper Wet Dock converted to be a gated dry
L104: dock
L105: 1737— channel rebuilt as the North Stone Dock (latterly No. 6 Dock) making
L106: the Upper Wet Dock a closed basin
L107: 1770— conversion of Upper Wet Dock into a reservoir for draining dry docks;
L108: construction at west end of its south side of a stone and brick platform for a
L109: well and culverts, with two horse-gins driving chain pumps in a 'pump house'
L110: 1798-9 - Samuel Bentham replaces the east horse gin on the pump platform
L111: with a single-storey steam-engine house, incorporating a 12hp table engine
L112: designed by James Sadler, an internal boiler and a chimney to the east (used
L113: to drive chain pumps and, in principle, a saw or saws)
L114: 1800-2 - whole reservoir built over with two tiers of brick vaults, affording
L115: storage space and building ground over the continuing reservoir
L116: 1800-1 - replacement of the west horse gin on the pump platform with a two-
L117: storey engine house for a 30hp Boulton & Watt beam engine with a boiler
L118: house to its south, Bentham intending (and perhaps starting) to build a steam-
L119: driven wood mill around the two engine houses
L120: April 1802 - authorisation to construct the wood mills, designed by Samuel
L121: Bunce and begun as twin three-storey ranges separated by an enclosed yard,
L122: the south range incorporating the engine houses

L148: range by a lean-to grindstone house, another engine house being added
L149: beyond. These extensions have been demolished.
