Research notes
Evidence for Earliest Olive-Oil Production in Submerged Settlements off the Carmel Coast, Israel
https://doi.org/10.1006/jasc.1997.0193
Journal of Archaeological Science 24(12), 1141–1150. Published article transcription on ResearchGate.
Inspected 2026-11-17. Original 1990s excavation and interpretation of Kfar Samir installations. Inspected publication copy at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248578882_Evidence_for_Earliest_OliveOil_Production_in_Submerged_Settlements_off_the_Carmel_Coast_Israel. The comparison in Galili et al. (2021) and the proposed basketwork use in Rosenberg et al. (2023) cite these excavations.

The archaeological findings, Figs. 1, 2 and 7: In pit 6, crushed and whole olive stones and pulp were recovered in alternating layers with organic clay above pebbles. The fill also contained reed and straw fragments.
Discussion and conclusions, Fig. 5: The authors explain the deposits as residues of oil extraction, suggest some reed or straw elements could have acted as strainers, and compare the basket photograph with later olive-processing containers. The analogy does not identify a particular basket as the tool used in pit 6.
