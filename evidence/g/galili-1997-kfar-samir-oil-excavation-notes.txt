Figs. 2 and 7 and pp. 1143–1146 document pit 6, a 60 cm by 50 cm pit with a limestone-pebble bed, crushed and whole olive stones, pulp and small reed, branch and straw fragments.
Fig. 5, p. 1145 calls the illustrated object a woven reed fragment associated with olive stones and gives a 20 cm scale. The published scan shares the striped scale, light oval pebble, clay fissure and fragment shapes with Galili et al. (2007) Fig. 3 and Rosenberg et al. (2023) Fig. 7a. It can therefore be identified as the in situ photograph of KS 2 despite the early material description. Fig. 5 gives no installation or accession number.
P. 1147 uses later pressing baskets as an analogy for the pictured object and separately proposes strainers among smaller pit 6 plant remains. Pressing, water separation, and deposition or storage of spent extraction waste are considered for the installations.
Table 3 records RT-1929A as pit 7 olive stones, RT-855 as the pit 8 woven mat and PTA-3821 as wood from well 3.
