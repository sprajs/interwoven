Source-identified research note; paraphrase, not original source bytes.
Gonzalo Fernández de Oviedo y Valdés, Primera parte de la historia natural y general de las Indias, Sevilla, 1535, book VII chapter 2. The original 1535 scan was not directly inspected. The relevant original folio 73va–b is transcribed in the separately inspected 2020 article by Martijn van den Bel (https://americae.fr/en/papers/make-cassava-bread-introduction-metal-graters-guianas-17century/), where the author identifies Oviedo’s observations with Santo Domingo in the second decade of the 16th century. The source passage describes Indigenous men and women preparing yuca: a long, loosely woven bark-fibre sack is filled, suspended, and stretched against the weight of stones, squeezing liquid out through the weave before the pressed pulp is baked. It does not give a precise date for a particular action or prove every chemical compound has been removed. The separate expanded digital transcription at https://ems.kcl.ac.uk/content/etext/e026.html, book VII chapter 2, was also read; it includes later material elsewhere and must not be treated wholesale as the 1535 edition. The 2020 article’s explicit identification and reproduction of the 1535 folio ground the publication bound.
