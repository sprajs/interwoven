Introduction, §§7–9, printed pp. 6–8 (PDF pp. 56–58): Laennec describes the practical limitations of listening with the ear directly on the chest. He recalls a consultation in 1816 where palpation and percussion provided little information and direct listening was unsuitable. A tightly rolled sheet of paper transmitted the heartbeat more clearly than he had heard by direct listening. He says he immediately started a series of observations at Hôpital Necker. He supplies a year for the paper-roll episode.
Introduction, §§11–13, printed pp. 9–11 (PDF pp. 59–61), and plate I (PDF p. 515): Laennec describes the paper cylinder, trials of materials, and the instrument he then used, a bored wooden cylinder with a joint for portability. He describes changing the end configuration according to which sound he was listening for. The 1819 description dates the published design.
Observation III, printed pp. 72–75 (PDF pp. 122–125): The patient “entra à l’hôpital Necker le 19 décembre 1817” and was “Examinée le lendemain”. The first examination used the cylinder to listen under the clavicles and to the heartbeat. The examination was repeated on 21 December and again in January, followed by an autopsy. In this case Laennec calls the instrument a cylinder and leaves its precise construction unstated.
