Printed pp. 153–154 (PDF pp. 183–184): Clark relates his hospital visits and describes the perforated wooden cylinder with one end at the patient’s chest and the practitioner’s ear at the other. While Laennec was unwell, Cayol showed Clark several Necker cases in which stethoscope findings had helped assess a disease. Récamier at the Hôtel-Dieu told Clark that he used the cylinder, which Clark saw him carrying on rounds.
Printed p. 158 (PDF p. 188): Clark discusses an expected review in Dr. Johnson’s journal. The adjoining footnote says: “An English Physician to whom I gave a Sthenoscope which I brought from Paris with me” reported that it had been useful in cardiac diagnosis. Clark leaves his recipient unnamed.
