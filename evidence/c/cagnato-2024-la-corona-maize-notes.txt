Abstract, p. 38: “we argue that nixtamalization as a cooking technique was occurring at La Corona during the 7th and 8th centuries CE.” Capitalization normalized. This conclusion is narrower than the general context range of ca. 600–900 CE given elsewhere in the same abstract.

The authors report microscopic starch spherulites in residues from a separate assemblage of vessels and grinding stones, analyzed with polarized microscopy, scanning electron microscopy and iodine staining. The article describes finds from burial and feasting contexts. This is independent site evidence for Classic Maya practice, with an analytical approach that draws on the same published experimental diagnostic.
