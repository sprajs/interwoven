Printed pp. 487–490 (scan pp. 645–646, 649–650) detail the 1896 acquisition at Jaluit, the 1897 return there, and accounts from Lojak and Laumanuan, mediated in part by Joachim de Brun. Winkler describes his first account of using a chart to steer as a misunderstanding attributed to interpretation. Printed pp. 490–491 report instruction and discussion as functions. Printed p. 494 (scan p. 656) identifies mattang as “only for general instruction without referring to any particular islands”. It distinguishes the more geographically specific meddo and rebbelib. Printed p. 495 (scan p. 659) describes charts as personal reminders and sources for teaching distinctive sea signs, and notes individual variations. Printed p. 496 (scan p. 660), Chart I, illustrates an abstract generalized island-pair example. The translation's first-page footnote identifies the 1898 German publication; the report's 1899 cover year differs from its English publication year.
