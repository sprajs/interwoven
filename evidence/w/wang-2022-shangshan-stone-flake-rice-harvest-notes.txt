New evidence for rice harvesting in the early Neolithic Lower Yangtze River, China
Jiajing Wang, Jiangping Zhu, Dongrong Lei, Leping Jiang. PLOS ONE, 17(12), e0278200; version of record.

Original use-wear and phytolith residue analyses on excavated flakes, distinct from the Shangshan sediment phytolith samples used to infer domestication traits.

Integrating use-wear and phytolith results, Figs. 6–8: Specimen SS-10 from Shangshan combines predominantly transverse striations with rice-husk phytoliths, interpreted as cutting panicles. Botanical residues and use-wear provide different proxies on the same flakes.

Discussion and conclusion: The authors propose that repeated selective panicle harvest can bias seed stocks towards non-shattering rice if later harvests are kept for sowing. SS-10 directly informs the technique but does not test seed selection for a particular population or the silica traits studied by Zuo and colleagues.
