Paulin (2007), “Perspectives of Māori fishing history and techniques: Ngā āhua me ngā pūrākau me ngā hangarau ika o te Māori,” Tuhinga 18, pp. 11–47, Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa.

Printed p. 27 describes the operation of a line-attached gorge: a fish swallows a narrow baited device and pull on the line turns it across the throat. It describes a fundamentally different mode of insertion from a spear- or shaft-mounted head thrust into an animal; rotation alone is shared and is not a unique invention of toggling harpoons. The article discusses Māori and other prehistoric contexts, but this passage does not date a particular early gorge. The 2025 Bradfield and Langley scholarly review discusses much earlier possible gorges and cautions about alternative identifications.
