Inspected full web-rendered original text printed pp. 6, 10–13 and 15: Boyle built an ammonia machine in Jefferson, Texas in 1873 but experienced major leaks, moved the prototype to Marshall and achieved reliable running in April 1874, with ice sales increasing over the summer; the paper cites contemporaneous retrospective trade accounts. It says De Coppet designed a compressor for the Merz New Orleans brewery after an unsuccessful ammonia trial in 1869 and that the subsequent machine was used to cool a beer cellar, but it does not unambiguously specify the use year. It notes Boyle’s 1872 patent does not specify ammonia, unlike the earlier Tellier 1870 patent. It treats Beath’s successful installations as absorption rather than established compressor operations and discusses glycerine seals.
