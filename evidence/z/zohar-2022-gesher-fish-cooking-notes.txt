Short quotation (Discussion): “no traces of cooking apparatus have been preserved at the site”

Research notes:
The age is an approximate site age. Eight occupation horizons in Area B Layer II-6 include L4b. These contain abundant cyprinid teeth and few bones, unlike the Area A assemblages used for a natural-death comparison (Results; Fig. 1; Supplementary Text C).
The authors compare tooth enameloid crystallite size and microstrain from ancient teeth and their heating experiments. Some of the Area B pharyngeal teeth were consistent with moderate heating. The experimental groups overlap in crystallite size, and the authors' archaeological temperature categories are indirect estimates (Fig. 4, Extended Data Fig. 5, Supplementary Text D and Tables 4–6).
Fig. 5 shows maps for L2, L3 and L6. The flint maps use the same counts as those rows of the 2008 fire study's Table 1. Supplementary Fig. 1 extends the maps to all eight horizons. Supplementary Text E (printed pp. 14–19) assigns plotting positions within recorded grid units for many small finds. Fig. 2 of that supplement reports separate clustering of fish remains. The overlap with burnt flint is read from density maps.
The paper favors cooking over purely natural deposition, diagenesis or discarding fish into a hot fire. The discussion's earth-oven proposal is a possible explanation for indirect heat; the authors report no physical cooking apparatus.
