Child's Rubber Boots

Subject

Accessories

Title

Child's Rubber Boots

Description

These are Wellington style rubber boots. The boots are very small in size indicating they were worn by a child. The length from the top of the vamp to the welt is only 2.25 inches, and the welt to the back strap is only about 6.25 inches. Of note is the design around the top edge as well as a patent printed on the bottom. Due to wear of the rubber, the only words on the manufacturer's stamp on the bottom of the soles are "Colchester, Conn." and "Goodyear Patent."

These shoes have an important cultural context. In the year 1839, Charles Goodyear discovered that mixing rubber with sulfur prior to heating made the rubber much harder and longer lasting than untreated rubber. This process was called vulcanization, and by 1844 vulcanized rubber was being produced for the public. The rubber on these boots would have gone through this process relatively soon after the discovery of vulcanization. Compared to other rubber shoes from the same time, this pair is in very good condition.
