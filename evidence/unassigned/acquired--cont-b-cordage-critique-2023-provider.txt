Recent excavations at Çatalhöyük1 in Anatolia, Turkey have recovered remains of textiles, cordage and basketry that add\nL22: to finds from previous work at this Neolithic site. Along with occurrences from other sites in the Near East, North Africa and Europe,\nL23: they enable charting the development of textile techniques. This paper argues that the woven textiles found in Çatalhöyük (6700–6500\nL24: cal. BC) fit in a long development in the Palaeolithic, Epipalaeolitic and Pre-Pottery Neolithic periods, during which strings and\nL25: basketry techniques were used to produce a large variety of objects such as shelters, rigid containers, and flexible fabrics that may be\nL26: termed textiles. Çatalhöyük is currently the earliest site where preserved woven textiles have been found and forms the starting point\nL27: for our discussion of the emergence of textile technologies. Weaving appears across a wide geographical area in the first half of the\nL28: 7th millennium cal. BC. We argue that two of the earliest attested basketry techniques, weft-twining, and weft-wrapping (soumak and\nL29: related techniques), are closely connected to the development of weaving. The size and edges of textiles, matting and basketry provide\nL30: the basis to reconstruct tools, loom types and production technology and give important insights in a society’s gendered technological\nL31: development, that are different from those provided by the study of lithic, ceramic or metal technology. Many find spots in the Near\nL32: East, North Africa and Europe document this development.\nL33: Résumé.
