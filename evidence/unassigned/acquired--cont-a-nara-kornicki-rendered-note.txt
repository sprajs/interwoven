Peter Kornicki, “Empress Shōtoku as a Sponsor of Printing”, in Tibetan Printing: Comparison, Continuities, and Change, Brill (2016), pp. 45–50, DOI 10.1163/9789004316256_006. Publisher URL: https://brill.com/display/book/9789004316256/B9789004316256_006.pdf
Inspected via the web tool indexed/rendered text, 2026-11-06; direct publisher PDF request returned HTTP 405. pp. 45–46: the Shoku nihongi chronicle (797) has an entry for 770 recording that the miniature pagodas were distributed to temples after completion; the chronicle does not say the included charms were printed. A later temple record mentions printed charms; the surviving printed objects and dated pagoda bases are additional witnesses. pp. 49–50: the printed charms were enclosed for ritual rather than intended primarily as reading texts; no later mention of printing until 1009 is noted, which does not prove that all printing ceased. Only these passages were inspected; this note paraphrases the rendered text.
