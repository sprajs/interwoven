Object No. 92/1

Specification of James Watt's separate condenser patent

Document, specification of James Watt's separate condenser patent, paper / ink, written by James Watt, England, 1769

Summary

Physical Description

This folio sized sheet of paper provides the detailed specification for James Watt's 'Method of lessening the consumption of Steam and Fuel in Fire Engines'. The sheet of paper has been folded in half, producing four leaves. The title of the document is printed on one leaf and repeated at the top of the second leaf. Details of the specifications are printed on the second and third leaves. The paper is watermarked with a seal consisting of a crown surmounting a cameo of a striding soldier. The patent is dated 5 January 1769 and was 'enrolled in the Court of Chancery on the 29th Day of April 1769'. The document lists seven separate principles covered by the specification. 1. The engine's cylinder, which he calls the Steam Vessel, is to be kept hot by surrounding it with steam, insulating it, and suffering neither water nor other substances to enter or touch it. 2. Steam is to be condensed in a separate vessel called the Condenser, which is kept as cold as the air in the neighbourhood. 3. Air is to be drawn out from the cylinder and condenser by means of pumps wrought by the engine itself. 4. In some cases, the force of the steam is to act upon the piston and be exhausted to the atmosphere after it has done its work, rather than being condensed. 5. For rotative engines, the cylinder is to be in the form of a hollow ring. 6. In some cases, cold is to be applied to the engine (but not so as to condense the steam) to encourage it to work by the expansion and contraction of steam. 7. Oils, wax, resinous bodies or fats are to be used to seal the piston.

PRODUCTION

Notes

The specifications were based on a concept that James Watt developed while working in Scotland. The document was produced in 1769 by the British Government's Court of Chancery, which had responsibiity for issuing patents.

HISTORY

Notes

The document was owned by Richard Michell, a Cornish mining engineer who worked closely with Boulton and Watt, and was passed down through his family until it was offered at auction in 1992. It was purchased by the Museum along with other documents and drawings (objects 92/2 to 92/21).
