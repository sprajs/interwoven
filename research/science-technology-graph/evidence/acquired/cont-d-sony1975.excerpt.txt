L38@P2: far-reaching recession since World War II.

L40@P2: the problem of coping with increased costs
L41@P2: while engaging in an all-out effort to increase sales. Sony's efforts in the area of
L42@P2: increased sales were most rewarding in that
L43@P2: its consolidated net sales established a new
L44@P2: high. Unfortunately, its increased sales were
L45@P2: insufficient to off set its increased costs,

L50@P2: percent above net sales of $1,297,552,000 for fiscal 1974. Consolidated net income for

L59@P2: percent in the European market, 14 percent in the Asian market (excluding Japan) and
L60@P2: 46 percent in other markets.

L65@P3: On April 16, 1975, Sony introduced a new video system for home use. This
L66@P3: system, named Betamax, is a small size, moderately priced videocassette system with high
L67@P3: resolution and fidelity. It was made possible through advanced technology developed by
L68@P3: the company. Betamax incorporates new developments such as a high-density color
L69@P3: recording system, new video heads and high-density recording tape. Since the Betamax
L70@P3: system makes it easy to record and play back TV programs, consumers can enjoy any
L71@P3: desired program on their TV screen at their own convenience. Betamax has been developed
L72@P3: primarily for consumer use. In Japan, three types of Betamax systems are currently
L73@P3: being marketed. One type consists of a console model in which the Betamax video deck
L74@P3: is combined with a Trinitron color TV and an automatic timer. The other two types
L75@P3: consist of separate video decks. The only model currently being sold in the United States
L76@P3: is the console model.
