John C. Whittaker, Levers, Not Springs: How a Spearthrower Works and Why It Matters, in Multidisciplinary Approaches to the Study of Stone Age Weaponry (2016), pp. 65–74, DOI 10.1007/978-94-017-7602-8_5. Inspected via the author-uploaded PDF rendering at https://www.researchgate.net/profile/John-Whittaker-2/publication/303626307_Levers_Not_Springs_How_a_Spearthrower_Works_and_Why_It_Matters/links/65970a696f6e450f19cacf6f/Levers-Not-Springs-How-a-Spearthrower-Works-and-Why-It-Matters.pdf.
Printed pp. 65–67 describe a hand-held launcher engaging a separate light spear and explain the launcher as a lever within the throwing motion. The chapter contrasts its operation with both a hand-cast projectile and an elastic bow. Individual form and performance vary. This mechanical account does not independently establish a particular archaeological date or an ancestry between weapons.
